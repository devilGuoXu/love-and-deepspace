I. Introduction: More Than a Game

Love and Deepspace is not just a mobile game. For millions of players worldwide, it is a shared emotional universe — a space where stories are not only consumed but also created, remixed, and debated. The fandom around Deepspace has grown into a vibrant ecosystem of artists, writers, cosplayers, theorists, and emotional supporters. This guide explores the many facets of that community.

Fandom by the Numbers (Estimated):
  • 📱 10M+ active monthly players globally
  • 🎨 500K+ fan artworks shared across platforms
  • 📖 100K+ fanfiction stories posted online
  • 🎭 50K+ cosplay photos and videos
  • 💬 5M+ daily posts and comments on Chinese social media
  • 🌍 Active communities in CN, JP, KR, EN, and growing SEA regions

II. The Fan Creation Ecosystem

Fan creations are the lifeblood of any passionate fandom. Deepspace players have embraced every form of creative expression imaginable. Here is a breakdown of the major categories.

2.1 Fan Art — From Sketches to Masterpieces

Popular Art Styles:
  • Semi-realistic digital painting: The most common style, mimicking the game's official art
  • Chibi/cute style: Popular for comics and merchandise
  • Watercolor and traditional media: Often used for softer, romantic pieces
  • Anime-style: Blending with other otome game aesthetics
  • Dark/gothic: Particularly popular for Sylus-themed works
Most Drawn Subjects:
  • ❤️ Romantic scenes between MC and each male lead
  • 🎭 Alternate universe (AU) settings — modern AU, fantasy AU, historical AU
  • 😭 Emotional moments from the story (Xavier's 200-year wait, Sylus's throne room, etc.)
  • 😂 Humorous comics depicting daily life or group chat interactions
  • 👥 Group shots of all four leads together

2.2 Fanfiction — Writing New Stories

Fanfiction Platforms and Volume:
  • Lofter (Chinese): 50,000+ Deepspace fics, most active platform
  • AO3 (International): 15,000+ English works, rapidly growing
  • Weibo super topics: Daily ficlets and threads
  • Pixiv (Japanese): 8,000+ stories + illustrations
Most Popular Fanfiction Tropes:
  • 📝 "What if" scenarios: Alternative endings or different first meetings
  • 🏠 Domestic fluff: Daily life after the main story
  • 💔 Angst and hurt/comfort: Exploring trauma and healing
  • 🌍 Crossover AUs: Deepspace characters in modern or historical settings
  • 👥 Polyamory/four-way relationships: A unique subgenre where MC is with all leads
  • 📜 Canon divergence: Rewriting specific story branches

2.3 Cosplay — Becoming the Character

Sylus cosplay — the most challenging due to his unique aesthetic

Group cosplays of all four leads are convention highlights

Cosplay Popularity Ranking:
  1. Sylus: Most popular to cosplay — dramatic, recognizable silhouette

  5. MC: Also popular — many cosplayers create their own version

Why the Differences?
  • China: The "dark and dangerous" archetype (Sylus) consistently tops otome polls. The redemption narrative appeals strongly.
  • Japan: Otome game tradition favors gentle, devoted, and slightly melancholic male leads — Xavier fits perfectly.
  • South Korea: Professional competence and emotional restraint (Zayne) align with cultural ideals of masculinity.
  • West: Mix of preferences — Sylus's intensity and Rafayel's dramatic expressiveness both find audiences.

V. Community Platforms — Where Fans Gather

The Deepspace fandom is spread across multiple platforms, each with its own culture, norms, and content focus.

Weibo (China)

The largest Deepspace community hub. Official news, fanart, ficlets, and daily discourse. Character "super topics" have millions of posts each.

Reddit (International)

r/LoveAndDeepspace — 50k+ members. Focus on gameplay discussion, lore theories, and news. More analytical than other platforms.

Discord (International)

Real-time chat, voice channels, and dedicated channels for fanart, cosplay, and co-op gameplay. The most interactive platform.

VI. The Emotional Bond — Why Fandom Becomes Family

For many players, the Deepspace fandom is more than a hobby — it is a source of emotional support. Players share personal struggles, celebrate milestones, and form genuine friendships through their shared love of the game.

Why Fandom Matters:
  • Validation of feelings: Players find others who understand their emotional attachment to fictional characters.
  • Creative community: Artists and writers receive feedback and encouragement.
  • Emotional support: Many servers have dedicated channels for mental health check-ins.
  • Crisis response: During real-world disasters, fandom communities have organized mutual aid.
  • Cross-cultural friendship: Language barriers are overcome through shared passion.

Image descriptions: Left — a screenshot of a supportive Discord conversation. Right — photos of fans meeting at conventions.

A Community Member's Testimony:

"I joined the Deepspace fandom during a difficult time in my life. I was lonely and struggling. Finding people who loved the same characters, who created art and wrote stories, who laughed at the same jokes — it gave me something to look forward to every day. Two years later, some of those online friends have become real-life friends. The game brought us together, but the community kept us together."

— Anonymous survey respondent
