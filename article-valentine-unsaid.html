Things She Doesn't Know

four men. one day. five things she never found out.

a valentine's story ⏱ 10 min read no tissues included

there are things they never told her.

not because they didn't want to. because they didn't know how. because the words felt too small. because some things, once said, change everything.

this is valentine's day. the day of confessions and chocolates and hearts on windows.

and this is what they did instead.

i.
xavier

he woke up before the sun.

not unusual. he didn't sleep much. two hundred years of watching the stars had trained his body to expect darkness. but today, he had a reason.

the observatory had a telescope. not the big one — the small one, the one no one used because it wasn't calibrated. he'd spent the last three nights fixing it.

there was a star. not a famous one. not one you'd find in any textbook. he'd discovered it himself, decades ago, when he was still learning which stars were which. he'd never named it. never told anyone.

today, he decided, he would name it after her.

he found a star. he never told her its name.

he spent the morning calculating coordinates. writing them down. erasing them. writing them again. he wanted it to be perfect.

by noon, he had a slip of paper in his pocket. small. folded twice. he touched it sometimes, when he thought no one was looking.

she came to the observatory that afternoon. not for him — she didn't know he'd be there. she was looking for a quiet place to read. the observatory was empty most days.

he watched her from the platform above. she sat in his chair. opened her book. didn't notice him.

he could have gone down. could have said something. could have handed her the paper and said "i named a star after you."

he didn't.

he stayed where he was. watched her read. watched the light change across her face.

when she left, he followed her with his eyes. then he took the paper out of his pocket. looked at it. folded it again. put it back.

he would tell her someday. maybe. if the words ever came.

"the star is still there. so is the paper. so is the someday."
ii.
zayne

he bought the flowers on his way to work.

not roses. something quieter. white camellias. he didn't know why he chose them. maybe because they looked like her. maybe because they didn't scream "i love you" the way roses did. maybe because he was a coward.

he put them on the passenger seat. drove to the hospital. looked at them. looked away. looked again.

he bought flowers. he never gave them to her.

she came to the hospital that day. not for him — she had a check-up. routine. blood work. she didn't even know he was working.

he saw her name on the schedule. stared at it. closed the file. opened it again.

he could have walked to the waiting room. could have said "happy valentine's day" like it meant nothing. could have handed her the flowers and said "these are for you" like it was nothing.

he didn't.

he stayed in his office. watched the clock. listened to the sound of her footsteps in the hallway. she laughed at something. he didn't know what. he wanted to know.

when she left, he went to his car. the camellias were still there. still white. still quiet. still ungiven.

he drove home with them. put them in a glass of water. they lasted a week. he threw them away on the eighth day.

"he kept one petal. pressed it in a book. never told her why."
iii.
rafayel

he painted all night.

not because he had a deadline. not because the inspiration struck. because he couldn't sleep. because her face kept appearing on the canvas whether he wanted it to or not.

he'd started the painting weeks ago. a portrait. her. laughing at something. he couldn't remember what. the memory had blurred at the edges, but the feeling hadn't.

he painted her. he never showed her the canvas.

he worked until dawn. mixing colors. layering paints. chasing something he couldn't name. the smile still wasn't right. the eyes still weren't her. he'd been painting her for years and he still couldn't get it right.

maybe that was the point. maybe some things weren't meant to be captured.

she came to his studio that evening. unannounced. she did that sometimes. brought food. sat on his floor. talked about nothing. he loved it. hated it. both.

the painting was facing the wall. he'd turned it before she arrived. she didn't ask what was behind it. he didn't offer to show her.

they ate takeout on the floor. she told him about her day. he listened. laughed at the right moments. asked the right questions. all the while, his eyes kept drifting to the covered canvas.

when she left, he turned it around. looked at her face. touched the still-wet paint.

maybe tomorrow, he thought. maybe tomorrow he'd show her.

he said that every day.

"the painting is still facing the wall. so is he."
iv.
sylus

he typed the message. stared at it. deleted it.

he typed it again. stared longer. deleted it again.

seven times.

the message was simple. three words. he'd said harder things to people who deserved worse. but this was different. this was her.

he typed seven messages. he deleted all of them.

he spent the day in meetings. onychinus didn't care about valentine's day. the zone didn't care about anything. but his mind kept drifting back to the phone in his pocket. to the words he couldn't say.

"today is valentine's day." no. too obvious.

"i've been thinking about you." no. too vulnerable.

"i love you." no. too final.

he settled for nothing. sent nothing.

she texted him at midnight. not about valentine's day. about a mission. something boring. something safe.

he replied. typed. deleted. typed again. sent "be careful."

she sent back a thumbs up.

he looked at the screen for a long time. then he called her. not to say the words. just to hear her voice.

"it's late," she said.

"i know."

"what's wrong?"

"nothing."

she was quiet. then: "sylus."

just his name. like she knew. like she always knew.

"happy valentine's day," he said. his voice was flat. controlled. his heart was not.

she laughed. soft. surprised. "you remembered."

"i remember everything about you."

another pause. longer this time.

"sylus."

"yeah."

"i know."

he didn't know what she meant. maybe that was enough.

"he didn't say it. but he thinks she knows anyway."

she never found out about the star.

or the flowers in his car, dying slowly on the passenger seat.

or the painting hidden behind a canvas, facing the wall.

or the seven unsent messages.

she never found out about any of it.

and maybe that's okay.

because some things — the biggest things — don't need to be said to be real.

— happy valentine's day. from the ones who never say it.
