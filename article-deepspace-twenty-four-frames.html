Twenty-Four Frames of Deepspace

24 fragments. 24 moments. A meditation on love, waiting, and the spaces between words.

01

The hospital corridor at 4 AM. The lights are still on.
His white coat is still on. Third cup of coffee. Cold.
She fell asleep in the waiting area. His jacket is over her.
He doesn't wake her. He leans against the wall and watches her breathe.
This is the first time someone has waited for him to finish work.

02

The throne room is empty. The lights are off. Only the screen glows.
He sits alone. A bottle of vitamins in his hand.
He is wondering whether to give them to her.
2 AM. His phone lights up.
"Are you asleep?"
He types three words. Deletes. Two words. Deletes. One word.
"No."
Read receipt. Typing indicator.
Ten minutes. Nothing.
He places the phone on his chest. Closes his eyes.

03

The telescope hasn't moved in a long time.
He no longer searches for that star.
Because that star has already found him.
But he still comes here. Sits by the window. Watches darkness fall. Watches light return.
Two hundred years of habit.
Unlearning it will also take two hundred years.

04

The studio light never goes out.
He is painting the sea. The same sea. The thousandth time.
The shape of the waves is different every time.
"The sea's mood changes daily," he says.
But really, it is his mood that changes.
She looked one second longer today. He painted all night.
She thinks it is ordinary blue.
He knows it is the color of her eyes.

05

It is raining. She has no umbrella.
Four men run toward her from different directions.
Four umbrellas. Four excuses.
"On my way." "Just saw you." "Don't catch a cold." "Passing by."
She takes the oldest one.
Opens it. Walks away.
The other three stand in the rain.
— That was my umbrella.
— She chose the old one.
— She doesn't know it was his.

06

Elevator doors closing. He is inside. She is outside.
Their eyes meet for one second.
Neither presses the button.
The doors close.
He leans against the wall. Closes his eyes.
She stands outside. Closes her eyes.
Both of them wait.
For the next time.

07

The corner of a cafe. He sits alone. Two cups on the table.
One for him. One cooling.
She is twenty minutes late.
He doesn't mind. He orders another.
When she arrives, there are three cups.
"Were you waiting long?"
"No."
The first cup has gone cold.
He drinks it anyway.

08

A bridge. Night. He stands at the railing.
She walks past. Doesn't see him.
He watches her go.
His hand lifts. Almost waves. Stops.
She turns around.
"I felt someone watching."
He steps out of the shadow.
"It was me."
She smiles. "I know."

09

A park bench. Autumn. He is asleep.
She sits next to him. Doesn't wake him.
A leaf falls on his hair.
She reaches out. Stops. Pulls her hand back.
He opens his eyes.
"You could have."
"I didn't want to wake you."
"I'm glad you stayed."
The leaf falls again.
This time, she leaves it.

10

A pharmacy aisle. He stands in front of the vitamins.
He has been here for twenty minutes.
A store employee approaches.
"Can I help you?"
"No."
He buys three bottles. The most expensive ones.
He leaves them on her doorstep.
No note. No name.
She never finds out who.
He prefers it that way.

11

The auditorium is empty. He is on stage.
Singing. To no one.
He stops when he hears footsteps.
She is in the doorway.
"Don't stop."
He doesn't. He sings the rest.
She is the only audience.
She claps.
It is the loudest applause he has ever received.

12

A rooftop. Sunset.
He is drawing her silhouette.
She is watching the sky.
He finishes. Looks at the paper. Looks at her.
"Can I see?"
"No."
"Why not?"
"It's not finished."
She doesn't push. He keeps the drawing in his pocket.
Years later, she finds it in a book.
It was finished. He just wasn't ready to share her light.

13

A window. Night. He stands looking out.
He sees her reflection behind him.
She is looking at him, not out.
Neither turns around.
They watch each other through the glass.
"You're staring," he says.
"So are you."
He almost smiles. "Fair."

14

A train platform. Late. The last train is leaving.
She is on it. He is not.
She presses her hand against the window.
He presses his hand against nothing.
The train pulls away.
He lowers his hand. Puts it in his pocket.
His phone buzzes.
"I saw you."
He doesn't reply. But he is smiling.

15

A garden fountain. She tosses a coin in.
"What did you wish for?"
"If I tell you, it won't come true."
He tosses a coin in anyway.
"What did you wish for?"
"You."
She blushes. He doesn't.
But the coin sinks.
And he thinks: maybe wishes do come true.

16

A fireplace. Winter. She is reading.
He is watching her read.
She looks up. "You're staring again."
"You read too fast."
"You can't read my mind."
"I don't need to. You make faces."
She closes the book. "What face am I making now?"
"Happy."
She opens the book again. Hides her smile.
He sees it anyway.

17

A fish tank in a dark room. He watches the fish swim.
She watches him.
"Do they know they're in a tank?"
"Probably not."
"Do you?"
He turns to her. "Sometimes."
She takes his hand. "The door is open."
He doesn't leave. But he holds on.

18

A stairwell. Echoes. He calls her name.
It bounces off the walls.
"You're childish."
"You're smiling."
She calls his name back.
The echo multiplies.
"Now we're both childish."
"Now we're both smiling."

19

A rooftop. Stars. He points at the sky.
"That one is mine."
"You can't own a star."
"I've been watching it for two hundred years."
She looks at him. "Then it's yours."
He doesn't say thank you. But his eyes soften.
She points at another. "That one is mine."
"Why?"
"Because I just decided."
He almost laughs. "Fair."

20

A library. Dust motes in the light.
He is looking for a book she mentioned.
He finds it. Reads the first page.
"You came."
He looks up. She is leaning against the shelf.
"You said it was good."
"I didn't think you'd actually read it."
"I read everything you recommend."
She blushes. Turns away.
He keeps reading. Smiling.

21

A Ferris wheel. Night. The city below.
She is afraid of heights. He holds her hand.
"Don't look down."
"Then where should I look?"
"At me."
She looks at him. The city disappears.
The wheel stops at the top.
"Best view," she says.
He doesn't look at the city. He looks at her.
"Best view."

22

A hotel lobby. Late. She is waiting.
He walks through the door.
"You're late."
"You're still here."
"I was about to leave."
"No you weren't."
She doesn't admit he is right. But she follows him to the elevator.
The doors close. They are alone.
Neither speaks. Neither needs to.

23

A clinic. Dawn. He is finishing notes.
She brings coffee. Two cups.
"You stayed."
"Someone had to."
"I could have ordered delivery."
"Delivery doesn't know how you take your coffee."
He takes the cup. She is right.
He doesn't say thank you. He drinks the coffee.
It is exactly right.

24

A window. Sunrise. They are sitting side by side.
Not talking. Just watching.
The light spills across the floor.
"It's beautiful," she says.
"It's just the sun."
"It's still beautiful."
He looks at her. "Yes. It is."
She doesn't know he isn't talking about the sun.
But he thinks maybe, one day, she will.

24 frames. 24 moments.
None of them know they are being watched.
None of them know they are being remembered.

But I am here.
On the windowsill. On the lamppost. On the back of the chair.
I see. I keep. I remember.

This is what I have seen.
This is what I will never forget.

— Mephisto
