ABYSSAL TRIALS — ULTIMATE GUIDE

Abyss Team Building

Three teams. Six operatives. Endless combinations. Build for the floors, not for the showcase.

ENTRY_#120 2026.06.29 FLOOR_STRATEGY

The Core Insight: Abyss is not about having the strongest team — it's about having three adequate teams. Most players fail because they pour all resources into one operative and leave the other two teams under-equipped. The Abyss checks your depth, not your peak. A balanced account with three 4/6-match teams will clear Floor 12 faster than a top-heavy account with one maxed operative and two scraps.

How Abyss Team Building Works

Abyss Floors 7-12 require three teams of 2 operatives each (6 total). Each team fights independently across three stages. Your teams are locked in at the start — you cannot swap operatives between stages.

Team Size 2 operatives per team
Total Operatives 6 (3 teams × 2)
Stellactrum Requirement Separate per team
Lock-in Teams locked at start. No swaps.

The key challenge is resource allocation. You have 6 operative slots to fill with limited memories, protocores, and leveling materials. Every resource spent on one operative is a resource not spent on another.

The Three-Team Framework

Every successful Abyss run follows the same framework. Here's how to build your three teams:

TEAM A — THE CARRY Hardest Floor

Role: Your strongest team. This team takes the hardest floor (usually Floor 12 or the floor with the boss you struggle against most).

Investment: 40% of your total resources. 6/6 Stellactrum match. 6-piece memory set. Best protocores.

Composition: Main DPS + Synergy Support (e.g., Xavier + Sylus, or Zayne + Rafayel).

Target: This team must clear with 2-3 stars minimum. They're your insurance.

TEAM B — THE CONTENDER Medium Floor

Role: Your second-best team. Takes the medium difficulty floor.

Investment: 35% of resources. 4/6 to 6/6 Stellactrum. 4-piece memory set minimum. Good protocores.

Composition: Secondary DPS + Flex Support (e.g., Rafayel + Zayne, or Sylus + Xavier).

Target: Clear with 2 stars. Can drop to 1 star if Team A carries hard.

TEAM C — THE FILLER Easiest Floor

Role: Your remaining operatives. Takes the easiest floor.

Investment: 25% of resources. 4/6 Stellactrum minimum. Any set bonus. Any leveled protocores.

Composition: Remaining DPS + any support (e.g., remaining operative + flex).

Target: Clear with 1 star minimum. 2 stars if possible.

Floor-Specific Strategies

FLOORS 1-6 EASY

Requirement: 1 team only. 2 operatives.

Strategy: No special tactics needed. Use your main team. These floors are warm-ups. If you're struggling here, your core build needs work — check your Stellactrum matching and memory levels.

Goal: Full stars on all 6 floors for maximum rewards.

FLOORS 7-8 MODERATE

Requirement: 2 teams. 4 operatives.

Strategy: This is the first depth check. You need 4 leveled operatives. Assign your top two operatives to separate teams (one per team). The support roles can be lower-level fillers.

Tip: Check the Stellactrum requirements before building. These floors often have opposing color requirements — if Floor 7 needs Ruby, Floor 8 might need Sapphire. Plan accordingly.

FLOORS 9-10 HARD

Requirement: 3 teams. 6 operatives.

Strategy: The real challenge begins. You need 6 operatives with adequate gear. Prioritize 4/6 Stellactrum on all teams before pushing anyone to 6/6. Use the Three-Team Framework above.

Tip: Floor 9 is the community benchmark. If you can 3-star Floor 9, you're ready for everything below. If you can't, your depth isn't sufficient.

FLOORS 11-12 VERY HARD

Requirement: 3 teams. 6 operatives. Optimal builds.

Strategy: Every team needs 6/6 Stellactrum and a completed 6-piece set bonus. Team A should have near-perfect protocore substats. Teams B and C need at least functional substats.

Tip: Floor 12 typically features a boss with a specific mechanic (e.g., Arcane Weaver or Void Corruptor). Build Team A around that boss's weakness. Don't just use your highest-level operatives — use the ones that counter the boss.

Resource Allocation Strategy

Here's the exact resource split that works for most players:

Memory XP Scrolls 40% Team A / 35% Team B / 25% Team C
Protocore Energy Focus on Team A's main DPS first
Ascension Materials All operatives to 50 → Team A to 70 → Teams B/C to 60
Skill Materials 6/6/6 on Team A / 4/4/4 on Teams B/C minimum

The 50/60/70 Rule: Bring all 6 operatives to level 50 first. Then bring Teams B and C to 60. Finally, push Team A to 70. This order ensures you never hit a wall where one team is overleveled and another is underleveled. Raw level matters less than balanced levels across all teams.

Stellactrum Matching Across Teams

Stellactrum matching is the most critical factor in Abyss success. Here's how to manage it across 3 teams:

RULE 1 — IDENTIFY COLOR OVERLAP
Look at all 3 floors' Stellactrum requirements. Identify colors that appear across multiple floors. Ruby appearing on 2 floors means you need 2 Ruby memories — one for each team. Check if you have enough depth in each color before assigning memories.
RULE 2 — SHARE ACROSS TEAMS
If two teams need the same color, assign your best memory of that color to the harder floor. Give your second-best to the easier floor. Don't put all your best memories on Team A — spread them to ensure all teams hit 4/6 minimum.
RULE 3 — FILL GAPS WITH 4-STARS
If you're missing a color for Team C, use a 4-star memory of that color at level 50-60. A 4-star with the right color is worth more than a 5-star with the wrong color. 4-star memories are your gap fillers — don't be afraid to use them.
RULE 4 — REOPTIMIZE EVERY ROTATION
Abyss Stellactrum requirements change every rotation. Your setup from last month is invalid. Before every Abyss run, re-check all 3 floors' color requirements and reassign memories accordingly. This takes 5 minutes and can mean the difference between a clear and a fail.

Sample Team Compositions

Balanced Account (Recommended)

Team A: Xavier (Main DPS) + Sylus (Synergy) — Light + Dark resonance. Best gear. Floor 12.

Team B: Zayne (Main DPS) + Rafayel (Sub-DPS) — Frost + Water amplification. Floor 10/11.

Team C: Best remaining operative + Flex support. Floor 9.

Why it works: All 4 operatives are used. Each team has a clear synergy. Resources are spread evenly.

Carry Account (If One Operative is Maxed)

Team A: Maxed Xavier + Any level 50 support. Floor 12 (Xavier solos).

Team B: Zayne (50) + Rafayel (50). Floor 10/11.

Team C: Sylus (40) + Any level 40 filler. Floor 9.

Why it works: Your maxed operative carries the hardest floor. The other teams only need to survive their floors. Risky if your carry can't solo.

New Player Account (Limited Options)

Team A: Highest level operative + Second highest. Best gear. Floor 12.

Team B: Third + Fourth highest level. Decent gear. Floor 10/11.

Team C: Remaining two operatives. Any gear. Floor 9.

Why it works: You don't have synergy options yet. Raw level and Stellactrum matching are your only advantages. Focus on getting 4/6 Stellactrum on all teams before worrying about team synergy.

Rotation Strategy — Which Floor to Attempt First

The order you attempt floors matters more than you think:

Best Practice: Attempt Floor 9 first (easiest 3-team floor). If you can 3-star it, your teams are adequate. Then do Floor 10, then 11, then 12. If you fail on Floor 11, you know exactly which team needs improvement — and you can retune without redoing the earlier floors.

Do NOT attempt Floor 12 first. If your team fails there, you've wasted time and have no information about which team was the problem. Work bottom-up: start with the lowest 3-team floor and climb.

Common Mistakes

Mistake #1: Over-investing in one operative. A level 70 Xavier with perfect gear is impressive. But if your Zayne and Rafayel are level 30 with 0/6 Stellactrum, you won't clear Floor 10. Spread your resources.

Mistake #2: Ignoring Stellactrum rotation changes. You cleared Floor 12 last month with Ruby/Emerald. This month it's Sapphire/Garnet. If you don't recheck and rebuild, you'll fail. The Abyss resets are designed to test your depth, not your memory.

Mistake #3: Using the same team comp every rotation. Different floors favor different operatives. Some rotations favor Zayne (Ice-heavy), others favor Sylus (Dark-heavy). Be flexible. Swap your Team A assignment based on which floor is hardest in the current rotation.

Mistake #4: Not leveling 4-star memories. Four-star memories are not trash. They are your color gap fillers, your Team C enablers, and your rotation-to-rotation flex options. A level 60 4-star with the right color and set bonus is a valuable asset.

The Bottom Line: Abyss is a resource management game disguised as a combat game. The player who distributes their resources wisely across 6 operatives will outperform the player who maxes 2 and ignores the rest. Follow the Three-Team Framework. Check Stellactrum every rotation. Balance your levels. Use 4-star fillers. And remember: a 4/6 match on all three teams is worth more than a 6/6 match on only one. Build depth, not peaks.
