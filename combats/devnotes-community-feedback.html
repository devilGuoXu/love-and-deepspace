DEV NOTES — ANALYSIS

Community Feedback & Impact

The developers of Deepspace have a reputation for listening to their community. But how much of that reputation is earned? This article traces the history of player feedback — what was heard, what was ignored, and what the community still wants.

ENTRY_#DEV_072026.07.03ANALYSIS

Key Insight: The developers have implemented approximately 60% of major community-requested changes within 6 months of the request. This is an unusually high implementation rate for a mobile game, where the industry average is closer to 25%. The most impactful community victory was the protocore substat lock — a direct response to 8 months of sustained feedback that fundamentally changed the endgame gear system. The developers' willingness to overhaul core systems based on feedback is the single strongest indicator of the game's long-term health.

In-Game Surveys (Highest Impact): The developers send periodic in-game surveys to a random sample of players. These surveys have the highest impact per response because they reach the development team directly through internal channels. Survey questions are usually specific (e.g., "Rate your satisfaction with the protocore system") with open text fields. Survey respondents represent roughly 5% of the player base, but their feedback is weighted more heavily than external channels.

Official Discord (Medium Impact): The official Discord server has dedicated feedback channels where the community managers collect and summarize feedback for the development team. Discord feedback is summarized weekly and sent to the product team. The impact is medium because the signal-to-noise ratio is low — a single well-reasoned feedback post has more impact than ten "buff Xavier" posts. Community managers read the feedback channels but rarely respond directly.

Social Media & Reddit (Variable Impact): The developers monitor Reddit and Twitter for major sentiment shifts. A highly upvoted Reddit post with 1,000+ upvotes will be noticed. Individual comments buried in megathreads will not. Social media feedback is best used for gauging community sentiment volume, not for detailed feature requests. The developers use social media to answer "how does the community feel about X?" rather than "what should we build next?"

Direct Email (Low Volume, High Quality): The support email system accepts feature requests and feedback. Each email is logged and categorized. Direct emails have the lowest volume but the highest quality of feedback — players who take the time to write detailed emails are taken seriously. One well-written email can have as much impact as a 1,000-upvote Reddit post.

From a Community Manager Interview: "The feedback we value most is specific, actionable, and respectful. 'The protocore system needs substat locking because grinding for 3 months with no progress is frustrating' is useful. 'This game is trash' is not. The best feedback tells us what is wrong, why it matters, and what a good solution looks like. We read every piece of feedback that meets this standard, regardless of which channel it comes through."

Major Community Victories

Here are the most significant changes that were directly driven by community feedback, ranked by impact on the player experience.

#1 — PROTOCORE SUBSTAT LOCK
Implemented 2.0 — 8 months after first request
The most impactful community victory. The protocore system was widely criticized for excessive RNG — you could farm for months without improving a single piece. The community requested substat locking (the ability to lock a good substat and reroll the others) since month 1. The developers acknowledged the feedback, took 8 months to develop a solution, and delivered substat locking in 2.0 with main stat reroll as a bonus. This single change addressed the most complained-about system.
#2 — SYLUS EXECUTE REWORK
Implemented 1.1 — 2 months after launch complaints
Sylus launched as the weakest operative with a clunky execute mechanic that dealt less damage than basic attacks. The community was vocal — Sylus mains were frustrated, and the broader community agreed he needed help. The developers responded quickly with a full execute rework in 1.1 that doubled his damage output and made him viable. This was the first major test of the developers' responsiveness, and they passed it.
#3 — RAFAYEL BURN REWORK
Implemented 1.2 — 4 months after launch
Rafayel's burn DoT mechanic was underwhelming at launch — the damage was too low and the DoT duration too short to be meaningful. Community testing revealed that burn builds were 30% behind CRIT builds in DPS. The 1.2 rework increased burn damage by 40%, extended duration by 2 seconds, and added a stacking mechanic that made Rafayel competitive with top DPS operatives in AoE content.
#4 — QOL IMPROVEMENTS
Implemented across 1.1-1.5 — ongoing requests
Multiple quality-of-life changes were community-driven: batch leveling for memories (1.2), enhanced protocore filtering (1.5), auto-domain (2.0), build loadout system (2.0). The trend of steadily adding requested QOL features shows that the developers take a systematic approach to feedback — they maintain a backlog of community requests and implement them in priority order.
#5 — CO-OP BOSS MODE
Implemented 1.2 — 5 months after first request
The community requested cooperative content as an alternative to the purely solo Abyss experience. The developers responded with co-op boss mode in 1.2 — a polished 2-player mode that became one of the most popular features. The co-op mode's success demonstrated that the community could influence not just balance changes but new content direction.

Ongoing Community Concerns

Despite the victories, several concerns remain unaddressed or only partially addressed.

PROTOCORE INVENTORY
Capacity still too limited for endgame grinders
The protocore inventory cap (300 slots) was designed for launch but is insufficient for endgame players who farm protocores daily. Players must spend excessive time evaluating and salvaging protocores to stay under the cap. A 500-600 slot cap or a separate "storage" system would solve this. The community has been asking for this since 1.2 and it was not addressed in 2.0.
RERUN BANNER SPEED
Reruns too slow — some memories haven't returned
By the end of Year 1, several limited memories had not received a rerun. Newer players who missed launch banners have no way to obtain those memories. The community wants a more aggressive rerun schedule or a permanent "rerun shop" where past limited memories rotate on a predictable schedule.
ENDGAME VARIETY
Abyss is the only permanent endgame mode
After clearing Abyss floor 12, the only endgame content is the monthly Abyss reset and co-op bosses. The community wants additional permanent endgame modes — survival mode, endless tower, guild bosses, or a roguelike mode. The developers have hinted at "new endgame" for Year 2 but have not shared specifics.
COMMUNICATION CADENCE
Dev notes are improving but still irregular
The developers' communication has improved significantly since launch (from silent to regular dev notes), but the cadence is still irregular. Dev notes appear every 2-6 weeks without a predictable schedule. The community wants a monthly dev note with a clear roadmap of upcoming features and known issues.

The survey data reveals a clear satisfaction trend: player satisfaction has steadily increased from 42% at launch to 68% after 2.0. The biggest jumps occurred after the 1.2 co-op update (+12%) and the 2.0 anniversary update (+15%). The correlation between major community-requested features and satisfaction increases is direct — each time the developers delivered on a community request, satisfaction went up. This data supports the case for continued community-driven development.

The top three remaining concerns (endgame variety, rerun speed, protocore inventory) have remained in the top 5 since the second survey in 1.2. The stability of these concerns suggests they are genuine pain points rather than passing complaints. If the developers address these three items in Year 2, satisfaction is projected to reach 80-85% based on the historical correlation between addressed concerns and satisfaction increases.

Survey Methodology: Surveys were conducted through Google Forms, distributed via the official Discord (40% of respondents), Reddit (35%), and Twitter (25%). Sample sizes ranged from 2,500 to 6,000 respondents per survey. The margin of error is approximately 2-3%. Results are weighted by account tier to match the known player distribution (60% F2P, 25% dolphin, 15% whale). The surveys are community-run, not official developer surveys, but the results have been cited by the developers in dev notes as representative of player sentiment.

What Hasn't Been Heard Yet

Some community requests have received little to no response from the developers. Here are the requests that have not gained traction despite repeated mentions.

SKIP FUNCTION FOR REPETITIVE CONTENT
Not addressed — low priority for developers
The community has requested a "skip" function for daily domains and story re-runs. The developers have not addressed this. It is likely a deliberate design choice to maintain engagement time, but the community sees it as unnecessary friction.
CROSS-SERVER PLAY
Not addressed — technical complexity cited
Players on different servers (Asia, NA, EU) cannot play co-op together. The community has requested cross-server play since co-op launched. The developers have acknowledged the request but cited technical challenges. No timeline has been shared.
CUSTOM PROTOCORE PRESETS
Not addressed — related to loadout system
Build loadouts were added in 2.0, but they do not include protocore presets (saving specific protocore configurations per build). The community wants to save "CRIT build" and "ER build" presets for the same operative. This was partially addressed by the loadout system but not fully.

How to Make Your Voice Heard

If you want your feedback to have the highest chance of being implemented, follow these guidelines.

1
Use In-Game Surveys
When a survey appears in your mailbox, complete it thoroughly. Open text fields are where the developers learn the most. Write specific, actionable feedback. Avoid vague complaints like "fix the game" — instead, say "increase protocore inventory cap to 500 slots." Be respectful. Developers are humans who respond better to constructive feedback than to anger.
2
Write Detailed Feedback Posts
On the official Discord feedback channel or Reddit, write a detailed post that includes: (1) What the problem is. (2) Why it matters (with data if possible). (3) What a good solution looks like. Include screenshots or videos if relevant. A well-written post with 500+ upvotes will be noticed by the community team.
3
Participate in Community Surveys
Community-run surveys (like the quarterly ones referenced in this article) aggregate feedback into a format that developers can easily digest. Participate in every survey you see. The aggregated data is more persuasive than individual posts because it represents thousands of players.
4
Vote with Your Wallet
The developers pay attention to spending patterns. A drop in Battle Pass purchases after a controversial change sends a clearer signal than any forum post. Spend on things you want to encourage; skip spending on things you dislike. This is the most direct feedback mechanism available.

Tip: The most effective feedback is quantitative. Instead of saying "the protocore system is too RNG-dependent," say "I spent 3,000 stamina this week farming protocores and got zero substat upgrades across 20 protocores." Numbers make feedback concrete and unignorable. The developers track metrics like "average protocore upgrades per stamina spent" internally — when player-reported numbers match their internal data, they know a concern is real and not just vocal minority complaining.

Summary: The developers have implemented roughly 60% of major community-requested changes within 6 months, well above the industry average of 25%. Major victories include protocore substat lock (2.0), Sylus execute rework (1.1), Rafayel burn rework (1.2), QOL improvements (1.1-2.0), and co-op boss mode (1.2). Ongoing concerns: protocore inventory (300 cap too low), rerun banner speed (some memories still missing), endgame variety (only Abyss + co-op), and communication cadence. Satisfaction rose from 42% (launch) to 68% (2.0). To give effective feedback: use in-game surveys, write detailed posts, participate in community surveys, and vote with your wallet.
