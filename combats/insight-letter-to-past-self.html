Dear Past Me, and anyone reading this who feels like they are doing it all wrong,

I am writing this on a Tuesday night. My stamina bar is full. I should probably go spend it before I go to sleep, because every minute it sits at cap is a minute of regen I am wasting, and I used to obsess over that kind of thing. I do not anymore. Or at least, I am learning not to.

I remember exactly where you are right now. You are probably level 35 or so. You have one SSR that you pulled from the standard banner and you are not sure if it was a good one. You are hoarding your gems because you read somewhere that you should only pull on limited banners, but there is no limited banner running right now and your finger is hovering over the standard banner pull button like an addict looking at a drink they promised they would not touch. You have 4,000 gems saved. You think that is a lot. Oh, sweetheart.

I remember the first time you hit soft pity. You were at pull 63 on the standard banner and you had given up hope, and then the golden light exploded across your screen and your heart stopped for a full second. You got a memory. You did not care which one. It was gold. It was yours. You leveled it to 60 immediately, spending resources you should have saved, because the high of finally getting something good made you reckless. That is how the game gets you. That is how it gets all of us.

I want to tell you some things that I wish someone had told me. Not advice, exactly. More like. observations from the other side of the hill. You will not believe most of them, because you are in the thick of it and everything feels urgent and permanent. But I am writing them down anyway, because maybe you will remember this letter when the moments come, and it will help.

On Stamina

The stamina anxiety does not go away. I am sorry. But it changes. Right now, you feel like every point of stamina is precious because you have so few domains unlocked and your energy cap is so low. You check the game every few hours to make sure nothing is capped. You plan your day around your stamina refresh. I know. I did the same thing.

Here is the thing nobody told me: when you get to the endgame, you will have more stamina than you know what to do with. Not literally more stamina. The cap goes up, sure, but the real change is that your goals get more specific. Instead of needing everything, you will need one thing. A specific protocore main stat. A specific ascension material. And when you only need one thing, the wasted stamina on days you do not play feels less painful, because it is only wasted on that one thing, not on everything.

Also, and I need you to hear this: go to sleep. Do not stay up until 2 AM waiting for your stamina to hit 140 so you can do one more run. The one more run is never worth it. The protocore you get from that run will be flat DEF and you will feel stupid and tired. I have done this fifty times. Trust me.

On Picking a Main

You are going to spend way too much time agonizing over who to main. You will read tier lists. You will watch comparison videos. You will ask Reddit and get fifteen conflicting answers and feel more confused than when you started. You will level one operative to 60, then second-guess yourself and start leveling another, and end up with two half-built operatives and no one at 70.

Stop. Just stop. Pick the one whose combat animations you like watching. Not the one the tier list says is best. Not the one your friend mains. The one whose attacks look cool to you. I promise you, the difference between the best operative and the worst operative is maybe 10% when both are fully built. The difference between an operative you enjoy playing and one you do not is 100%. A character you like using will make you want to play, will make you learn their mechanics, will make you push through the walls. A character you picked because they were meta will feel like homework.

I spent three months forcing myself to play an operative I did not enjoy because the math said they were better. I was miserable. I switched to the one I actually liked and my clear times went up because I was actually paying attention and learning the fight instead of autopiloting through content I resented.

On Spending

I am not going to tell you not to spend money. That is not my place. But I want you to understand something about the way the game asks you to spend. It never asks you to spend when you are happy. It asks you to spend when you are frustrated. When you lost the 50-50. When you are five pulls from pity and the banner is about to end. When you have been grinding for weeks and you are still stuck on the same floor. The game waits for you to be at your weakest, and then it presents you with the solution, which costs money.

I fell for this so many times. I lost count. But here is the pattern I noticed: every time I spent money out of frustration, I regretted it. Every time I spent money because I was excited about something, I did not regret it. The difference was not the amount. It was the emotion behind the purchase. Happy spending feels good. Frustrated spending feels like relief, and relief fades fast, and then you are left with less money and the same frustration.

Set a rule for yourself now, while you are still early in the game. Something like: I will only pull on the first day of a banner, never the last day. Or: I will only spend what I budgeted before the banner was announced, not what I feel during the banner. The specific rule matters less than having one. The game will test your rule. It will find your edges. But having a rule is better than having no rule, because a rule gives you something to hold onto when the frustration hits and your credit card is in your hand and the banner timer is ticking down.

On the Community

You will find the community eventually. Maybe you already have. There are incredible people in this community. Theorycrafters who spend hours deriving formulas so you do not have to. Artists who pour their hearts into fan content. Writers who analyze the story with more depth than I knew existed. I have made genuine friends through this game. People I talk to every day. People who have helped me through tough times that had nothing to do with protocores.

But there is a dark side too, and I want you to be ready for it. There are people who will try to make you feel bad about who you like. About how much you spend. About how fast you progress. The tribalism in this community is real and it is exhausting. Xavier fans who hate Sylus fans for no reason. F2P players who call whales idiots. Whales who call F2P players freeloaders. People who treat a video game like a team sport and you are either on their team or you are the enemy.

You do not have to engage with any of that. You are allowed to like whoever you like. You are allowed to spend however much you want to spend. You are allowed to play at your own pace. The people who matter in the community will not care about any of those things. They will care about whether you are kind, whether you are interesting, whether you have something to share. Find those people. Hold onto them. Ignore the rest.

On the Story

I want to tell you something that surprised me about this game. I came for the combat. I stayed for the story. I did not expect that. I do not usually care about mobile game stories. They are usually thin, predictable, designed to make you feel attached to characters so you spend money on their banners. And maybe Love and Deepspace is that too, on some level. But it is also more than that.

The writing is genuinely good in places. I have sat with certain story beats for days, turning them over in my head, finding new layers. The characters are more complex than they first appear. The world has thought put into it. There are moments that made me put my phone down and just. sit with what I had read. I did not expect that from a gacha game. I was wrong to underestimate it.

Do not skip the story. I know you are in a rush to unlock domains and clear content. But the story is not filler between combat. It is the reason the combat matters. It gives weight to the characters you are building. It makes you care about the numbers on the screen. I cannot prove this, but I believe that playing through the story with attention makes the game better. The pulls mean more when you know who you are pulling for. The fights matter more when you know what is at stake.

On Quitting

You will think about quitting. Probably more than once. There will be a day, maybe around month three or four, when you look at your account and it feels like you have made no progress. You are stuck on a floor. Your gems are gone. Your protocores are bad. The banner you wanted ended and you did not get what you wanted. You will think: why am I doing this? This is not fun. I should quit.

Maybe you should. I do not know your life. But I know that for me, the urge to quit was almost always connected to a specific frustration that passed. I took a break. Three days. A week. Two weeks, once. And when I came back, the frustration was gone and the game was still there, waiting for me, with new content and new banners and the same familiar loop that I actually, underneath the frustration, enjoyed.

Taking a break is not quitting. It is maintenance. The game is designed to make you feel like you cannot afford to miss a day, but you can. You absolutely can. Missing a week of dailies costs you maybe 420 gems and some materials. That is nothing in the long run. That is two pulls. Missing a month costs you maybe 1,800 gems. That is one multi-pull. In exchange, you get your sanity back. You get to miss the game and remember why you loved it in the first place.

I have played 314 consecutive days. I am proud of that streak in a weird way. But I would trade thirty of those days for the ability to take a break without guilt. Do not let the streak own you. It is a number. It does not love you back.

The Part I Still Cannot Explain

There is something about this game that I cannot quite articulate. Something that keeps me coming back beyond the loops and the numbers and the gacha. It is the feeling of building something. Of watching a team come together over months. Of recognizing how far you have come when you look at old screenshots. Of knowing a boss's patterns so well that the fight becomes a kind of dance.

I remember the first time I cleared Abyss floor 12. I had been stuck on it for three weeks. I was not expecting to clear it that run. I was just practicing, not caring about the timer, focusing on dodging. And then suddenly the boss was at 10% HP and I realized I had not taken damage the entire fight. I went all in. I hit every rotation perfectly. The boss went down and I sat there staring at the victory screen, heart pounding, hands shaking slightly. I took a screenshot. I sent it to my friends. They congratulated me. And then I closed the game and went to bed, and I felt a type of satisfaction that I do not get from most things in my adult life.

That is why I play. Not for the pulls. Not for the numbers. For the moments when everything clicks and you remember that games, at their best, make you feel like you are capable of more than you thought.

You will have that moment too. Maybe not on floor 12. Maybe on a different boss. Maybe when you get a perfect protocore. Maybe when you finally pull the character you have been saving for. But it will come. And when it does, you will know why you kept playing through the frustration and the bad luck and the days when it felt like the game was designed to make you miserable. Because it was. But it was also designed for this. For the moments when it all comes together.

Play well. Take breaks. Love who you love.

— Kaelen, July 2026
