Glasses & Eyewear

Glasses Impact: Glasses are the most impactful accessory because they change the character's face directly. The right frames can make a character look smarter, cooler, or more approachable. The wrong frames can obscure the eyes — the most expressive part of the face. In photo mode, glasses can also create reflections and catch lights that add depth to portraits.

There are three frame styles available in the game: round wireframes, square wayfarers, and aviators. Round wireframes give a scholarly, gentle appearance — best for Xavier and Zayne. Square wayfarers convey confidence and edge — best for Sylus. Aviators are versatile but lean casual — best for outdoor and action shots. Each frame style interacts differently with lighting: round frames catch light evenly, square frames create sharp reflections, aviators have gradient tinting that affects eye visibility.

ROUND WIREFRAME — XAVIER & ZAYNE
Best for scholarly and gentle character concepts
The round shape softens facial features and creates a warm, intellectual aesthetic. Xavier with round glasses and a cardigan is a popular community aesthetic. Zayne with round glasses emphasizes his doctor background. The thin wireframe minimizes face obstruction — the eyes remain fully visible. Lighting tip: position the key light slightly higher to create a subtle shadow under the frames that defines the cheekbones.
SQUARE WAYFARER — SYLUS
Best for confident and edgy character concepts
The sharp angles of wayfarers contrast with facial curves, creating visual tension. Sylus with wayfarers is the most popular accessory combination in the community. The thicker frames draw attention to the upper face. The frames cast shadows on the upper cheeks in dramatic lighting — use this to create a mysterious look. Avoid wayfarers in soft, romantic shots — the angular frames clash with the mood.
AVIATOR — RAFATEL
Best for casual and action-oriented concepts
Aviators have a teardrop shape that follows the eye's natural contour. They look natural in outdoor and action settings. The metal frame reflects light, creating catchlights in portraits. Aviators with a gradient tint (available from events) add a mysterious element — the eyes are partially visible but shaded. Best used with casual outfits (jackets, open collars) rather than formal wear.

Glasses Photography Tip: Glasses create reflections that can obscure or enhance the eyes. To minimize unwanted reflections, adjust the key light angle to 30-40° instead of the standard 45°. This shifts the reflection off the eyes and onto the frame. For intentional reflection effects (stylish lens flare), align the key light with the camera axis. The reflection creates a cool "disco ball" effect in the lenses that some community photographers use for avant-garde portraits.

Necklaces & Earrings

Jewelry adds points of visual interest that draw the eye to specific areas. Necklaces draw attention to the collarbone and chest area. Earrings frame the face and draw attention to the jawline. In photo mode, jewelry catches light and creates sparkle effects that add a premium feel to portraits.

Chain necklaces work best with open-collar outfits. The chain creates a V-shape that extends the neckline visually. Silver chains are versatile, gold chains add warmth. In close-up portraits, chain links create interesting texture. The light catches each link differently, creating a subtle sparkle pattern across the chest. Avoid chains with high-collared outfits — the chain bunches up and looks messy.

Pendants create a focal point at the center of the chest. A pendant with a colored gemstone can tie together the outfit's color palette. For example, a blue pendant complements Xavier's light blue aesthetic. A red pendant matches Sylus's dark red accents. Position the key light to hit the pendant directly — the gemstone catchlight creates a premium jewelry advertisement look.

Earrings are the most effective jewelry for close-up portraits because they frame the face. Stud earrings are subtle — they add a sparkle point near the ear without drawing too much attention. Drop earrings add movement and elegance. Hoop earrings create a frame around the lower face. For male characters, earrings are available as single studs — place on the left ear for a subtle accent that does not overpower the portrait.

Hats & Headwear

Hats dramatically change a character's silhouette. They add height, frame the face from above, and create shadow that can hide or emphasize the eyes. Hats are the most expressive accessory because they change the overall shape of the character's head in the frame.

BASEBALL CAP
Casual, sporty — Shadows upper face — Adds youthful energy
The brim creates a shadow over the eyes, which can be used to create mystery or casual coolness. Angle the camera slightly below eye level to capture the eyes under the brim. Best for outdoor and casual concepts. The cap's fabric texture (visible in close-ups) adds realism.
BERET
Artistic, elegant — Sits to one side — French aesthetic
The beret adds an artistic, European flair. It sits to one side, creating asymmetry. Best for elegant and creative character concepts. The beret's soft fabric does not cast harsh shadows. Position the key light from the opposite side of the beret to keep the face evenly lit.
BEANIE
Warm, cozy — Covers forehead — Winter aesthetic
The beanie covers the forehead and sits low, drawing attention to the lower face. Best for winter and cozy concepts. The knit texture adds visual interest. The beanie compresses the hair, changing the head's overall shape. Works best with high-collared winter coats.

Headwear and hair interaction: Hats change how hair is displayed. Some hats tuck hair in, others leave it flowing. Check the hair-hat interaction before finalizing a shot. The baseball cap typically pulls hair back (ponytail visible). The beret leaves hair mostly visible with the hat sitting on top. The beanie covers the forehead and pushes hair down. Some event hats have unique hair interactions that are not available elsewhere.

Hat Photography Rule: When shooting a character with a hat, increase the rim light intensity by 20%. The hat creates a shadow over the top of the head and upper face. Rim light from behind separates the hat's edge from the background, preventing the hat from blending into dark environments. This is especially important for dark-colored hats against dark backgrounds.

Wristwear & Rings

Wristwear and rings are subtle accessories that reward close-up shots. They are barely visible in full-body shots but become prominent in hand-focused and half-body portraits. These accessories add detail that dedicated viewers will notice and appreciate.

WRISTWATCHES
Watches come in leather strap and metal bracelet variants. Leather straps are classic and formal — best with suits and formal wear. Metal bracelets are modern and sporty — best with casual and smart-casual outfits. The watch face reflects light — position the character so the watch catches a subtle reflection. In close-ups (FOV 50mm+), the watch details are visible and add realism to the character. Each character has different watch preferences in community style guides: Xavier wears leather strap, Sylus wears metal bracelet.
BRACELETS
Bracelets add color and texture to the wrist area. Leather wrap bracelets are casual and rugged. Chain bracelets are edgy and modern. Beaded bracelets add color — match bead colors to the outfit's accent color. For photo purposes, bracelets are most visible when the character has their hands at their sides or crossed. Arm-reaching poses show off bracelets well. Stack multiple bracelets for a layered look — but keep it to 3 or fewer to avoid visual clutter.
RINGS
Rings are the smallest accessory but the most personal. Signet rings convey authority. Band rings are minimalist. Gemstone rings add color. In hand-focused poses (holding a book, touching the face, reaching toward the camera), rings become prominent focal points. Position a hand near the face in a portrait to incorporate the ring into the composition. The ring catching light creates a small bright point that draws the eye.

Ring Stacking Guide: Index finger ring + middle finger ring + pinky ring = balanced stack. Avoid rings on the thumb and ring finger simultaneously — the visual weight is uneven. For photo purposes, rings on the hand that is closer to the camera are more visible. In side-profile shots, showcase the hand that is in front with rings. Silver rings photograph better than gold in artificial lighting — gold can look brassy under cool LED light.

Matching Accessories to Outfits

The golden rule of accessory matching: accessories should complement the outfit, not compete with it. An outfit with strong patterns and textures needs minimal accessories. A simple, solid-color outfit can handle bold accessories.

✓ DO
  • Match metal tones — silver with cool outfits, gold with warm outfits
  • Use one statement accessory per outfit (glasses OR necklace, not both)
  • Consider the neckline — V-neck = necklace, crew neck = earrings
  • Balance visual weight — heavy hat = no chunky necklace
  • Match accessory color to outfit accent color
  • Use accessories to tell a story — glasses for scholar, hat for adventurer
✗ DON'T
  • Don't use all accessory slots — empty space is part of the composition
  • Don't mix warm and cool metal tones (silver + gold = clash)
  • Don't let accessories obscure the character's eyes
  • Don't use hats with hoods — they clip through each other
  • Don't match accessory shapes to face shapes poorly
  • Don't forget to check the back of the character — accessories look different from all angles
1
Start with the outfit
Choose the outfit first. Identify its dominant color, neckline style, and overall mood (casual, formal, edgy, romantic). The outfit determines which accessories will work.
2
Choose one focal accessory
Select one statement piece. Glasses are the strongest focal point. Necklaces are second. Hats are third. Limit yourself to one focal accessory per outfit.
3
Add subtle secondary pieces
If the outfit feels bare, add subtle secondary accessories (small earrings, a simple ring). Secondary accessories should not compete with the focal piece.
4
Check from all camera angles
Accessories look different from different angles. A necklace that looks perfect from the front may look awkward from a 45° angle. Rotate the camera and check every angle before finalizing.
5
Lighting check
Accessories interact with light. A reflective necklace can create unwanted glare. Rim light can make earrings glow beautifully or create harsh shadows. Adjust lighting after adding accessories.

How to Unlock Accessories

Accessories are unlocked through various systems. Some are available from the start, others require specific progression.

Base game glassesUnlocked at Affection Level 15 with any character
Premium glassesBattle Pass level 30 reward
Jewelry setsEvent shop purchases (limited time). Check the current event shop.
Event hatsLimited-time event rewards. Missed hats may return on reruns.
WatchesAchievement rewards (reach Hunter Level 40 and 50)
Rare ringsAbyss floor 10+ completion rewards (cycle rewards)

Top priority accessories to unlock: (1) Round wireframe glasses (Affection 15) — most versatile photography accessory. (2) Silver chain necklace (event shop) — works with most outfits. (3) Leather strap watch (Hunter 40) — adds realism to formal shots. (4) Beret (limited event) — unique silhouette that changes portrait dynamics. (5) Signet ring (Abyss 12) — prestige accessory that signals achievement.

F2P Accessory Strategy: Focus on unlocking base game accessories first (Affection rewards, achievement rewards). These are permanent unlocks that do not expire. Event accessories are tempting but temporary — only spend event currency on accessories if you have already bought the essential items (pulls, protocores, or memory shards). Accessories are cosmetic only — they do not affect gameplay stats. Prioritize gameplay upgrades over cosmetic accessories unless photography is your primary interest.

Summary: Glasses are the most impactful accessory — choose frame style based on character personality (round for Xavier/Zayne, square for Sylus, aviator for Rafayel). Jewelry adds visual interest — chains for necklines, pendants for focal points, earrings for face-framing. Hats change silhouette — baseball cap for casual, beret for elegant, beanie for cozy. Match metal tones to outfit temperature (silver = cool, gold = warm). Use one statement accessory per outfit. Unlock base game accessories first (Affection 15 glasses, Hunter 40 watch), then expand with event pieces. Accessories are cosmetic — spend upgrade currency on gameplay first, cosmetic items second.
