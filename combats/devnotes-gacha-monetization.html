DEV NOTES — MONETIZATION

Gacha & Monetization Analysis

Love and Deepspace's gacha system is the engine that drives the game's revenue and the primary source of player anxiety. This analysis breaks down every aspect of the monetization system — banner types, pity mechanics, expected value calculations, revenue comparisons, and how the system compares to industry standards.

ENTRY_#DEV_092026.07.07MONETIZATION

Key Insight: Love and Deepspace's gacha system is actually more generous than the industry average for comparable games. The soft pity system, combined with the 50/50 guarantee and the regular flow of free currency, means that a dedicated F2P player can expect 1 limited SSR every 2-3 banners. The perception of stinginess comes from comparison to non-gacha games rather than from the actual math. However, the system has legitimate problems — particularly the Protocore monetization layer and the lack of a reliable dupe system.

Banner Types & Mechanics

Limited SSR Banner (The Main Event). The primary revenue driver. Each limited banner features a new SSR memory card for a single operative with a 50% rate-up on the first SSR pull. If you lose the 50/50 to a standard SSR, your next SSR is guaranteed to be the limited card. The hard pity is 70 pulls with soft pity activating around pull 60. Limited banners run for 2-3 weeks and are the most efficient use of premium currency for character-specific progression.

Standard Banner (The Safety Net). Permanent banner with all standard SSR memories available. No rate-up, no guarantee for specific cards. Pity carries over indefinitely. The standard banner is where free standard tickets are spent. It is not efficient for targeted progression but provides long-term collection value. Most players will complete their standard collection within 6-12 months of active play.

Weapon/Equipment Banner. Separate banner for limited weapons and equipment. Different pity timer and currency. Equipment banners are generally considered lower priority because equipment has less impact than memories, and there is no 50/50 guarantee — the rate-up is lower. Equipment banners are best skipped by F2P and low-spender players.

Rerun Banners. Limited banners that return older SSR memories. Reruns typically run for shorter durations (7-10 days) and overlap with new banners. A rerun is the only opportunity to acquire a limited memory you missed. The frequency of reruns is approximately 6-9 months after the original run. The developers have committed to eventually rerunning all limited memories, though the schedule is not fixed.

Special Banners. Occasional banners with unique mechanics — step-up banners (discounted first pulls), selector banners (choose the rate-up character), or double-rate banners (increased SSR rates for a limited time). These are rare and typically tied to anniversaries or major celebrations. Special banners represent the best value for pulling and should be prioritized when available.

Pity System Breakdown

The pity system is the most important mechanic to understand for efficient pulling. Here is how it works in detail.

Soft Pity Range. The rate of pulling an SSR increases significantly starting at pull 60 on any banner. Pulls 60-69 have incrementally increasing rates, and pull 70 is guaranteed hard pity. The soft pity range means that most SSRs actually drop between pulls 65-69 rather than at hard pity. Only approximately 15% of SSRs require the full 70-pull hard pity. The average pulls per SSR across the player base is approximately 55-58.

50/50 Mechanics. On a limited banner, the first SSR you pull has a 50% chance of being the featured card and a 50% chance of being a standard SSR. If you lose the 50/50, your next SSR is guaranteed to be the featured card. This guarantee carries over between limited banners — if you lose the 50/50 on the current banner, your first SSR on the next limited banner is guaranteed to be the featured card. This carryover is essential to understand because it means that losing the 50/50 is not a waste — it is an investment in a future guarantee.

Pity Carryover. Pull count carries over between banners of the same type. Limited banner pulls carry over to the next limited banner. Standard banner pulls keep their own count. Equipment banner pulls are separate. This carryover means that you can safely pull on a banner without fear of "wasting" pity — any pulls you make count toward the next banner if you do not get an SSR.

Dupe System. Pulling duplicate SSR memories grants ascension materials that enhance the memory's stats and unlock additional effects. The dupe system is the primary endgame resource sink for whales. For F2P and low-spenders, duplicates are rare and should not be targeted — the value of a new memory far exceeds the value of a dupe enhancement.

Expected Value Calculations

Understanding the expected value of your pulls helps you make informed decisions about resource allocation.

Expected Pulls for Target SSR. On a limited banner, the expected number of pulls to get the featured SSR is approximately 93 (accounting for the 50/50 and averaging over the soft pity distribution). This means that if you save 100 pulls worth of currency before a banner starts, you have approximately a 55% chance of getting the featured card. Saving 140 pulls (two full pities worth) gives you a 95%+ chance.

Monthly Free Currency. A F2P player can expect approximately 30-40 pulls worth of free currency per month, including daily missions, events, and maintenance compensation. A monthly pass subscriber can expect 45-55 pulls per month. A battle pass + monthly pass player can expect 55-70 pulls per month. These numbers vary based on event frequency and generosity.

Banner Clearing Cost. To guarantee a single copy of a limited SSR (worst case: lose 50/50 at hard pity, then hit hard pity again), the cost is 140 pulls. In real currency terms, this is approximately $230 if purchasing top-up packs at maximum inefficiency, or approximately $160 if purchasing the best-value packs. Using monthly pass savings over time, the effective cost drops significantly.

Revenue Model Analysis

Love and Deepspace's revenue model combines multiple monetization layers that target different player segments.

Character Monetization. The primary revenue driver. Players pull for limited SSR memories of their favorite operatives. The character attachment drives spending more than gameplay necessity. Revenue spikes during popular character banners (the "husbando/waifu factor") and during anniversary events. Character banners account for approximately 65% of total revenue.

Cosmetic Monetization. Outfits, glider skins, and weapon skins form the second revenue layer. Outfits are priced at a premium ($20-50 equivalent) and generate significant revenue from players who prioritize fashion over combat. Cosmetic items account for approximately 20% of total revenue and have the highest profit margins (no development cost for game systems, purely art production).

Convenience Monetization. Battle pass, monthly pass, and resource packs form the third layer. These items sell convenience — faster progression, more resources, less grinding. The battle pass is the most popular purchase item due to its high value ratio. Convenience items account for approximately 15% of revenue but have the highest purchase rate (largest number of individual buyers).

Purchase Value Ranking

Not all purchases are created equal. Here is a ranking of every purchase option by value per dollar.

Tier 1 — Monthly Pass. The best value in the game. At approximately $5 per month, it provides 3000+ crystals over 30 days plus daily rewards. Value per dollar: 10-12x compared to direct top-up. This is the only purchase that every player should consider if they are willing to spend anything at all.

Tier 2 — Battle Pass (Premium). Excellent value at approximately $10 per season. Includes guaranteed SSR resources, exclusive outfit, exclusive profile items, and premium currency. Value per dollar: 6-8x compared to direct top-up. The battle pass is the best value for style-focused players due to the exclusive cosmetic items.

Tier 3 — New Player Packs. One-time purchase packs that offer steep discounts on pulls and resources. These are designed to convert new players into spenders and offer genuine value. Value per dollar: 4-6x. These packs are time-limited and should be prioritized by new spenders.

Tier 4 — Limited Shop Packs. Event-specific packs that offer limited resources and pulls at moderate discounts. Value varies by event but is generally 2-4x direct top-up. These packs are best purchased only if you need the specific resources they contain.

Tier 5 — Direct Top-Up. The baseline. Purchasing crystals directly at full price. The value is the same regardless of when you purchase. Direct top-up should be the last resort after exhausting all discounted purchasing options.

F2P Experience & Fairness

The F2P experience in Love and Deepspace is better than the industry average but has specific pain points that are worth understanding.

What F2P Players Get. A dedicated F2P player can clear all PvE content, collect approximately 4-6 limited SSR memories per year, build 1-2 strong teams, and participate in all events. F2P players are not locked out of any content — there is no F2P-only content restriction. The gameplay experience is complete without spending.

What F2P Players Miss. F2P players cannot collect every limited memory (this requires whaling), cannot compete at the top of PvP leaderboards, and miss cosmetic items tied to the battle pass. F2P progression is slower in the endgame, particularly in Protocore farming where refresh currency is limited.

Fairness Assessment. Love and Deepspace scores above average on F2P fairness compared to similar games. The pity system ensures that F2P players can guarantee specific memories with enough saving. The free currency income is sufficient for steady progression. The primary F2P complaint — limited Protocore farming attempts — is valid but affects all players equally regardless of spending.

Industry Comparison

How does Love and Deepspace's monetization compare to other popular gacha games? Here is a comparative analysis.

vs. Genshin Impact / Honkai Star Rail. Love and Deepspace has a slightly higher pity (70 vs 90) and slightly more generous free currency income. However, it lacks a weapon banner guarantee system (Genshin's epitomized path) and has worse dupe value. Overall, the system is comparable in fairness, with Love and Deepspace being slightly more generous for F2P players and slightly less generous for dolphins.

vs. Nikke / Blue Archive. Love and Deepspace has higher pity costs (70 pulls vs 200 mileage in Nikke) but significantly better rate-up mechanics. The 50/50 guarantee in Love and Deepspace makes targeted pulling more reliable than the mileage-based systems in competitor games. F2P players in Love and Deepspace have an easier time getting specific characters than in mileage-based games.

vs. Otome/Date Sim Competitors. Love and Deepspace is significantly more expensive than traditional otome games (which typically offer full routes for a fixed price) but provides ongoing live-service content that traditional otome games do not. The gacha model allows for continuous content updates that would not be financially viable under a premium pricing model. For players who value ongoing content, the gacha model provides better long-term value despite higher per-character costs.

Future Monetization Predictions

Based on industry trends and developer comments, here are projected changes to the monetization system.

Possible Improvements. A dupe exchange system (trade duplicates for selector tokens), a weapon banner guarantee (matching Genshin's epitomized path), expanded battle pass tiers with more cosmetic options, and subscription bundles with better value for mid-spenders.

Possible Concerns. Power creep in new memories that pressures players to pull, expanded Protocore monetization (paid refresh packs), battle pass feature splitting (separating paid tracks for outfits vs resources), and increased banner frequency that overwhelms F2P savings.

Our Assessment. The developers have demonstrated a player-friendly approach to monetization, avoiding aggressive monetization tactics common in the industry. We expect this approach to continue as long as revenue targets are met. If revenue declines, expect monetization to become less generous. For now, the system is fair and sustainable.

Spending Rule of Thumb: Never pull on a banner unless you are prepared to reach full pity (140 pulls for guarantee). Partial pulling — spending 30-40 pulls hoping to get lucky — is the most inefficient spending pattern. Either commit to the full guarantee or skip the banner entirely.

Summary: Love and Deepspace's gacha system uses limited SSR banners with 70-pull hard pity, 50/50 rate-up, and pity carryover between banners. The expected pulls for a target SSR is approximately 93. Free currency income allows F2P players approximately 30-40 pulls per month. The revenue model targets three segments: character collectors (65% of revenue), cosmetic buyers (20%), and convenience purchasers (15%). The best purchase value is the monthly pass, followed by the battle pass. The F2P experience is above industry average — complete PvE access with slower endgame progression. Compared to competitors, Love and Deepspace is slightly more generous than Genshin Impact and more reliable than mileage-based systems. Future monetization changes may include dupe exchange and expanded battle pass options, but the developers' player-friendly track record suggests continued fairness.
