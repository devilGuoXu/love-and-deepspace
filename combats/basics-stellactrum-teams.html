BASIC PROTOCOLS — STELLACTRUM

Stellactrum Team Building

Color coverage is the unsung hero of team composition — plan your stellactrum investments to cover all endgame requirements.

ENTRY_#0762026.07.02STELLACTRUM

How to Read This: This guide assumes you know the basics of stellactrum matching. If you do not, read Stellactrum Guide (#002) first. This guide focuses on strategic color planning for endgame content.

Written by Kaelen — Deepspace Hunter Station Team

Why Color Coverage Matters

Stellactrum matching gives a flat damage bonus per matched color. A full 6-color match gives approximately 15-20% more damage than a 2-color match. In Abyssal Trials and SHC, this bonus is often the difference between a clear and a wipe. However, you cannot cover all 6 colors for every operative — resources are limited. The solution is strategic coverage: invest in 3-4 colors deeply, and use flex memories for the remaining colors.

Abyss Color Requirements

Abyssal Trial floors have specific stellactrum requirements. Here are the patterns: Floors 1-40: Any color works. Use this range to test new team compositions. Floors 40-60: One specific color is required per stage. If you lack it, you lose 20% damage. Solution: Build 2 memories of each color for your main operatives. Floors 60-80: Two colors required. You need at least 2 operatives covering the required colors. Floors 80-100: Specific elemental + color requirements. These are the hardest to plan for because they overlap. General rule: Build 2 memories of each stellactrum color for your 2 main operatives. This covers 90% of Abyss requirements.

SHC Color Requirements

Senior Hunter Contest is more forgiving than Abyss on colors but less forgiving on gear score. SHC stages have recommended colors but do not penalize mismatches as heavily. The usual requirement: 2-4 matching colors per stage. How to plan: (1) Check the SHC rotation schedule for upcoming color requirements. (2) Build a "color flex" memory for each operative — a memory of a color they do not normally use. This memory might be suboptimal in stats but provides the color match bonus. (3) Use your best gear on stages where your colors naturally align. Use flex memories on stages where they do not.

Stellactrum matching affects resonance chains as well as base damage. A team with three matching colors has 40% higher chance of 2-element resonance chains. Four matching colors gives 60% higher chance. Two B-tier operatives with perfect matching often outperform two A-tier operatives with no matching.

The 70/20/10 Rule: Spend 70% of your memory investment on your 2 main operatives' primary colors. Spend 20% on secondary color memories for your main operatives (flex slots). Spend 10% on a third operative's memories for coverage. This allocation ensures you have strong primary teams for most content while maintaining flexibility for color-restricted stages. Do not try to build all 6 colors evenly — that is a trap that leaves every color underpowered.

Color Investment Priority

Not all colors are equal in value. Based on endgame content analysis: 1st Priority: Your main DPS operative's signature color. This is the color you will use in 70% of content. 2nd Priority: The secondary color that shares a set bonus with your main color. Many memory sets have 2-color bonuses that pair naturally. 3rd Priority: The color your second operative excels in. 4th Priority: Wildcard colors that appear frequently in Abyss/SHC rotations. Last Priority: Colors that only appear in 1-2 floors per rotation. You can brute-force these with gear — the 20% color bonus is not worth building an entire memory for if it only applies once per month.

Set Bonuses vs Color Matching

There is an important tension: set bonuses require specific memory combinations, but color matching requires specific stellactrum colors. When they conflict: (1) If your set bonus is >15% damage (most 4-set bonuses), prioritize the set over the color. (2) If your set bonus is <10% (most 2-set bonuses), prioritize the color match. (3) If you can achieve both through gear swapping, always choose the combination. The ideal state is having multiple memories of the same set with different colors, allowing you to mix and match based on content requirements. This is the endgame gear goal.

Summary: Color matching gives 15-20% damage bonus. Use the 70/20/10 rule for investment: primary colors > secondary > flex. Build 2 memories per color for your main operatives. Prioritize set bonuses over colors when the set is >15% damage. Endgame goal: multiple memories per set with different colors for flexible composition.
