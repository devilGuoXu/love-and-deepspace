Community Growth Timeline

The Love and Deepspace community has experienced remarkable growth since launch. January 2025 — Global launch, Reddit reaches 10K subscribers, Discord at 5K members. April 2025 — First major update (1.2), Reddit hits 50K, Discord at 20K. July 2025 — Summer event + first free SSR event, subreddit breaks 100K. October 2025 — First anniversary (CN server), community crossover with other Papergames titles. January 2026 — Reddit at 250K, Discord at 100K, first major fan convention meetup at Anime Expo. May 2026 — 2.0 update, subreddit surpasses 500K subscribers. July 2026 — 2nd Anniversary, total community across all platforms estimated at 1.5M+ members.

The community's growth has been organic, driven by the game's quality and the developers' support for fan content. Papergames has officially recognized community events through: retweeting fan art on official channels, sponsoring cosplay contest prizes, providing in-game items for community giveaways, and featuring fan-created content in official social media posts. This developer-community relationship is unusually collaborative — many gacha game communities feel disconnected from the developers, but LADS has maintained an open dialogue through regular surveys, community manager engagement, and fan content repurposing.

Past Contests & Competitions

Launch Fan Art Contest (Feb 2025)
The first official community contest, held during the global launch period. Theme: "First Impressions" — art depicting your first encounter with each operative. 500+ entries across Twitter and Pixiv. Winners received in-game currency bundles and exclusive title cards. The contest set the template for all future art events: 2-week submission window, community voting for top 20, panel judging for final winners.
Summer Cosplay Showcase (Jul 2025)
A cosplay-focused event coinciding with the first summer in-game event. Categories: Best Combat Outfit, Best Casual Outfit, Best Prop, and Best Group (4+ cosplayers). Featured a virtual runway on Discord with cosplayers submitting 30-second videos. 200+ participants globally. Winners received in-game outfits and cosplay material vouchers.
Memory Card Redesign Challenge (Nov 2025)
Community members redesigned existing memory cards in their own art styles. Trending hashtag #LADSRedraw reached 50K+ posts on Twitter. The challenge was not officially sponsored but was unofficially recognized by the developers, who featured top entries on their Twitter account. This contest demonstrated the power of community-driven events without official sponsorship.
2nd Anniversary Fan Festival (Jul 2026)
The largest community event to date — a month-long celebration across all platforms. Events included: Art Contest (1,200+ entries), Cosplay Parade (300+ participants), Build Challenge (competitive Abyss speedruns with community-submitted teams), Fan Fiction Competition (200+ stories), and a Community Charity Drive ($15,000 raised for mental health organizations). The festival was officially supported with in-game rewards for participants and winners.

Contest participation tips: Read the rules carefully — most contests have specific formatting requirements (file type, resolution, watermark rules). Submit early — many contests have a "first X entries" bonus or early submission recognition. Engage with other participants — comment on their entries, share tips, build community connections before the winners are announced. The community recognition from being an active participant often exceeds the value of winning. Even if you do not place in the top 3, consistent participation builds your reputation in the community.

Each milestone event has been organized by volunteer community moderators, demonstrating the strength of the community's self-organizing capacity.

Fan-Led Initiatives

Beyond official and milestone events, the community has organized numerous fan-led initiatives. The LADS Build Help Collective (Discord-based, 15,000+ members) provides free build evaluations and rotation coaching. The Translation Project (200+ volunteers) translates guide content between English, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, and Spanish. The F2P Savings Club (Reddit weekly thread, 50,000+ participants) tracks diamond savings goals and provides pull-planning accountability. The Lore Archive (community wiki, 5,000+ pages) catalogs all story content, character backgrounds, and world-building details.

These initiatives are entirely volunteer-run and demonstrate the community's investment in the game beyond playing it. The most impactful initiative has been the F2P Savings Club — a weekly Reddit thread where players post their current diamond count, their target banner, and their progress toward hard pity. The thread has helped thousands of F2P players maintain discipline and avoid impulse pulls. The thread's community accountability model (checking in weekly with your savings goal) is more effective than individual willpower for most players. If you are F2P and struggling to save diamonds, join the Savings Club thread — it is posted every Monday on r/LoveAndDeepspace.

Starting your own initiative: If you have an idea for a community project, the best approach is to start small. Create a Discord server or Reddit thread with a clear purpose. Announce it in the main community channels (with moderator permission — message mods first). Recruit 2-3 co-organizers to share the workload. Set realistic goals for the first month (50-100 members, not 5,000). Document everything — successful initiatives often serve as templates for future organizers. The LADS community is welcoming to new initiatives, especially those that solve a specific problem (build help, savings accountability, translation). Find a gap and fill it.

Charity & Fundraising Events

The LADS community has organized four major charity events. The first (Oct 2025) raised $5,000 for disaster relief following a typhoon that affected the Papergames headquarters region. Community members organized a 12-hour live stream featuring build workshops, art tutorials, and co-op boss carries. The second charity event (Dec 2025 — "Winter Warmth Drive") raised $8,000 for children's hospitals, with donations matched by a group of whale players. The third (Feb 2026 — "Anniversary Gives Back") raised $10,000 for mental health awareness, coinciding with the game's themes of emotional support and interpersonal connection.

The fourth and largest charity event was part of the 2nd Anniversary Fan Festival (Jul 2026), raising $15,000 for mental health organizations across three countries. The event featured 48-hour streaming marathon, art auction (community artists donated prints and commissions), and a cosplay calendar fundraiser. Charity events are organized by a rotating committee of community members, with new organizers recruited each cycle to prevent burnout. If you want to contribute to charity events but cannot donate money, volunteer your skills — artists create donation incentives, streamers host fundraising blocks, translators help with international outreach. Every skill set is valuable in charity organizing.

How to Get Involved

1
Join the community hub
The LADS Community Hub (link in Workshop resources) aggregates all official and fan-run event announcements. Subscribe to the calendar for contest deadlines, meetup dates, and charity events. The hub also has a "volunteer board" where initiative organizers post roles they need filled.
2
Introduce yourself
Post an introduction in the relevant community channel: #artists for art contest participants, #cosplayers for cosplay events, #builders for build challenges. Include your main operative(s) and what kind of events interest you. The community is welcoming to newcomers — do not be shy.
3
Participate in a small event first
Start with a low-commitment event: a weekly fan-art prompt, a build discussion thread, or a community poll. These require minimal time investment (15-30 minutes) and help you understand the community's culture and norms before committing to larger events.
4
Volunteer for an initiative
Once you are comfortable, offer your skills to an existing initiative. Even 1-2 hours per week of volunteer time (moderating a thread, proofreading translations, helping new players in the build channel) makes a meaningful difference to the community.
5
Organize your own event
After 2-3 months of community participation, consider organizing a small event. A themed fan-art prompt for a specific operative's birthday, for example. Start with a defined scope (1 week, 1 platform, clear rules). Use the Community Event template (available from event organizers) to structure your planning.

Lurking is okay too: Not every community member needs to be an active participant. If you prefer to just enjoy the art, read the builds, and watch the streams without contributing, that is perfectly valid. Communities need consumers as much as they need creators — your views, upvotes, and encouragement are valuable. The most important thing is to be respectful in comments and to credit creators when sharing their work. Passive participation that follows community norms is always welcome. Participate at the level that feels comfortable for you.

Upcoming & Recurring Events

The LADS community event calendar includes both one-time and recurring events. Recurring events: Monthly Fan-Art Prompt (first week of each month — specific theme voted by community), Weekly Build Help Thread (every Monday on Reddit), Bi-weekly Cosplay Check-in (every other Sunday on Discord — share WIP progress, get feedback), Quarterly Charity Stream (March, June, September, December — organized by rotating committee).

Upcoming one-time events: Xavier Birthday Celebration (Mar 15-21, 2027 — fan-art showcase, cosplay gathering, community video project), Summer 2027 Cosplay Contest (Jul 2027 — categories: best screen-accurate, best creative interpretation, best group), Fan Fiction Anthology (Dec 2027 — community submission book, proceeds to charity), and 3rd Anniversary Fan Festival (Feb 2028 — the biggest community event yet, planning starts Sep 2027). Mark your calendar and set reminders 1 month before each event you want to participate in.

Event planning timeline: If you want to organize a community event, start planning 2-3 months in advance. Month 1: Define scope, recruit co-organizers, set budget (if any). Month 2: Announce event, open submissions, recruit judges/volunteers. Month 3: Run the event, manage submissions, promote daily. Post-event: Distribute prizes, publish results, gather feedback for next iteration. The most successful community events are planned with this timeline. Last-minute events (announced 1-2 weeks ahead) struggle with participation and organization quality. Give yourself and the community enough time to prepare.
