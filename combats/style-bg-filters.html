Photo Backgrounds & Filters

Every background location, every filter combination, and how to compose shots that look like official promo art.

ENTRY_#STY_042026.07.02PHOTO

How to Read This: New photographers start with the background unlock guide. Veterans skip to the filter stacking section for advanced techniques.

Background Locations Guide

Photo mode backgrounds are divided into three categories: Story Locations: Unlocked by progressing through main story chapters. Each major story area becomes a photo background once visited. If you are missing a background, check your story progress. Event Backgrounds: Limited-time backgrounds from events. These are the rarest — if an event background expires, it may not return for 6-12 months. Always screenshot the event background during the event period even if you do not plan to use it immediately. Companion Room Backgrounds: Unlocked by leveling companion affinity to specific thresholds. Each operative's room has 2-3 unlockable variations based on furniture placement and affinity level. Pro tip: The best backgrounds are often the simplest — solid color walls from companion rooms let the character stand out. Avoid overly busy backgrounds that compete with the operative for visual attention.

Filter System Explained

The filter system has 8 base filters, each with adjustable intensity (0-100%). Warm: Adds golden/orange tones. Best for sunset vibes and romantic shots. Cool: Blue/cyan tones. Best for night scenes, ice-themed Zayne shots, and melancholic moods. Vivid: Increases saturation. Use at 30-50% for a natural pop. Above 60% looks artificial. Moody: Increases contrast and darkens shadows. Best for Sylus and dark-themed compositions. Soft: Diffuses highlights and reduces contrast. Ideal for Rafayel's ethereal aesthetic. Noir: Black and white with high contrast. Timeless but niche — use for dramatic portraits. Fade: Washes out colors for a vintage look. Good at 20-40% intensity. Bloom: Adds a dreamy glow to highlights. Excellent for Xavier's light-themed abilities. Stacking rule: You can combine up to 3 filters. The order matters — the game applies them left to right. Place your strongest filter last.

Pro Composition Rules: (1) Rule of thirds — enable the grid overlay and place your subject's eyes on the top horizontal line. (2) Leading lines — use background architecture, roads, or furniture edges to draw the eye toward the operative. (3) Negative space — leave 30-40% of the frame empty for a premium magazine-cover look. (4) Depth layering — position the operative with at least one object in the foreground and one in the background. This creates a 3D feel. (5) Catchlights — position the light source so it reflects in the operative's eyes. A small white dot in each eye makes the character look alive.

Background-Specific Settings

Each background type needs different settings: Outdoor (Glintwood, N109, beach): Use Warm or Vivid filter at 30%. Aperture f/2.8-f/4 for background blur. Avoid direct noon lighting — the golden hour (warm-tinted setting) is universally flattering. Indoor (companion rooms, cafe,豪宅): Use Soft or Bloom filter at 20%. Aperture f/1.8-f/2.2 for maximum bokeh. Position artificial lights to create rim lighting on the operative's hair. Fantasy (deepspace, dream sequences): Use Moody + Cool at 50% each. Aperture f/2.0. Add fog/particle effects if available through event props. Studio (plain backgrounds): Use Noir or Fade at 30%. Focus entirely on the operative's expression and pose. The simpler the background, the more the character's emotion matters.

Event Background Priority

Not all event backgrounds are equal. Must-get: Anniversary backgrounds (usually the most elaborate), Valentine's Day backgrounds (romantic lighting is versatile), beach/summer backgrounds (universally useful for casual shots). Optional: Holiday-specific backgrounds (Christmas, Halloween — seasonal use only), collab backgrounds (good but limited reuse). Skip if low on currency: Minor festival backgrounds, recolored versions of existing backgrounds. Tip: Event backgrounds cost event currency. Budget enough to grab the background before spending on other items. A good background is used hundreds of times; a costume recolor is used once.

Summary: Unlock backgrounds through story progression and events. Stack up to 3 filters — strongest last. Use rule of thirds and leading lines. Outdoor = Warm, Indoor = Soft, Fantasy = Moody+Cool. Event backgrounds are high priority investments. The best photo uses 30% negative space and catchlights in the eyes.
