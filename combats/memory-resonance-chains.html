Memory Resonance Chains: R1 to R6 Power Analysis

Every memory can be ranked up six times by pulling duplicates. Each rank grants stat bonuses and passive upgrades — but not all ranks are equal. This guide breaks down which resonance tiers transform your account and which ones are best left on the bench.

How Resonance Works

Every memory in Love and Deepspace can be ranked up six times — from R1 to R6 — by pulling duplicate copies of that memory on gacha banners. Each resonance rank grants additional stat bonuses and, at specific breakpoints, upgrades to the memory's passive abilities. The rank-up system is identical across SSR and SR memories, though the magnitude of stat gains scales with rarity.

Resonance ranks are transformative at different thresholds depending on the specific memory. Some memories gain entirely new mechanics at R1 or R2 — a second hit on their resonance skill, a new team buff, or a completely different rotation pattern. Others gain only incremental stat improvements until R6, where a final capstone ability unlocks. Learning to distinguish transformative ranks from minor ones is the single most important skill for efficient duplicate spending.

The stat bonuses per rank follow a predictable curve: ATK, DEF, and HP increase by roughly 4-6% per rank, with R1 and R6 receiving slightly larger bumps (7-9%) alongside their mechanical upgrades. The power difference between an R0 memory and an R6 memory of the same card is typically 40-60% in raw stats plus whatever mechanical advantages the rank-up passives provide.

Rank-Up Tiers

Not all resonance ranks are created equal. Based on extensive testing across every SSR memory currently available, we classify rank-ups into four power tiers. Use this classification to prioritize where your duplicate pulls go.

Transformative (S-Tier)
Ranks that fundamentally change how a memory plays. These typically unlock a new mechanical interaction — a second resonance charge, a team-wide buff that reshapes rotation priority, or a completely new damage trigger. Examples: Xavier's Stellar Contract R1 (adds a second light stack), Sylus's Twilight Oath R1 (enables self-heal loop). If a memory has a transformative rank, that rank is the #1 priority target.
Strong (A-Tier)
These ranks provide a 20% or greater increase in effective combat performance. They don't change how the memory functions, but they dramatically improve its numbers — enough to push a team over a DPS check or enable clears that were previously impossible. Most R2 ranks fall here, along with certain R4 ranks on high-investment carries.
Moderate (B-Tier)
A solid 10-20% boost. These ranks are noticeable but not run-defining. They help with stat benchmarks and scoring consistency. The majority of R3 and R5 ranks sit in this tier. Worth getting if you're maining that memory, but not worth pulling extra copies specifically for.
Minimal (C-Tier)
Under 10% power increase or purely quality-of-life improvements. Slightly longer buff duration, marginally better healing, a tiny CD reduction. These ranks are nice-to-haves but should never be a spending target. Most R6 capstone abilities fall here — the final rank is often more about completionism than combat necessity.

Rule of Thumb: If a rank doesn't add a new icon to your buff bar or change your rotation, it's probably Moderate or Minimal. Transformative ranks always come with visible gameplay changes.

F2P Resonance Strategy

For free-to-play and low-spending players, every pull matters. You cannot afford to chase every memory's resonance ranks. Here is a practical framework for allocating duplicate pulls efficiently.

★
Worth Chasing R1
  • Memories where R1 unlocks a new mechanic (e.g., Stellar Contract, Twilight Oath)
  • Limited SSR memories that are best-in-slot for their element
  • Memories you plan to use in every abyss rotation for the next 3+ months
Sweet spot target: 1-2 R1 memories per limited banner cycle
✓
Fine at R0
  • Most standard-pool SSR memories
  • All SR memories (never chase SR ranks)
  • Memories whose R1 is only stat-based (no new mechanic)
  • Support memories you only use for their element type
Majority of your collection should sit here
✗
Skip (Need R3+ to Function)
  • Memories with critical upgrades locked behind R3 or R4
  • Memories whose base kit has anti-synergy without high ranks
  • Any memory requiring R6 to match R0 performance of alternatives
Hard skip — use better alternatives at R0 instead

The rule of thumb for limited banners: R1 is the most cost-effective resonance target. You need exactly 2 copies of a memory to reach R1 (the initial pull + one duplicate). To reach R3, you need 4 copies total — the cost doubles for diminishing returns. Past R3, the cost-to-power ratio drops off a cliff: R6 requires 7 copies total, and the last 3 ranks combined usually contribute less than 20% additional power.

For standard-pool memories, never chase resonance. You will eventually get duplicate copies through lost 50/50s, off-rate pulls, and free selector boxes. The only exception is if a standard memory's R1 is S-Tier transformative AND you have no limited alternative that fills the same role.

F2P Mantra: One copy is a character. Two copies is an investment. Four copies is a luxury. Seven copies is a mistake (unless you're whaling). R1 is the efficiency sweet spot — maximize your roster breadth before deepening any single memory.

Whale vs F2P Perspective

The resonance calculus changes dramatically depending on your spending tier. Here is how different budgets should approach the rank-up system.

Whale targets (R6 chase): Memories that scale exceptionally well into late ranks are worth taking to R6. The key indicators are: (1) the memory gains meaningful power at every rank, not just at breakpoints, (2) the R6 capstone provides a unique effect that cannot be replicated by any other memory, and (3) the memory is best-in-slot for a meta-relevant team comp. Stellar Contract (Xavier) and Twilight Oath (Sylus) are the current top R6 targets — both gain significant power at every rank and their capstones enable mechanics no other card provides.

F2P targets (R1 on key pieces): Save hard for R1 on 2-3 key SSR memories per year. A well-chosen R1 memory outperforms an R6 memory of a lower-tier card in almost every scenario. Prioritize memories that unlock new mechanical interactions (which scale with your skill) over ones that provide raw stats (which have diminishing returns against content).

The cost-to-power curve tells a clear story: R1 is where you get the most bang for your pull. The first duplicate adds 25-40% effective power at roughly 2x the cost of the base memory. R2 adds another 10-20% at 1.5x the R1 cost. Past R2, each additional rank costs more pulls but delivers smaller returns. For whales, the total cost to take one memory from R0 to R6 is approximately 490 pulls (assuming average luck) — enough to R1 three different memories instead.

The final verdict: A roster of six R1 limited SSRs will outperform a roster of two R6 limited SSRs and four R0 standards in every game mode. Resonance depth is a trap for most players. Invest in breadth first, deepen only on your absolutely most-used carries, and never feel pressured to chase ranks past R2. The game's content is clearable at R0 — every rank above that is a luxury that makes life easier, not a necessity.

Summary: R1 is the sweet spot — new mechanics at the best efficiency. R0 is fine for most memories. R2 is good for your carries. R3+ is for whales only. Prioritize memories with transformative S-Tier ranks (Stellar Contract R1, Twilight Oath R1, Inferno Waltz R4). Spend on breadth, not depth. The cost to R6 one memory (490 pulls) could R1 three different memories instead.
