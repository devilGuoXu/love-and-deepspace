Dev Balance Philosophy

Dev Balance Philosophy: Love and Deepspace's developers rarely nerf characters directly. Instead, they buff underperformers and add new content that challenges dominant strategies. This means: (1) Your favorites will never be nerfed into uselessness. (2) But they may become less effective as new content favors different characters. (3) Investing in buffed characters is always safe — the devs do not reverse buffs.

This philosophy sets Deepspace apart from many gacha games where the meta shifts through direct nerfs. In Deepspace, your investment is always protected — a character who is strong today will never be made weak tomorrow. However, "not nerfed" does not mean "always optimal." New characters and new content can indirectly reduce a character's relative strength even if their absolute power stays the same.

"We believe players should feel confident in their investments. If you build a character, that character should remain viable forever. Our job is to make every character feel like the right choice for someone, not to force a single meta."

— Developer Note, Patch 1.1

Xavier — Light Seeker

VERSION 1.0 — LAUNCH
No direct changes. Xavier was the most-balanced operative from day 1. He dominated Abyss due to Light element's wide coverage (only one enemy type resists Light). Xavier remains the consistent DPS benchmark that all other operatives are compared against.
VERSION 1.1 — MINOR ADJUSTMENT
Light Slash projectile speed increased by 15%. This was a quality-of-life change, not a DPS increase. It made the skill easier to land on moving targets. Effective damage increase: 0%. Xavier's damage output was already well-balanced; the devs only polished his feel.

Verdict: The devs consider Xavier the "baseline" for DPS. Other operatives are balanced relative to him. If an operative is weaker than Xavier, they get buffed. If an operative is stronger, the devs introduce content that counters them rather than nerfing them. Xavier has never been touched directly because he represents the "correct" power level.

Sylus — Dark Champion

VERSION 1.0 — LAUNCH (UNDERPOWERED)
Execute threshold: 20% HP. Execute bonus: 30% damage. Cooldown: 15s. At launch, Sylus was widely considered the weakest operative. The execute mechanic was undertuned — by the time enemies reached 20% HP, they were already dying to auto-attacks. Sylus was a "win more" character when he should have been an execute finisher.
VERSION 1.1 — MAJOR REWORK
Execute threshold increased from 20% HP to 30% HP. Execute damage increased from 30% bonus to 40% bonus. Cooldown reduced from 15s to 12s. Result: Sylus went from bottom tier to A-tier in a single patch. The execute window is now large enough to make a meaningful difference in boss fights.
VERSION 1.2 — BUG FIX (HIDDEN BUFF)
Execute now properly benefits from CRIT DMG (was bugged at launch — only base ATK was used for execute damage). Effective damage increase: ~25%. This was listed as a "bug fix" in patch notes but was actually a significant damage buff. Sylus's execute now scales with his entire build, not just base ATK.

Verdict: Sylus is the most-buffed operative. The devs clearly wanted him to be a viable alternative to Xavier, not a meme pick. From bottom tier at launch to A-tier by 1.2, Sylus's trajectory shows the devs' willingness to rework underperformers aggressively. Total effective damage increase from launch to 1.2: ~80–100%.

Zayne — Frostguard

VERSION 1.0 — LAUNCH
Zayne was already the best support at launch. His shield provided the only consistent sustain in the game. He was mandatory for Abyss floor 8 (survival check). However, his healing was considered weak — 12% max HP over 3 seconds was not enough to recover from heavy hits.
VERSION 1.1 — HEALING BUFF
Shield healing increased from 12% of max HP to 15% of max HP. Duration increased from 3s to 4s. Result: Zayne became the best sustain support in the game. The extra 1 second of healing made a significant difference in recovery between boss attacks.
VERSION 1.2 — UTILITY BUFF
Frostguard 4pc set effect: Shield now also cleanses one debuff every 8s. Result: Zayne became mandatory for Abyss floors with debuff mechanics. This change effectively created "Zayne-check" floors — if you bring Zayne, the debuff floor is easy. If you do not, it is significantly harder.

Verdict: Zayne has been consistently buffed. The devs value support characters and want them to feel impactful. Zayne's trajectory shows that the devs consider sustain and utility to be underappreciated by the community. They deliberately make support characters stronger over time to encourage diverse team building.

Rafayel — Inferno Blaze

VERSION 1.0 — LAUNCH (AOE NICHE)
Rafayel was considered a niche AoE operative. His burn DoT was slow (2s tick interval) and his single-target damage lagged behind Xavier. Players only used him in multi-target floors where his burn could hit multiple enemies simultaneously.
VERSION 1.2 — MAJOR REWORK
Burn DoT tick interval reduced from 2.0s to 1.3s (with 4pc). Burn damage increased by 15% base. Result: Rafayel's total DPS increased by ~35%. He became the best AoE operative and competitive with Xavier in single-target if the enemy was vulnerable to Fire.
VERSION 1.3 — BUG FIX (STACKING)
Burn DoT now properly stacks with other fire damage sources (was overwriting instead of stacking). Effective increase: ~10% in team compositions with multiple fire sources. This change made Rafayel essential in fire-element team compositions.

Verdict: Rafayel was a late bloomer. His 1.2 rework made him competitive with Xavier in AoE scenarios. Total effective DPS increase from launch to 1.3: ~50%. The devs clearly wanted every operative to have a distinct identity — Xavier (single-target), Sylus (execute burst), Zayne (sustain), Rafayel (AoE burn).

System improvement pattern: The devs release system improvements every 2–3 patches. These always benefit long-term players more than new players. The substat roll floor (1.1) made farming more consistent for everyone. The main stat guarantee (1.2) reduced early-game frustration. The pattern suggests we can expect another major system improvement around 1.4–1.5 — possibly the protocore substat lock feature teased in developer notes.

Pattern Recognition

Pattern Recognition: The devs make 3 types of balance changes: (1) Direct operative buffs — always happen in the first 2 patches after a character's release. If a character is not buffed in the first 2 patches, they are considered "balanced" and will not receive direct changes. (2) System improvements — happen every 2–3 patches. (3) Content shifts — new enemies or mechanics that indirectly shift the meta without changing any numbers. Watch for pattern (3) — it is the most common and most overlooked balance lever.

Pattern 1 — Release weak, buff later: New characters are often released slightly undertuned. After 4–6 weeks, they receive a buff. This makes early adopters feel rewarded while giving the devs data to calibrate. Sylus and Rafayel both followed this pattern.

Pattern 2 — Powercreep through systems, not numbers: New memories and protocores powercreep old ones, not direct character nerfs. If a character is too strong, the devs introduce content that counters them (e.g., enemies that resist their element) rather than nerfing the character directly.

Pattern 3 — Anniversary shakeup: Every anniversary introduces a new system (SSR selector, substat lock, new memory tier) that significantly shifts the meta. Save resources for anniversary.

Pattern 4 — Event character exclusivity: Limited characters return approximately once every 6 months on a rerun banner. They do not enter the standard pool. If you miss a limited character, you wait 6 months minimum.

Lessons from Balance History

Six key takeaways from the balance history of Deepspace:

1. Never judge a character in the first 2 weeks
They will almost certainly be buffed. Sylus was considered "worst operative" at launch. Today he is A-tier. Give new characters time before deciding to skip or invest.
2. Supports are future-proof
The devs value support characters (Zayne) and have consistently buffed them. Investing in supports is safe — they become more valuable over time as content gets harder and requires more sustain.
3. System improvements favor long-term players
The longer you farm, the more you gain from system changes like substat roll floors and main stat guarantees. Stick with the game and the systems will reward your patience.
4. Bug fixes = hidden buffs
After every patch, test your main's skills for undocumented changes. Sylus's "bug fix" in 1.2 was a 25% damage increase. Read the bug fix section of patch notes carefully.
5. No character has ever been nerfed
Your investments are never wasted — but they may become less optimal as new characters release with stronger baseline kits. The devs never take away power; they only add more options.
6. Read every patch note in full
The "bug fixes" section is where the most important changes hide. Cross-reference community tests after every patch to find undocumented changes.

Summary: Xavier — baseline, never nerfed. Sylus — major buff (1.1), now A-tier (+80–100% from launch). Zayne — consistently buffed, best support (healing + cleanse). Rafayel — late bloomer (1.2 rework), AoE king (+50% from launch). System changes favor long-term farmers. No character has ever been nerfed. Buffs always within first 2 patches. Bug fixes = hidden buffs. Read every patch note — the changes that matter are in the "bug fixes" section.
