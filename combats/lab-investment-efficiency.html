F2P vs Whale Investment Efficiency

Not all spending is equal. The first dollar you spend has 10x the impact of the hundredth. Understand the diminishing curves across every resource type to know exactly where your gems, stamina, and money are best deployed.

Resource Types & Diminishing Ceilings

Key Concept: Marginal returns on every resource type drop sharply after a threshold. The gap between doing nothing and doing something is enormous. The gap between doing well and doing perfectly is tiny. Recognising where each resource ceiling sits is the single most important skill for efficient account progression.

Deepspace has four resource categories, each with a distinct diminishing curve. Stamina has a fixed daily cap — you cannot buy more than ~600 per day without exorbitant gem costs. Gems are purchasable but the price escalates with every refresh tier. Real Money is unlimited in principle but the per-dollar value drops the more you spend (monthly card > battle pass > top-up packs > direct gem purchases). Time is the only unlimited resource — but every hour spent grinding is an hour not spent doing something else, and the opportunity cost compounds.

The critical insight: each resource type has a different "ceiling" where further investment yields <10% of the initial return. A whale who spends $500/month is not 10× stronger than a monthly-card player spending $50/month. The actual power gap is closer to 30–40% — and the last 10% of that gap costs 90% of the total spend. Understanding where your personal ceiling is lets you stop throwing resources at dead ends.

Stamina Efficiency Curve

Stamina is the most fundamental resource in Deepspace. It gates memory XP, protocore enhancement materials, ascension materials, and event currency. The value of each stamina point depends entirely on which tier of refresh you are using.

Stamina Rule: Never refresh past the 50-gem tier unless it is a double-drop event or the last day of a limited banner shop. The 50-gem refresh is borderline value (only for double-drops). The 100-gem refresh is for emergency use only. The 150+ gem refreshes are whale territory and give worse gem-to-progress ratios than simply buying a top-up pack and pulling for memories. One limited memory SSR gives more account value than 5 days of 200-gem stamina refreshes.

F2P strategy: Use all natural regen (240) plus daily pack (60) for 300 stamina/day. Never do gem refreshes. This gives ~2100 stamina/week — enough to farm 3–4 domain types consistently. Prioritise protocore farming above all else because protocores are permanent power, while ascension materials become obsolete once a memory is maxed.

Light spender strategy: Same 300/day baseline. Consider the 50-gem refresh (1 per day) during double-drop protocore events. Skip normal-day refreshes. Spend the saved gems on monthly card + battle pass instead — those give you more total stamina-equivalent value through the rewards track.

Whale strategy: 50-gem refresh daily is acceptable. 100-gem refresh only during double-drop weeks. Beyond that, the gems are better spent on memory banner pulls — new memories unlock new team compositions and content clearing ability, whereas extra stamina only accelerates existing builds by marginal amounts.

Memory Pull Investment

Memory banners are the primary gem sink in Deepspace. The value of each pull depends on whether you are F2P (free gems only), a light spender (monthly card), or a whale (unlimited budget). The key metric is SSR per patch — how many five-stars you can expect to acquire in each 3-week patch cycle.

F2P
Pull Priority: New SSR > nothing
You get one SSR every 6 weeks. Every pull must count. Never pull for dupes. Never pull on standard banner. Save all gems for limited banners where the featured SSR is a character you do not already own. A new character gives you 100% new team-building flexibility. A dupe gives you 20–30% more damage on an existing team. The new character wins every time.
Light Spender
Pull Priority: New SSR > C1 for main > C2 for main DPS
You get roughly one SSR per patch. You can afford to C1 or C2 your primary DPS over time. The optimal strategy is: first patch pull the character, second patch (on rerun) pull C1/C2. Do not chase C3 — the cost of three extra copies (360+ pulls on average) is better spent pulling a different SSR. A team of four C0 SSRs is stronger than one C3 SSR with three SRs.
Whale
Pull Priority: C3 main DPS > C0 supports > C6 vanity
If you are whaling, the most efficient path is C3 on your primary DPS (the last resonance rank before dupe value collapses) and C0 on every support. Going past C3 is for flexing, not for clearing content. No content in the game requires C6. The damage difference between C3 and C6 is ~20–30%, but the cost difference is 3× the pulls. That same gem investment in a second team would give you 100% more roster depth.

Universal Pull Rule: A new SSR is always more valuable than a dupe, regardless of your spending tier. The only exception is if you already own every Limited SSR in the game — and even then, saving for the next patch's new character is better than vertical investment on an existing one. Roster breadth beats roster depth until you have at least two complete teams for Abyss.

Protocore Investment

Protocores are the deepest power system in Deepspace and the area where spending tiers converge most dramatically. Unlike memory pulls — where whales can brute-force by buying more — protocores are gated by RNG, not by money. A whale can buy more stamina to farm more protocore domains, but they cannot buy perfect substats. The gap between a well-invested F2P account and a whale is surprisingly narrow in this system.

The implication is stark: protocores are an F2P-friendly system. An F2P player with correct main stats and +12 enhancement is only 15% behind a monthly-card player with +15 and good substats. And that monthly-card player is only 5% behind a whale with perfect god-rolls on every piece. The whale paid 100× more for that 5% — and in real gameplay, 5% DPS is rarely the difference between clearing and not clearing a floor.

Protocore Investment Rule: Stop at "good enough." Correct main stat + 2 useful substats + +12 enhancement = 90% of the potential power. Going from this baseline to perfect god-roll +15 takes ~10× the stamina investment for ~10% more power. That stamina is better spent building a second team or farming for a different operative. The last 10% of protocore perfection is the most expensive power in the game — and it is completely unnecessary for clearing all content.

F2P priority: Get the correct main stat on all 6 slots. Even if every substat is flat DEF, the correct main stat gives you 80% of the piece's value. +12 enhancement is the cost-effective cap — going from +12 to +15 costs ~200% more enhancement XP for ~15% more stat value (diminishing returns on the enhancement cost curve).

Light spender priority: Take DPS protocores to +15. Start filtering for 2+ usable substats (ATK%, CRIT Rate, CRIT DMG, Energy Recharge). +15 on slot 4 (CRIT Rate/DMG) and slot 6 (ATK%) gives the biggest return. Leave support protocores at +12 with correct main stats.

Whale insight: Even with unlimited stamina refreshes, god-roll protocores are RNG-gated. You can spend $1000 on stamina refreshes and still not see a quad-ATK% roll. The expected cost for a single perfect protocore is ~$200–400 in refresh stamina. The expected cost for a full set of 6 perfect protocores is ~$1500–3000. That investment gives you ~5% more total team DPS. Compare this to $1500 in memory pulls — which could give you 3–4 new SSRs and completely transform your roster. The choice is clear.

Where to Spend vs Save

The decision framework is simple: invest your resources where they give the highest marginal return per unit spent. Below is a tiered evaluation of every major spending option in the game, ranked from best value to worst.

Stamina refreshes during double-drop events — Best stamina value in the game. Double-drop effectively doubles the value of every stamina point. A single 50-gem refresh during double-drop gives the same protocore farming value as two normal-day 50-gem refreshes. This is the only time gem refreshes are unequivocally worth it for light spenders.

Monthly card — Best real-money value in the game. 300 gems/day for 30 days + 300 paid gems upfront = ~9300 gems for $5. That is 1860 gems per dollar. Compare to direct top-up which gives ~120 gems per dollar. Monthly card is ~15× more efficient. Every player who spends any money should buy the monthly card first. It is not close.

Battle pass — Second-best value. ~$10 for ~3000 gems value + materials + exclusive rewards. The materials alone (enhancement XP, ascension crystals, protocore boxes) are worth roughly 2000 stamina if purchased separately. The gem value is ~1000. Total package ≈ 3000 stamina-equivalent for $10. Only buy if you can reach level 50 — incomplete passes are poor value.

Limited memory banners — Good value, but conditional. Pulling for a new character you will actually use is excellent value (100% new account power). Pulling for a character you do not need is zero value regardless of cost. The key is selectivity: F2P should pull on ~1 of 4 banners. Light spenders on ~1 of 2. Whales can pull on all, but should still prioritise new characters over dupes.

Normal banner (standard wish) — Never pull with gems. The standard banner has no rate-up, no pity carryover between banners, and a diluted pool. The only acceptable way to pull on standard banner is with free standard tickets the game gives you through events and battle pass. Every gem spent on standard banner is a gem wasted.

Skin purchases — Cosmetic only. Zero combat value. Skins cost 980–1680 paid gems typically. For the same price (980 gems), you could do 19 limited banner pulls — 1/3 of the way to pity. If fashion matters to you, skins are the only way to customise your character's appearance and they will never be power-crept. But they do not make your account stronger in any measurable way. Buy skins only if you have disposable income and understand you are paying for aesthetics, not power.

Summary decision framework: Ask yourself three questions before every resource expenditure. (1) Does this increase my account power? (2) Is this the most efficient way to increase my account power? (3) Would I regret this if a better banner or event drops next week? If the answer to any of these is "no" or "not sure," save your resources. The most powerful action in Deepspace is doing nothing — hoarding gems until the optimal spending window appears.

Summary: F2P and whale accounts converge in protocore power (20% gap, diminishing after +12/correct main stats). They diverge in roster breadth — whales own 3–4× more SSRs. The most efficient investment path is monthly card → battle pass → limited banners for new characters → C1/C2 for mains → stamina refreshes during double-drops → everything else. Never pull standard banner. Never chase C6. Never refresh stamina outside double-drop events. The first $5 on the monthly card gives more account value than the next $500 on top-up packs. Spend smart, not big.
