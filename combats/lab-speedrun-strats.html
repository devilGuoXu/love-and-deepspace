Speedrun Optimization Guide

The gap between a casual clear and a speedrun clear is not gear — it is rotation optimization. Frame-perfect skill sequencing, animation cancels, and AI pathing abuse can cut 30-50% off your clear time without changing a single protocore.

ENTRY_#LAB_092026.07.03DPS

Key Insight: The single biggest time save in any Abyss speedrun is eliminating "dead time" between skill casts. Most players lose 2-4 seconds per rotation due to animation lock, delayed input queuing, and unnecessary movement. A 30-second Abyss clear has ~25 seconds of active skill usage and ~5 seconds of dead time. The goal of speedrunning is to reduce dead time to under 1 second. This is achievable through precision input timing, not faster reflexes.

Core Speedrun Principles

Before diving into specific rotations, understand the four principles that govern every speedrun technique in Deepspace.

Principle 1 — Minimize Movement: Every step you take is time you are not dealing damage. Speedrun rotations are designed to minimize repositioning. Pre-position before the fight starts. Use dashes for movement only when they also deal damage (Sylus execute dash). Avoid strafing — stand still and let the enemy come to you if possible. The ideal speedrun has you in the same 2-meter radius for the entire fight.

Principle 2 — Input Queuing: Deepspace's combat system allows you to queue the next skill input during the current skill's animation. The input window opens approximately 0.3 seconds before the current skill ends. Pressing the next skill during this window eliminates frame delay between casts. Players who do not use input queuing lose 0.5-1.0 seconds per skill transition. Over a 5-skill rotation, that is 2.5-5.0 seconds of dead time — enough to drop a full clear tier.

Principle 3 — Cooldown Alignment: Speedrun rotations are built around aligning your highest-damage skills with your damage buff windows. A non-aligned rotation misses 15-25% of potential DPS because burst skills are used outside of buff windows. Plan your skill order so that your two main burst skills (typically skill 3 and ultimate) are always used within your self-buff window. The standard pattern is: Buff → Skill 3 → Skill 2 → Ultimate → Skill 1 → Auto-attack filler.

Principle 4 — Enemy Pathing Abuse: Enemy AI follows predictable pathing. You can abuse this to clump enemies together for AoE skills. In multi-wave floors, position yourself so that the next wave spawns within your current AoE's radius. This technique, called "wave merging," can save 5-10 seconds per floor by eliminating the gap between waves. Practice enemy spawn locations — they are fixed per floor.

Speedrun Mindset: "Every frame is a resource. If you are not dealing damage, buffing, or repositioning efficiently, you are wasting that frame. The best speedrunners in Deepspace do not have faster reaction times — they have better input planning. They know what button they will press 3 skills ahead. Speedrunning is a puzzle of efficiency, not a test of reflexes." — Top 10 Abyss Speedrunner Interview

Speedrun Rotation Steps

Here is the optimized speedrun rotation sequence that applies to most DPS operatives. Adjust the specific skill names for your operative.

0
Pre-Fight — Pre-Position & Pre-Buff
Before the timer starts: move to the exact center of the enemy spawn zone. Activate any pre-castable buffs (some self-buffs can be activated 0.5s before the fight starts). Queue your opening skill. This pre-fight setup saves 2-3 seconds compared to players who buffer after the timer starts.
1
Opening — Self-Buff + Gap Close
Cast your self-buff ability. While the buff animation plays, input-queue your gap closer or opening skill. The ideal opening has the buff animation ending simultaneously with your first damaging skill reaching the enemy. This sync eliminates the 0.5s delay most players have between buffing and attacking.
2
Burst Window — Skill 3 + Ultimate
Within the buff window (usually 6-8 seconds), cast your two highest-damage skills. Skill 3 first (faster cast time), then immediately cancel into ultimate. The ultimate animation provides i-frames that protect you during the end of your buff window. Queue ultimate input during Skill 3's final 0.3s.
3
Filler — Skill 2 + Auto-Attacks
After the burst window, use Skill 2 (medium damage, shorter cooldown) while auto-attacking. Use animation canceling on auto-attacks: attack twice, dodge-cancel the third attack's recovery, attack twice again. This pattern increases auto-attack DPS by ~15% compared to full auto-attack chains.
4
Wave Transition — Zero-Delay Reposition
As the last enemy in a wave dies, immediately use a movement skill toward the next spawn point. The death animation of the last enemy provides ~1 second of "free" repositioning time. If you move during this window, you lose zero clear time between waves. Practice spawn positions until you can muscle-memory the rotation.
5
Execute Phase — Burst Reposition
If using Sylus or another execute mechanic, save your execute for the final 25% of the boss's HP. Execute damage is often doubled or tripled compared to normal damage. Do not use execute at the start of the fight — the damage penalty makes it inefficient. Execute timing is the difference between a 25-second clear and a 35-second clear on boss floors.

Rotation Time Budget: Pre-fight (0s), Opening + Buff (2s), Burst Window (4s), Filler Phase (8s), Wave Transition (0s with practice), Execute (4s). Total optimized rotation: ~18 seconds for a single-wave floor, ~28 seconds for a two-wave floor. Compare this to the average player's rotation: Pre-fight (2s delay), Opening (3s), Buff (1s), Burst (5s with delay between casts), Filler (10s with dead time), Wave Transition (3s delay), Execute (5s). Total average: ~29s for single-wave, ~42s for two-wave. The optimized rotation saves 30-35% of clear time through input efficiency alone.

Animation Cancels & Input Buffering

Animation canceling is the most advanced speedrun technique. Here are the exact frames where you can cancel skills.

BASIC ATTACK CANCEL
Attack → Attack → Dodge → Attack → Attack
The third hit of most basic attack chains has a long recovery animation (~0.5s). Cancel this recovery by dodging immediately after the second hit lands. The dodge animation takes 0.2s, and you can immediately start a new attack chain. This increases auto-attack DPS by 12-18% depending on the operative. Practice until the cancel is muscle memory — it takes ~50 reps to automate.
SKILL CANCEL — ULTIMATE
Skill 3 → Ultimate (queue during final 0.3s)
Most skill 3 animations have a 0.3-0.5s recovery window at the end. Canceling this recovery by queueing your ultimate input during the recovery window saves 0.3-0.5s per rotation. This is the highest-value single cancel in the game. The timing window is tight — too early and the skill does not cast; too late and you waste the recovery frames.
DASH CANCEL — SKILLS
Any skill → Dash during animation recovery
Dash canceling is useful when you need to reposition mid-rotation without losing DPS. The dash animation overlaps with the skill's recovery frames, so you lose zero time. Only dash-cancel if repositioning is necessary — otherwise, use the ultimate cancel for maximum DPS.
SWAP CANCEL (CO-OP)
Skill → Swap Operative → Skill
In co-op mode, swapping operatives cancels the current skill's recovery animation. Use this to chain skills between two operatives without any dead time. The optimal co-op rotation is: Operative 1 Skill → Swap → Operative 2 Skill → Swap → Operative 1 Skill. This allows both operatives to contribute DPS simultaneously with zero downtime.

Tip: Practice animation cancels in the training room. Set the dummy to "stationary" and practice the Skill 3 → Ultimate cancel until you can do it consistently 10 times in a row. Time yourself — your cancel should add zero additional time between the two casts. If you see even a 0.2s delay on the timer, your input queue timing is off. Most players need 100-200 reps to master the timing.

Time-Saving Techniques by Operative

Each operative has unique speedrun techniques that leverage their specific kit mechanics.

Xavier (Light Seeker): Xavier's speedrun advantage is his light dash — a movement skill that also deals damage. Use light dash off-cooldown as both repositioning and damage. The optimal Xavier rotation is: Buff → Light Dash → Skill 3 → Ultimate cancel → Light Dash → Skill 2 → Auto-attack cancel. Light dash should have 100% uptime in a speedrun — a missed light dash is 2-3 seconds of lost DPS.

Sylus (Execute Protocol): Sylus speedrunning revolves around execute timing. The optimal strategy is to bring the boss to 30% HP as fast as possible, then execute at exactly 25% HP (not 20% or 15%). The execute damage multiplier peaks at 25% HP. Using execute earlier (30%) or later (20%) reduces total execute damage by 15-25%. Sylus speedruns are won or lost on this timing.

Rafayel (Inferno Waltz): Rafayel's burn DoT benefits from pre-stacking. In multi-wave floors, apply burn to the first wave and let the DoT finish them off while you start damaging the second wave. This technique, called "burn carry-over," effectively gives Rafayel double DPS during wave transitions. Apply burn -> swap target -> burn spreads automatically with his AOE passive.

Zayne (Frostguard): Zayne speedruns are unusual because he is a support. In co-op speedruns, Zayne is the enabler. His optimal rotation is: Shield (pre-fight) → ER buff → Swap to DPS → Re-shield when ER buff expires. Zayne should not be on the field for more than 2 seconds per rotation — every second Zayne is active is a second your DPS is not dealing damage.

✅ DO for Speedrunning
  • Practice input queuing — press next skill during current skill's last 0.3s
  • Pre-position before the timer starts — stand at spawn center
  • Use animation cancels (auto-attack cancel, ultimate cancel)
  • Wave merge — time kills so next wave spawns in your AoE
  • Align burst skills with self-buff windows
  • Record and review your runs — identify dead time visually
❌ DON'T for Speedrunning
  • Don't move unnecessarily — every step is lost DPS
  • Don't use execute above 30% HP — damage penalty is severe
  • Don't break auto-attack chains with early dodges (use the 2-hit cancel pattern)
  • Don't ignore wave spawn positions — learn them per floor
  • Don't play passively — speedrunning requires aggressive positioning
  • Don't forget to pre-buff before the fight starts

Tip: The most underrated speedrun tool is the in-game timer. After each run, note your clear time and identify which rotation segment had the most dead time. If your opening takes 4 seconds instead of 2, practice input queuing. If your wave transition takes 3 seconds, practice pre-positioning. Incremental improvements of 0.5-1.0 seconds per segment add up to 5-10 second total improvements. This is how top speedrunners improve — they do not make massive changes; they optimize each micro-segment.

Summary: Speedrunning is about eliminating dead time. Core techniques: input queuing (press next skill during current skill's last 0.3s), animation cancels (auto-attack cancel after 2 hits, ultimate cancel during Skill 3 recovery), pre-fight pre-positioning, wave merging, and cooldown alignment. Xavier: maximize light dash uptime. Sylus: execute at exactly 25% HP. Rafayel: burn carry-over between waves. Zayne: minimize field time — shield and swap. Practice each technique individually in the training room before combining them into a full rotation.
