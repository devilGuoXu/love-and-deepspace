Damage Calculator Tutorial

Step-by-step guide to using the community DPS calculator — from importing your stats to comparing theoretical builds.

ENTRY_#WRK_032026.07.02TOOLS

How to Read This: If you have never used a damage calculator before, start from the beginning. If you know the basics, jump to the build comparison section.

What is a Damage Calculator?

A damage calculator is a spreadsheet or web tool that simulates your operative's damage output based on their stats, protocores, memory sets, and rotation. Instead of guessing whether 50% CRIT + 120% CDMG is better than 40% CRIT + 150% CDMG, you can plug in the numbers and get an exact answer. The community maintains several calculators — they all use the same damage formula but have different interfaces. This guide uses the most popular web-based calculator (link in workshop resources). The calculator takes about 10 minutes to set up initially, then 2-3 minutes per build comparison.

How to Import Your Build

Step 1: Open your operative's stat screen in-game. Screenshot or write down: Base ATK, bonus ATK%, CRIT rate, CRIT damage, elemental damage%, energy regen%. Step 2: Open your memory set screen. Note which sets you have equipped (4-pc or 2+2) and their activation status. Step 3: Open your protocore screen. Record each protocore's main stat and the 4 substat values. This is the most tedious part — but most calculators let you skip substats if you just want a rough estimate. Step 4: Enter everything into the calculator's input fields. The calculator will display your current DPS, your burst damage, and your sustained DPS. Tip: After the first full input, save your build as a preset. Most calculators support save/load functionality. You only need to do a full input once per operative.

Comparing Builds

This is the calculator's real power. Create a second build with different stats and compare: Swap a protocore: Change one protocore's main stat (e.g., HP% → ATK%) and see the exact DPS difference. Change your memory set: Switch from 4-pc Frostbite to 2+2 and see if the set bonus is worth losing substats. Try different substat distributions: Move 10% from CDMG to CRIT and see which ratio performs better. The key metric: Look at "average DPS over a 60-second rotation," not just burst damage. Burst damage is flashy but sustained DPS clears content. A build with 20% less burst but 15% more sustain is usually better for abyss floors. Common discovery: Most players find that their current build is 5-15% below optimal. The fix is usually swapping one protocore or adjusting one substat focus.

Common Pitfalls: (1) Forgetting to factor in set bonus uptime — 4-pc sets that only activate for 8 seconds every 30 seconds are weaker than they look. The calculator handles this if you set uptime correctly. (2) Not including teammate buffs — if your team has a support with ATK% buff, include it in the calculator. Solo DPS numbers are misleading for content where you run a full team. (3) Over-relying on theoretical max DPS — the calculator assumes perfect play (100% uptime, no missed dodges, optimal rotation). Real DPS is 80-85% of theoretical. If the calculator says you need exactly X DPS to clear a floor, you actually need ~1.2X. (4) Ignoring energy regen — many calculators default to 100% ER. If your build has 140% ER, the calculator will underestimate your DPS because it does not account for extra ultimates. Adjust the rotation length parameter.

Calculator Recommendations

Not all calculators are equal. Here are the recommended ones: Web-based (recommended for most players): The community spreadsheet linked in the workshop resources. Free, always updated, covers all operatives. Good for quick comparisons. Desktop app (for power users): Python-based local calculator. Supports batch simulation (compare 100 builds at once). Requires basic Python knowledge to run. Mobile-friendly: Google Sheets template optimized for phone screens. Slower to input but accessible anywhere. Choosing a calculator: Start with the web-based one. If you find yourself comparing builds more than once a week, graduate to the desktop app. The web version handles 95% of use cases.

Summary: A damage calculator takes 10 minutes to set up and 2 minutes per comparison. Input stats, sets, and protocores. Compare sustained DPS, not burst. Remember 80-85% efficiency factor. Start with the web-based calculator. For most players, the optimal build is 5-15% better than their current one — a small tweak, not a full rebuild.
