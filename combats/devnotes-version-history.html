DEV NOTES — VERSION HISTORY

Version History & Meta Analysis

Every patch changes the game. New characters, buffs, nerfs, and system changes shift the meta. Understanding the history of these changes helps you predict the future.

ENTRY_#DEV_012026.07.02VERSION

Why Bother? Gacha games evolve. The meta you play today is different from last patch and will be different next patch. Understanding how the devs think (what they buff, what they nerf, what they introduce) lets you invest resources in characters and memories that will stay relevant, not ones that will be powercrept next patch.

Launch to Present — Major Milestones

Version 1.0 (Launch): Four operatives: Xavier (Light Seeker), Sylus (Dark Champion), Zayne (Frostguard), Rafayel (Inferno Blaze). Abyss 12 floors. Basic protocore system. Standard banner + first limited banner. Meta at launch: Xavier dominated Abyss due to Light element's wide coverage. Sylus was considered weak (execute mechanic was undertuned). Zayne was mandatory for floor 8 survival. Version 1.1 (First Major Patch): Sylus execute rework — damage threshold increased from 20% to 30%. First limited event with SSR memory shard system. Abyss floor 13-15 teased. Meta shift: Sylus became competitive. Hypercarry Xavier teams remained king. Version 1.2 (Summer): Summer event with limited outfits. Co-op boss mode introduced. Rafayel burn DoT rework — tick interval reduced. Meta shift: Rafayel became the top AoE operative. Co-op meta established (Zayne mandatory, Sylus for execute, Xavier for single target). Version 1.3 (Anniversary): Free SSR selector. Double top-up reset. Limited SSR (new character or variant). Protocore substat lock system teased. Meta shift: Anniversary typically resets the meta — new character introduces new team compositions.

Balance Change Patterns

The devs follow consistent balance patterns: Pattern 1 — Release weak, buff later: New characters are often released slightly undertuned. After 4-6 weeks, they receive a buff. This makes early adopters feel rewarded while giving the devs data to calibrate. Pattern 2 — Powercreep through systems, not numbers: New memories and protocores powercreep old ones, not direct character nerfs. If a character is too strong, the devs introduce content that counters them (e.g., enemies that resist their element) rather than nerfing the character directly. Pattern 3 — Anniversary shakeup: Every anniversary introduces a new system (SSR selector, substat lock, new memory tier) that significantly shifts the meta. Save resources for anniversary. Pattern 4 — Event character exclusivity: Limited characters return approximately once every 6 months on a rerun banner. They do not enter the standard pool. If you miss a limited character, you wait 6 months minimum.

Predicting Future Changes

Based on patterns: (1) New operatives may release with undertuned kits and receive buffs post-launch. Do not judge a new character in the first 2 weeks. (2) The next system change will likely involve protocore substat locking or rerolling (devs teased this). Save protocore dust for this feature. (3) Co-op content will expand — building support sets (Zayne HP builds) will become more valuable over time. (4) Element advantage content will increase — level up all 4 elements, not just your favorite. Investment advice: Xavier is the safest long-term investment (Light is never resisted). Zayne is the second safest (sustain is always valuable). Sylus and Rafayel are more specialized but become essential in their specific content types.

Pro Tip: Read every patch note in full. The "bug fixes" section is where the most important changes hide. A "bug fix" that changes skill behavior is actually a balance change in disguise. The devs rarely list balance changes directly — they bury them in bug fixes. Cross-reference community tests after every patch to find undocumented changes.

Community Meta Consensus Timeline

Launch — "Xavier is the only DPS you need." 1.1 — "Sylus execute is viable now." 1.2 — "Rafayel burn is broken in AoE." Current — "Full team of all 4 at 60 is the endgame target." The community meta always lags behind the actual meta by 2-4 weeks. Guide sites (including this one) capture the meta after it has stabilized. If you want to be ahead of the meta, test patch changes yourself on day 1 and form your own opinions. By the time the community agrees on the meta, the devs are already planning the next shakeup.

Summary: Game evolves through patches. New characters release undertuned then buffed. Powercreep happens through new memories/systems, not direct nerfs. Anniversary is the biggest meta shift. Read patch notes — especially bug fixes. Community meta lags 2-4 weeks behind reality. Xavier is the safest long-term investment. Save resources for anniversary systems.
