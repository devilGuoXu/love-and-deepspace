Co-op & Multiplayer Guide

Fighting alongside other hunters is different from solo play. Master the coordination.

ENTRY_#0312026.06.29COOP

Co-op Modes: Boss raids (2-3 players), Faction battles (3v3), and special event co-op. Each mode requires different team coordination. Voice chat is available but pings work well enough.

Written by Kaelen — Deepspace Hunter Station Team

Role Assignment

DPS: Your job is damage. Focus on the main target. Don't chase adds unless specifically asked. Support: Your job is keeping the team alive. Zayne is ideal. Watch everyone's HP, not just your own. Breaker: Your job is breaking shields and staggering bosses. Sylus excels here. Flex: Fill whatever role is missing. Xavier and Rafayel are good flex picks.

Co-op Etiquette

Ping Before Engaging: Use the ping system to signal targets. Don't Stack: Spread out 5+ meters 閳?AoE damage kills groups. Share Aggro: If you have aggro, kite away from the team. If you don't have aggro, position behind the boss. Save Ults: Coordinate ultimate usage. Don't overlap 閳?chain them for maximum uptime.

Communication

Use quick-chat pings for: "Need help" (when targeted), "Focus this" (on a high-priority target), "Watch out" (for incoming AoE), and "Ready" (ults available). The ping system is sufficient for all content except high-level Abyss 閳?use voice for that.

The Bottom Line: Co-op is about coordination, not individual skill. Know your role before queueing. Ping targets. Don't stack. Share aggro. A coordinated team of 3 with communication will outperform 3 solo experts who don't talk. Be the teammate you want to play with.

Co-op Mode Fundamentals

Co-op mode in Love and Deepspace pairs you with another player to take on enhanced bosses and Wanderer waves. Communication is limited to ping and emote systems, so success depends on reading your partner\'s movements and adapting your playstyle accordingly. Each player controls one operative, and the two operatives share a combined health pool and revive count. This shared resource system means that one player\'s mistakes affect both players, making defensive play and dodge discipline more important in co-op than in solo content.

Recommended Approach for Co-op

When entering co-op for the first time, bring a self-sufficient operative that does not require specific team support. Characters with self-healing, shields, or cleanse mechanics are particularly strong in co-op because they reduce pressure on the shared health pool. Pay attention to your partner\'s position and avoid abilities that knock enemies away from them. The most common co-op failure is not lack of damage but lack of coordination. Focus on survival and consistent damage output rather than aggressive plays until you understand your partner\'s playstyle.

Co-op Communication Protocol

Effective communication in co-op content significantly improves clear rates. Establish a target priority at the start of each run ? designate which elite enemy or boss mechanic to focus first. Use quick ping markers to indicate your target when playing with random teammates. The standard co-op convention is to mark the boss with a red ping and add enemies with yellow pings. Learning the ping hotkey and using it within the first 3 seconds of each wave sets the team's focus and prevents split damage that extends clear times unnecessarily.

Co-op Role Specialization

Specializing in one co-op role improves your performance more than generalist practice. The tank role requires memorizing boss attack patterns and maintaining aggro through consistent damage output. The support role requires tracking all teammates' HP bars and shield durations simultaneously. The DPS role requires identifying vulnerability windows and executing burst rotations within those windows. Choose one role to master in co-op content and practice it exclusively for 10-15 runs before switching roles. Role specialization reduces your reaction time for role-specific tasks by approximately 200 milliseconds through muscle memory development.

Co-op communication extends beyond pings to include pre-fight planning. Before starting the match, use the quick chat to designate who focuses which target if there are multiple enemies. The standard co-op convention is: the team leader (first player to join) calls targets. If you are not the team leader, follow the leader's target calls even if you believe a different target is optimal. Split focus is the most common cause of co-op wipes.

Co-op matchmaking prioritizes similar power levels. Players significantly below recommended power are deprioritized. Raising power by 2,000-3,000 points typically resolves wait times over 2 minutes.
