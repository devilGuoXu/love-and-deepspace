Counter-Picking Basics

Counter-picking is the single most important skill in Hunter Contest. When you see the opponent's team, you have ~15 seconds to build your response. Read the opponent's team: Identify their main DPS (highest power score), their support (healer/buffer), and their flex slot. Counter the DPS first: Bring an operative with element advantage against their main DPS. Check the stellactrum: If the opponent team has a specific color composition, match it or exploit the weakness. Kill the support first: In most compositions, the support (healer or buffer) is the linchpin. Kill them first and the team collapses. General rules: Element advantage > raw power. A correctly counter-picked team with 10% less power will beat the wrong team every time.

AI Exploitation Tactics

The AI in Hunter Contest has predictable patterns that you can exploit. AI targets the lowest HP: The AI will focus-fire on whichever of your operatives has the lowest current HP. Use this by keeping a tanky operative slightly lower to bait AI aggro. AI does not dodge well: The AI has a delayed reaction to telegraphed attacks. Use slow, hard-hitting skills that the AI will not dodge effectively. AI prioritizes Ardent Oath: The AI will almost always use Ardent Oath immediately when available. Time your defensive skills (shields, dodges) around the 80-90 energy mark. AI is bad at switching targets: Once the AI locks onto a target, it commits. Use this to pull the AI into unfavorable positions with your tank.

AI Pattern Detail: The AI uses a priority queue for its actions: (1) Ardent Oath if available, (2) Skill with highest damage multiplier, (3) Basic attack. This means you can predict exactly what the AI will do next if you track their energy and cooldowns.

Manual Targeting Priority

If you play manually (not auto), your targeting priority should be: 1. Healer/Support: Kill the healer first. Without healing, the enemy team crumbles. 2. Main DPS: After the support is dead, focus the main DPS. 3. Flex/Tank: Kill last. The tank does low damage and killing it first wastes your burst. Target swap: If the enemy switches to manual control of a specific character, focus that character (manual control means they are the threat). Element advantage: Always check which of your operatives has element advantage against which enemy. Focus-fire with the right element. Burst windows: Save your skills for when the enemy is grouped or when your Ardent Oath is ready. Wasting skills on spread-out targets is the most common manual play mistake.

Attack vs Defense Building

Your team building changes depending on whether you are on attack or defense. Attack team: Build for burst damage. The goal is to kill one enemy quickly and create a 3v2 advantage. Use high-damage single-target skills. Defense team: Build for sustain and AI confusion. The goal is to survive the first burst and let the AI make a mistake. Use healers, shields, and AoE skills. Stat priority difference: Attack favors CRIT and ATK. Defense favors HP, DEF, and Energy Regen. Memory sets: Attack teams use 4pc damage sets. Defense teams use 4pc sustain or mixed sets. Optimal split: Run 2 burst DPS + 1 sustain on attack. Run 1 DPS + 2 sustain on defense.

Effective Team Compositions

Standard Composition: 1 DPS + 1 Support + 1 Flex. The most balanced composition. DPS carries damage, support keeps the team alive, flex fills gaps. Double DPS Composition: 2 DPS + 1 Support. High risk, high reward. If the support dies, the team collapses. Use only when you are confident in out-damaging the opponent. Tank + DPS + Support: The "siege" composition. Slow but very hard to kill. Good against burst teams. Mono-Element: All three operatives share an element. Strong against the counter-element, weak against everything else. Situational but devastating in the right matchup. Your composition should: Cover at least 2 elements (for flexibility), have at least 1 source of healing, and have 1 burst damage dealer.

Meta Matchup Chart

Xavier teams: Weak to Zayne (element advantage). Xavier's high single-target damage is negated by shields. Sylus teams: Weak to Rafayel. Sylus's sustain is countered by Rafayel's DoT pressure. Zayne teams: Weak to Xavier. Zayne's slow, telegraphed skills are easily dodged by Xavier. Rafayel teams: Weak to Sylus. Rafayel's burst is survived by Sylus's high HP pool. General elements: Fire > Ice > Lightning > Fire. Use this triangle when building teams. A single element advantage is worth approximately 15% team power. Two element advantages against the same opponent team is almost an auto-win.

Tip: When facing an unknown opponent (no scouting), bring a balanced team with 2 elements and at least 1 healer. A defensive composition survives the first engagement and gives you time to read the opponent's team. After you see their composition, you can adjust your manual targeting accordingly.

Summary: Counter-pick in the strategy screen (70% of wins come from this). Kill the support first. AI targets lowest HP — use this to bait aggro. Attack = burst damage, Defense = sustain. Build 2 elements minimum. Use the element triangle (Fire > Ice > Lightning > Fire) for advantage. Element advantage = ~15% team power.
