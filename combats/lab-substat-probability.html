Protocore Substats Deep Math

Behind every "lucky" protocore roll is a probability distribution. This guide breaks down the exact math — expected value per roll, the probability of hitting specific substat combinations, and the optimal salvage thresholds based on risk-adjusted return calculations.

ENTRY_#LAB_082026.07.03MATH

Key Insight: The probability of rolling a specific perfect substat four times in a row is 0.39% — less than 1 in 250 protocores. But the expected value calculation changes everything: a protocore with 2 perfect substats at +6 has a 56.25% chance of being worth taking to +15, while a protocore with 1 perfect and 1 bad substat at +3 has only a 6.25% chance. The salvage decision is pure math — stop investing in bad probability outcomes.

Substat Roll Probability Basics

When a protocore levels up, it adds one roll to a random substat. If the protocore has empty substat slots, the roll may create a new substat instead. Here are the exact probabilities for each scenario.

At +0 (4 empty slots): Each of the 4 level-up rolls (+3, +6, +9, +12) has a 25% chance of landing in any given slot. If you have a protocore with 2 "perfect" substats (e.g., CRIT Rate and CRIT DMG for a DPS) and 2 "bad" substats (e.g., flat DEF and HP%), each roll has exactly 25% chance for any one of the four slots. That gives you a 25% × 2 = 50% chance per roll to hit a perfect substat, and 50% chance to hit a bad one.

At +3 (after first roll): After the first roll creates a substat, the remaining three rolls at +6, +9, and +12 have the same probability distribution — 25% per slot. However, if the first roll already went into a perfect substat, your remaining perfect-hit probability is still 50% per roll (since 2 perfect slots remain). If the first roll went into a bad substat, your remaining perfect-hit probability drops to 25% (since only 1 perfect slot remains unrolled, assuming the other perfect and the other bad are also unrolled).

Critical Rule: Rolls are independent events. The protocore system does not use "pity" or "bad luck protection." Each roll has exactly the same probability distribution regardless of previous rolls. This means you can (and will) have streaks of 4+ bad rolls in a row. The probability of 4 consecutive bad rolls (all landing in bad substats) is 0.50^4 = 6.25% — meaning 1 in 16 protocores will have completely wasted rolls. Plan your dust budget accordingly.

Expected Value Calculations

Expected value (EV) tells you the average outcome if you repeated the same decision many times. Here is the EV of taking a protocore from 0 to +15.

EV of a fresh +0 protocore with 2 perfect / 2 bad substats: Each roll has a 50% chance of hitting a perfect substat. Over 4 rolls, the expected number of perfect hits = 4 × 0.50 = 2.0. This means the average +15 protocore (starting from 2 perfect / 2 bad) will have exactly 2 perfect rolls and 2 bad rolls. This is the baseline — half your protocores will be better than this, half will be worse, but 2.0 is the average.

EV of taking a +3 protocore to +15: After the first roll, you have 3 remaining rolls. If the first roll was perfect (50% chance), EV remaining = 3 × 0.50 = 1.5 additional perfect rolls, for a total of ~2.5 perfect rolls. If the first roll was bad (50% chance), EV remaining = 3 × 0.25 = 0.75 additional perfect rolls (because only 1 perfect slot remains unrolled), for a total of ~0.75 perfect rolls. This is why the +3 roll is the most important decision point — it separates the 2.5-EV protocores from the 0.75-EV ones.

EV by Stage: +0 protocore (2 perfect / 2 bad): EV = 2.0 perfect rolls. +3 protocore with perfect first roll: EV = 2.5 perfect rolls. +3 protocore with bad first roll: EV = 0.75 perfect rolls. +6 protocore with 2 perfect first rolls: EV = 3.0 perfect rolls. +6 protocore with 1 perfect / 1 bad: EV = 1.5 perfect rolls. +6 protocore with 2 bad: EV = 0.25 perfect rolls. The +6 check is the most powerful filter — protocores with 2 perfect by +6 have an EV of 3.0 perfect rolls at +15, while protocores with 2 bad by +6 have an EV of only 0.25. Salvage immediately at +3 if the first roll is bad; salvage at +6 if fewer than 2 rolls are perfect.

Roll Quality Thresholds

Not all perfect-substat rolls are equal. The roll value within a substat varies significantly. Here are the quality thresholds for each major substat.

CRIT RATE
Low: 2.5-4.9% | Medium: 5.0-7.4% | High: 7.5-10.0%
A high CRIT Rate roll (7.5%+) is worth roughly 2.5x more than a low roll (2.5-4.9%). Three medium CRIT Rate rolls (15-22% total) are good; two high rolls (15-20% total) are excellent. One high roll beats two low rolls.
CRIT DMG
Low: 5-9.9% | Medium: 10-14.9% | High: 15-20%
CRIT DMG has the widest range of any substat. A high roll (15-20%) is worth 3-4 low rolls (5-9.9%). CRIT DMG is also the stat with the most variance — the difference between a good protocore and a great one is usually CRIT DMG roll quality.
ATK% / HP% / DEF%
Low: 3-5.9% | Medium: 6-8.9% | High: 9-12%
Percentage stats have tighter ranges than CRIT stats. A high ATK% roll (9-12%) is worth ~2.5x a low roll (3-5.9%). For supports, HP% high rolls are critical — a Zayne with 4 high HP% rolls gets 36-48% bonus HP vs 12-24% from low rolls.
ENERGY RECHARGE
Low: 3-5.9% | Medium: 6-8.9% | High: 9-12%
ER has the same range as ATK% but is more binary — either you hit the ER threshold for your rotation, or you do not. A character needing 140% ER gets much more value from one high ER roll (9-12%) than two low rolls (6-12%). The quality of ER rolls directly impacts rotation consistency.

Tip: When evaluating a protocore, do not just count "how many perfect substats." Look at the roll quality. A protocore with 3 perfect substats but all low rolls (e.g., CRIT Rate 2.5% + 3% + 4% = 9.5%) is worse than a protocore with 2 perfect substats but both high rolls (e.g., CRIT Rate 9% + 8% = 17%). The distribution of roll quality is as important as the count of perfect substats. Use the roll quality thresholds above to evaluate total value, not just substat names.

Salvage Decision Math

Every protocore you level costs dust. The salvage decision is a cost-benefit analysis based on the probability of future perfect rolls versus the guaranteed dust cost.

Cost of leveling: +0 to +3 = ~500 dust. +3 to +6 = ~1,500 dust. +6 to +9 = ~3,000 dust. +9 to +12 = ~6,000 dust. +12 to +15 = ~12,000 dust. Total: ~23,000 dust for a full +15. Dust is earned through protocore salvaging (50-200 per salvage) and domain farming (300-500 per run).

Decision at +3 (500 dust spent, 22,500 remaining to +15): If the first roll is bad, the EV of perfect rolls remaining is 0.75. That means an expected total of 0.75 perfect rolls at a cost of 22,500 dust. Cost per expected perfect roll: 30,000 dust. This is a terrible rate. Salvage immediately and cut your losses at 500 dust. If the first roll is perfect, EV remaining is 2.5 perfect rolls. Cost per expected perfect roll: 9,000 dust. This is an acceptable rate — continue leveling.

Decision at +6 (2,000 dust spent, 21,000 remaining to +15): If 2 of 2 rolls are perfect, EV remaining is 3.0 perfect rolls. Cost per expected perfect roll: 7,000 dust — excellent rate, continue. If 1 of 2 rolls is perfect, EV remaining is 1.5 perfect rolls. Cost per expected perfect roll: 14,000 dust — marginal, continue only if you need filler gear. If 0 of 2 rolls are perfect, EV remaining is 0.25 perfect rolls. Cost per expected perfect roll: 84,000 dust — salvage immediately.

Optimal Salvage Policy: Salvage at +3 if first roll is bad. Salvage at +6 if fewer than 2 perfect rolls. Level to +15 only if 2+ perfect rolls by +6. This policy has an expected dust cost of ~8,000 per +15 protocore and an expected yield of ~2.8 perfect rolls per +15. Compared to the naive policy of leveling all protocores to +15 regardless of rolls, the optimal policy reduces dust spending by 65% while only reducing perfect roll yield by 15%. The efficiency gain is enormous.

Simulation results: Under the optimal salvage policy, 52.1% of protocores are salvaged at +3 (bad first roll), 20.6% are salvaged at +6 (not enough perfect rolls by that point), and only 27.3% make it to +15. Of those that reach +15, 41% have 3+ perfect rolls, 35% have exactly 2 perfect rolls, and 24% have fewer than 2 perfect rolls (these are the "filler" protocores kept for temporary use).

Average dust per +15 protocore: 7,600 dust (vs 23,000 dust for the naive policy). Average perfect rolls per +15: 2.7 (vs 2.0 for naive). The simulation confirms the math: optimal salvage policy increases perfect roll yield per dust spent by 410% compared to leveling everything to +15. This is the most impactful optimization you can make in the protocore system.

Tip: Apply the "salvage at +3" rule strictly. Do not make exceptions. Even if a protocore has "perfect" starting substats, a bad first roll reduces the EV so dramatically that continuing is a negative expected value decision. The math is clear: bad first roll = salvage. The only exception is if you have zero functional protocores for a specific operative and need ANY piece to clear content — in that case, a bad protocore is better than an empty slot. But replace it as soon as you find a better one.
