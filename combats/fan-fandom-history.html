The Complete History of Love and Deepspace Fandom

From a whisper to a roar — tracing the growth, culture, and community of one of mobile gaming's most passionate fandoms across regions, platforms, and years.

FAN_082026.07.07FANDOM

Key Insight: The Love and Deepspace fandom did not emerge fully formed — it was built piece by piece by thousands of creators, commenters, and community organizers across multiple language communities and platforms. Understanding this history reveals not just where the fandom has been, but where it is going. At roughly 24 months since global launch, the fandom has reached a critical mass that transforms it from a niche interest into a sustained creative ecosystem.

Prelude: Before Launch

Before Love and Deepspace was a global phenomenon, it was a Chinese-language title that generated modest pre-registration interest. Papergames, known previously for the Nikki series (Love Nikki, Shining Nikki), announced their foray into the 3D otome/action RPG hybrid space with significant skepticism from the existing otome game community. The otome gaming space was dominated by well-established titles like Mr. Love: Queen's Choice (also by Papergames), Tears of Themis (miHoYo), and a host of Chinese and Japanese mobile otome games. A new entrant would need to differentiate itself dramatically.

Pre-launch marketing focused on three differentiators: fully 3D characters with high-fidelity real-time rendering, action-oriented combat sequences that went beyond typical visual novel mechanics, and a darker sci-fi narrative tone distinct from the lighter romantic comedy approach of many competitors. The early trailers released in late 2023 generated cautious optimism. The character designs — Xavier with his ethereal light aesthetic, Zayne with his medical precision, Rafayel with his artistic flamboyance, and later Sylus with his commanding dark presence — were immediately recognizable as high-quality 3D modeling work that exceeded industry standards for mobile games.

The Chinese pre-registration campaign launched in December 2023, accumulating approximately 2 million pre-registrations. Global pre-registration followed shortly after. The official Discord server opened its doors in January 2024, staffed by a small team of moderators who would become the first community leaders. At this stage, the server had fewer than 5,000 members, and the primary conversation was speculation: Would the combat system be fun? Would the romance be satisfying? Would the gacha be generous or punishing? These three questions would define the fandom's relationship with the game for the next two years.

Fan art existed before launch — primarily speculative character illustrations based on promotional materials. The earliest fan works were on Pixiv and Twitter, with artists experimenting with their interpretations of Xavier and Zayne (Rafayel and Sylus had less promotional material at this stage). The fandom was tiny but earnest, a group of approximately a few hundred creators and commenters who believed the game might be something special.

Phase 1: Global Launch (January — March 2024)

Love and Deepspace launched globally on January 18, 2024, and the response exceeded all reasonable expectations. The game topped download charts in multiple regions simultaneously. Within the first week, the official Discord server grew from 5,000 to over 50,000 members. Twitter exploded with screenshots, reactions, and early fan art. The fandom was born not through careful cultivation but through explosive organic growth.

The initial wave of discussion centered on several key topics. First, the gacha system. Players compared the rate of SSR memory card acquisition to other games in the genre. The 1.5% base SSR rate (with the 50/50 system familiar to Genshin Impact players) was analyzed, debated, and eventually accepted as industry-standard. Second, the combat system generated lively debate between players who loved the action-RPG elements and those who preferred the traditional visual novel experience. Third, and most passionately, character discussions began in earnest: Xavier fans vs. Zayne fans vs. Rafayel fans, each camp defending their preferred operative's merits in threads that stretched for hundreds of messages.

The first major community event occurred approximately two weeks after launch: the "First SSR" sharing trend on Twitter and Reddit. Players posted screenshots of their first SSR memory card pulls, creating a wave of celebratory posts that served as organic marketing for the game. This trend established a community norm of sharing milestones — a practice that would continue with every subsequent banner and event.

February 2024 saw the first major banner — the "Veiled Whisper" event featuring Xavier and a new SSR memory card. The discussion around this banner established patterns that would persist: banner value analysis, pull planning, FOMO (fear of missing out) management, and the eternal debate between pulling for favorites vs. pulling for meta. Content creators publishing guides on whether to pull became the first wave of Love and Deepspace theorycrafters, a role that would grow in sophistication over time.

The first instance of organized fandom creativity appeared in February as well. A small group of fan fiction writers on AO3 (Archive of Our Own) published their first works. The initial stories were tentative — short character studies and missing scenes from the main story. By March, the AO3 tag for Love and Deepspace had grown to over 200 works. Simultaneously, the first fan artists began posting regularly, developing recognizable styles and building audiences within the community.

March 2024 brought the game's first major patch, introducing quality-of-life improvements and the initial Limited-Time Bounty event. The community response was generally positive, and the game's Reddit community crossed 100,000 subscribers. The first seeds of the competitive element appeared — players comparing Abyssal floor clear times and sharing technique advice. The first "what's your Abyssal team" threads appeared, laying groundwork for the team-building meta discourse that would become a pillar of the community.

By the end of Q1 2024, the Love and Deepspace fandom had established its foundational structure: a rapidly growing Reddit community focused on news and discussion, a bustling Discord server for real-time conversation and content sharing, a thriving art community on Twitter and Pixiv, and the beginnings of a fan fiction ecosystem on AO3. The fandom was raw, unorganized, and passionate — a classic early-stage community finding its feet.

Phase 2: Community Formation (April — June 2024)

The second quarter of 2024 was when the Love and Deepspace fandom matured from a collection of excited players into a structured community with norms, leaders, and traditions. Several factors drove this transformation. The game's first major content update introduced the "Deepspace Trial" tower mode, giving the theorycrafting community a concrete problem to solve. Content creators who had been making general guides now had specific floors to analyze, optimal team compositions to calculate, and rotation strategies to document.

April 2024 saw the first fan-organized event: a community screenshot contest on the official Discord. The theme was "Best Photo Studio Composition," and the contest drew over 500 entries. This event established the Photo Studio functionality as a major creative outlet for the community — a trend that would only grow as more outfits, poses, and locations were added to the game. The winning entries were shared across social media, generating positive buzz and encouraging more players to explore the game's photography features.

The first significant fandom controversy also emerged in April 2024. A debate over power creep and character balance erupted when certain memory card combinations were discovered to produce substantially higher damage output than others. The discussion split the community into two camps: those who advocated for following the meta (pulling for meta-defining memory cards) and those who advocated for pulling based on character preference regardless of power level. This tension — favorites vs. meta — would become a defining feature of fandom discourse, resurfacing with every new banner and balance patch. The controversy was ultimately productive: it led to the creation of the first comprehensive damage calculation frameworks, which theorycrafters published as Google Sheets documents. These spreadsheets were the ancestors of the sophisticated tools that would appear later.

May 2024 brought two significant developments. First, the game's first major update added new story chapters and a new game mode. Second, and perhaps more importantly for the fandom, the first major shipping wars broke out. The "Hunter/Operative" relationship dynamic — where the protagonist (Hunter) has romantic tension with all four operatives — inevitably led to factionalism. Xavier fans, Zayne fans, Rafayel fans, and Sylus fans (Sylus had been released by this point) formed distinct subcommunities with their own Discords, Twitter circles, and creative traditions. The shipping discourse was occasionally heated, but it also drove enormous amounts of fan content. Each ship community developed its own tropes, inside jokes, and shared interpretations of canon material.

June 2024 marked the fandom's first major anniversary event (the game's six-month anniversary). Papergames celebrated with login rewards and a limited-time event. The fandom celebrated with a massive outpouring of content: celebratory fan art, retrospective essays, and community-organized appreciation threads. A group of fans created a "Fandom Yearbook" — a PDF compilation of notable community creations from the first six months. This grassroots project, organized entirely by volunteers, demonstrated the community's capacity for self-organization and its genuine affection for the game beyond mere gameplay.

By the end of Q2 2024, the fandom had developed recognizable infrastructure. Multiple Discord servers existed for different purposes: the official server for general discussion, server-specific communities for each operative, a theorycrafting-focused server for number crunchers, and a fan creation server for artists and writers. The Reddit community had developed a robust wiki system. Content creators had established posting schedules and recognizable brands. The fandom was no longer a crowd — it was a community with structure.

Phase 3: Creative Explosion (July — December 2024)

The second half of 2024 was the golden age of Love and Deepspace fan creation. Several factors aligned to produce an unprecedented wave of creative output. The game's roster was complete with all four operatives, each with multiple SSR memory cards providing rich source material. The Photo Studio features had expanded significantly, giving artists reference material and cosplayers outfit inspiration. And the community had reached a critical mass where creators received meaningful engagement, encouraging sustained output.

July 2024 saw the first Love and Deepspace fan convention — an informal gathering organized by fans in Tokyo. Approximately 200 attendees met in person for a day of cosplay, art trading, and discussion. The event was small by conventional standards but enormously significant symbolically: it demonstrated that the fandom had moved beyond purely digital interaction. Similar meetups followed in Los Angeles (August), London (September), and Shanghai (October). The global nature of the fandom was becoming apparent — Love and Deepspace had genuine cross-regional appeal, with active communities in East Asia, Southeast Asia, North America, Europe, and Latin America.

August 2024 brought the "Summer Splash" event, which introduced swimsuit outfits for all operatives. The event generated an extraordinary volume of fan art — Twitter recorded over 50,000 posts with relevant hashtags within the first week. The quality range was staggering: from simple sketches to fully rendered digital paintings that rivaled official promotional art. Several pieces went viral beyond the fandom, bringing new players to the game. This event established a pattern: seasonal events with new outfits would consistently produce the highest spikes in fan activity.

September 2024 saw the first major fan fiction event: "LaDS Big Bang," a collaborative writing challenge where authors and artists paired up to produce illustrated long-form stories. The event, organized entirely by volunteers, produced 45 completed works, each exceeding 10,000 words with accompanying illustrations. The Big Bang format was borrowed from other fandoms but adapted to Love and Deepspace's specific dynamics. It established a tradition of large-scale collaborative events that would continue with subsequent Big Bangs and Mini Bangs.

October 2024 through December 2024 was a period of sustained creative output. Halloween content, winter holiday content, and the game's one-year anniversary (for the Chinese server, which had launched earlier) created a near-constant stream of new material for fans to engage with. The AO3 tag for Love and Deepspace crossed 5,000 works. Pixiv featured art in the tens of thousands. YouTube content creators who specialized in Love and Deepspace — guide makers, lore analysts, reaction channels — were establishing sustainable audiences. The fandom had become a self-sustaining creative economy.

A notable development during this period was the emergence of "fandom historians" — community members who documented the fandom's own development. These individuals maintained timelines of significant events, archived notable fan works, and wrote analysis pieces about community trends. Their work, though often underappreciated in real-time, would become invaluable records of the fandom's early years.

Phase 4: Maturity & Institutionalization (January — June 2025)

By 2025, the Love and Deepspace fandom entered what can be described as its institutional phase. The explosive growth of 2024 had subsided, replaced by steady, sustainable community development. The fandom now had established norms, recognized leaders, recurring events, and a clear sense of its own identity. This period was less about growth in raw numbers and more about deepening the quality of community engagement.

January 2025 saw the first "Fandom Awards" — a community-organized event recognizing outstanding fan works across categories including Best Fan Art, Best Fan Fiction, Best Cosplay, Best Theorycrafting Guide, and Best Community Contributor. The awards were conducted via community nomination and voting, with winners receiving digital badges and recognition on community platforms. The event drew over 2,000 participants and established an annual tradition. The awards served an important function: they provided recognition for creators who had been contributing without formal acknowledgment, and they helped new community members discover high-quality existing content.

February through April 2025 saw significant developments in the theorycrafting and meta-analysis side of the community. As more memory cards were released and more game modes introduced, the complexity of optimal play increased dramatically. Community theorycrafters responded by developing increasingly sophisticated tools: damage calculators, team builders, banner value simulators, and resource planners. These tools, shared freely within the community, became essential resources for serious players. The relationship between theorycrafters and the broader community crystallized: theorycrafters provided analysis, the community debated and stress-tested it, and consensus emerged through iterative discussion.

May 2025 marked the game's full one-year anniversary for the global server (counting from launch). Papergames ran a substantial in-game celebration with generous rewards, and the fandom responded with an even larger outpouring of content than the six-month mark. The anniversary saw multiple community-organized events running simultaneously: an art contest with over 1,000 entries, a fan fiction marathon producing 60+ new works in a single week, a cosplay showcase featuring 100+ participants, and a community podcast special where prominent members reflected on the first year. The anniversary demonstrated the fandom's remarkable capacity for coordinated action.

June 2025 brought a significant challenge: the first major content drought. Between major updates, players had less new material to discuss, and community activity dipped noticeably. This period tested the fandom's resilience. Some members drifted away, but the core community adapted. Fan events filled the gap — community members organized re-watch parties of memory card stories, re-read events of fan fiction classics, and "rewrite the story" challenges that encouraged creative engagement with existing material. The content drought ultimately strengthened the community by forcing it to generate its own entertainment rather than relying solely on official releases.

By the end of Q2 2025, the Love and Deepspace fandom had achieved institutional stability. Annual events were established. Community roles were well-defined. Communication channels were optimized. The fandom was no longer a fragile early-stage community but a mature ecosystem capable of sustaining itself through both content-rich and content-lean periods.

Phase 5: The Present Era (July 2025 — Present)

The current era of the Love and Deepspace fandom is characterized by sophisticated community infrastructure, a rich creative tradition, and the challenges that come with maturity. The fandom now operates across multiple platforms with specialized subcommunities, each with its own culture and norms.

The creative output has diversified dramatically. Beyond the established categories of art and fiction, the fandom now produces: video essays analyzing narrative themes, musical compositions inspired by character motifs, data visualizations tracking community trends, translation projects bridging language communities, and even indie game demos set in the Love and Deepspace universe. The creative ambition has scaled from individual works to collaborative projects involving dozens of contributors.

Community governance has also evolved. Major Discord servers have developed formal moderation structures with clear codes of conduct, dispute resolution processes, and content policies. The days of unmoderated chaos are largely behind the community. While this professionalism has made the community more welcoming to new members, some veteran members nostalgia for the "wild west" period of early 2024. This tension between institutionalization and spontaneity is a natural feature of maturing fandoms.

The relationship between the fandom and Papergames has also evolved. Early on, the relationship was primarily reactive — Papergames released content, and fans responded. By the present era, the relationship has become more dialogic. Papergames has shown awareness of community sentiment in their design decisions, and community-created content occasionally influences official materials. Fan translations of Chinese-language content help bridge the gap between the original and global communities. The fandom has moved from being a passive audience to an active participant in the game's cultural ecosystem.

Current challenges facing the fandom include managing the scale of content creation (the volume of new fan works makes discovery difficult), maintaining inclusivity across language and cultural barriers, and navigating the tension between different community visions (casual vs. competitive, lore-focused vs. gameplay-focused, shipping-centric vs. character-generalist). These are not problems to be solved but dynamics to be managed — they arise naturally from the fandom's size and diversity.

Regional Community Analysis

The Love and Deepspace fandom is genuinely global, but each region has developed distinct characteristics worthy of examination. Understanding these regional differences is essential for a complete picture of the fandom.

Chinese-speaking community (mainland China, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Southeast Asia). The largest and most established regional community. Chinese fans had access to the game earlier and thus established community norms before the global launch. The Chinese community operates primarily on Weibo, Bilibili, and QQ. Art trends in the Chinese community tend toward higher polish and more frequent updates. The Chinese theorycrafting community is extremely sophisticated, often producing analysis before global equivalents. The relationship between Chinese fans and Papergames is more direct, as the developer responds to Chinese community feedback more immediately.

Japanese-speaking community. Japan has a strong otome game tradition, and Love and Deepspace occupies a unique position in this market. Japanese fans bring expectations from the domestic otome genre — they value character consistency, voice acting quality, and romantic payoff. The Japanese community is highly organized on Twitter with systematic use of hashtags for content categorization. Japanese fan art has a distinct aesthetic tendency toward softer rendering and more emphasis on emotional expression. The Japanese community is also notably disciplined about spoiler etiquette.

English-speaking community (North America, Europe, Oceania, parts of Southeast Asia). The most linguistically diverse regional community, encompassing native English speakers and non-native speakers who use English as a lingua franca. The English community operates primarily on Reddit, Twitter, Discord, and Tumblr. English fan fiction is the largest by volume on AO3. The English community tends to be more focused on gameplay discussion and meta analysis relative to other regions. The shipping discourse in the English community is notably more explicit about relationship dynamics compared to East Asian communities.

Korean-speaking community. Korea has a passionate but smaller community relative to the above regions. Korean fans are active on Naver and Twitter. Korean fan art is highly regarded for its distinctive coloring techniques. The Korean community has been particularly influential in the cosplay space, producing some of the most technically accomplished costumes.

Latin American and other emerging communities. Spanish and Portuguese speaking communities have grown steadily, organizing primarily through dedicated Discord servers and Twitter circles. These communities face additional challenges of translation and time zone coordination but have produced notable creative works.

Fandom Culture & Traditions

Every fandom develops distinctive cultural practices, and Love and Deepspace is no exception. The following traditions have emerged organically and become defining features of community identity.

Memory Card Release Day. When a new SSR memory card is announced, the community enters a predictable cycle: teaser analysis (scrutinizing every pixel of promotional images), hype building (sharing expectations and hopes), release day frenzy (mass posting of pull results), and post-release discussion (character analysis, art appreciation, power evaluation). This cycle has become a ritual that structures community activity around banner releases.

Photo Studio Challenges. The in-game Photo Studio has spawned an entire subculture of virtual photography. Weekly themed challenges — organized by community members on Discord and Twitter — encourage creative use of outfits, poses, and locations. The best entries are shared widely, and particularly skilled virtual photographers have developed dedicated followings.

Fandom Inside Jokes. Shared humor is a powerful community bonding mechanism. Love and Deepspace has accumulated a rich lexicon of inside jokes: Xavier's notorious nap-taking, Zayne's clinical bedside manner that works better on patients than love interests, Rafayel's dramatic artistic temperament, Sylus's intimidating presence undermined by his obvious soft spot for the Hunter. Memes about these character traits circulate constantly, creating a shared language that reinforces community identity.

Cross-community Pollination. Love and Deepspace fans frequently create content that crosses over with other fandoms — Genshin Impact, Honkai Star Rail, Tears of Themis, and other gacha games. Crossover art imagining characters from different games interacting is a popular genre. These crossovers serve as cultural bridges, introducing Love and Deepspace to broader anime/gacha game fandom spaces.

New Member Welcoming. A notable positive feature of the community is the active welcoming of new members. Veteran fans regularly create "starter guides," answer beginner questions, and encourage new creators. This welcoming culture is frequently cited by community members as a key reason they stayed engaged with the fandom after discovering it.

Challenges & Growing Pains

No fandom history is complete without honest discussion of challenges. The Love and Deepspace community has faced and continues to navigate several significant issues.

Shipping Conflict. The most persistent source of intra-community tension is shipping. Fans who strongly prefer one operative over others occasionally engage in behavior that ranges from dismissive (ignoring content about other operatives) to hostile (denigrating other characters or their fans). The shipping wars, while generating creative output, have also created environments where some community members feel unwelcome. Community leaders have responded with moderation policies emphasizing respect for all characters and their fans.

Gatekeeping. As the fandom has grown, instances of gatekeeping have emerged — veteran fans dismissive of newer members, meta-focused players looking down on casual players, and purity policing around what qualifies as "valid" fan engagement. These behaviors, while not dominant, create friction. The community's best defense has been the active promotion of inclusivity by influential members and moderators.

Monetization Pressure. The gacha model creates inherent tension. Players who spend heavily (whales) and players who spend little or nothing (F2P) have fundamentally different experiences of the game. Discussions around banner value and pull priority can inadvertently create hierarchies of spending. The community has generally handled this well, with most discourse explicitly acknowledging that spending is a personal choice and not a measure of commitment.

Content Attribution. As the volume of fan works has grown, so have issues around content theft and misattribution. Art reposted without credit, fiction republished without permission, and cosplay photos used without consent are recurring problems. The community has developed norms around proper attribution, and most platforms have reporting mechanisms, but enforcement remains imperfect.

Language Barriers. Despite a growing translation ecosystem, the fandom remains partially fragmented by language. Chinese-language content that doesn't get translated misses global audience, and English-language discussions are inaccessible to non-English speakers. Community translators do heroic work, but the scale of content far exceeds translation capacity.

The Future of the Fandom

Predicting the trajectory of any fandom is speculative, but several trends suggest where the Love and Deepspace community may be heading.

Increasing Professionalization. As the fandom matures, the line between fan creator and professional creator is blurring. Fan artists are securing commercial illustration work. Fan fiction writers are publishing original novels. Community organizers are building resumes in community management. The fandom is functioning as an incubator for creative talent.

Generational Shift. The first wave of fans joined at launch and have been playing for over two years. A second wave joined later through word-of-mouth, social media discovery, or content updates. The dynamics between these waves — the "launch veterans" and the "post-launch recruits" — will shape community culture going forward.

Platform Evolution. Social media platforms are constantly changing, and the fandom's platform strategy will need to adapt. The decline of Twitter under new management has prompted some community migration to Bluesky and Threads. Discord remains stable, but its feature evolution affects how communities organize. The fandom's ability to adapt to platform changes will determine its long-term resilience.

Official Recognition. Papergames has shown increasing willingness to acknowledge fan creations. Official social media accounts occasionally share fan art. The line between official and fan content may continue to blur, creating new opportunities and new questions about ownership and creative freedom.

Sustainability. The fundamental question for any aging fandom is sustainability. Can the community maintain its creative output and engagement levels as the game itself ages? The answer depends on both Papergames' ability to keep the game engaging and the community's capacity for self-renewal. Based on the trajectory to date, the outlook is cautiously optimistic — the fandom has demonstrated remarkable resilience and creative self-sufficiency.

Note for New Readers: If you're new to the fandom and reading this history feels overwhelming, remember: every single person in this timeline started exactly where you are. The first fan artist who posted in January 2024 was nervous. The first Big Bang organizer in September 2024 had never run an event before. The community is built by people who decided to participate rather than just observe. Your contribution — whether it's a piece of art, a comment on someone's post, or just being present — adds to the story.

Summary: The Love and Deepspace fandom has evolved from a small group of pre-launch speculators into a mature, multi-platform, multilingual creative community spanning over two years of history. Key phases include explosive launch growth, community formation with structured subcommunities, a creative explosion in late 2024, institutionalization in early 2025, and the current era of sophisticated creative output and community governance. The fandom faces ongoing challenges including shipping conflict, gatekeeping, and monetization tension, but has demonstrated remarkable resilience and capacity for self-organization. As the community moves into its third year, its future depends on sustainable engagement, platform adaptation, and continued creative renewal.
