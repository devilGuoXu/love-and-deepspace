Why Banners Matter Again

For most of Love and Deepspace's first year, limited banners were a one-and-done proposition. If you missed Stellar Contract or Permafrost Covenant during their debut windows, those memories simply vanished from the gacha pool. That changed when Papergames introduced the rerun system: dedicated limited banner reruns that bring back past SSR memories for a second (or third) chance at acquisition. With the introduction of the Rerun Calendar and double-limited banner slots in version 2.0, the question is no longer "should I pull?" — it's "which past banner should I prioritize on its rerun?"

This matters more than ever because the game's power curve has shifted significantly since launch. Newer memories tend to have tighter stat distributions, better set synergies, and more impactful resonance chain bonuses. Some early limited SSRs that were considered must-pulls in 2025 have been quietly outclassed by standard-pool alternatives. Conversely, a handful of launch-era limited cards remain best-in-slot for specific teams even in the current meta.

The rerun system also introduces a new resource management challenge: gems spent on a rerun are gems not spent on a brand-new limited banner that might run simultaneously. Knowing which past memories justify that opportunity cost is critical for F2P and low-spending players who cannot afford to collect everything.

Tip: Papergames has confirmed that all limited SSRs will eventually receive at least one rerun. However, the interval between debut and rerun varies widely — some memories returned within 6 months, while others are still waiting after 18+ months. Patience is a virtue, but so is having a concrete savings plan.

Review Methodology

Each limited memory in this retrospective was evaluated across five dimensions, scored on a 1-10 scale, then assigned a composite tier and rerun verdict. The methodology is designed to reflect both current competitive relevance and future-proofing potential:

Meta Performance
How well does this memory perform in Abyssal Chaos, Orbital Tower, and high-difficulty event content at the current power level? Accounts for resonance chain requirements — a memory that only shines at R3+ is weighted lower than one that delivers at R0.
Team Fit
How many viable teams can this memory slot into? Does it enable an entire archetype (e.g., HP-sacrifice Sylus, support Zayne), or is it a replaceable DPS stick? Memories that unlock new playstyles score higher.
Stat Efficiency
How do the memory's base stats, stat growth, and set bonuses compare against the current ceiling? Considers whether the memory's stat distribution aligns with modern damage formulas.
Replacement Availability
Are there standard-pool or farmable alternatives that do the same job? A memory with no good replacement scores higher than one that can be substituted with a free SR.
Rerun Value
The overall recommendation: given its current meta standing, do you pull on the rerun, skip it, or pull only under specific conditions (e.g., you main that operative, you have enough gems for both this and the concurrent new banner).

Composite scores are translated into four verdict badges: TOP-TIER (still elite, pull on sight), DECENT (solid but not essential), POWERCREPT (outclassed by newer options), and LEGACY (niche use only, skip unless collecting).

Past Banner Reviews

Stellar Contract
Xavier
Lunar Radiance
Original: 2025.01.15
TOP-TIER

Stellar Contract remains Xavier's best-in-slot memory for virtually every game mode. The Lunar Radiance set bonus provides a multiplicative damage amp on crit that no other Xavier set has matched. The base SSR stat spread is nearly ideal — high ATK, balanced crit rate and crit damage — and the R1 resonance chain upgrade gives a 22% damage increase on active skill that keeps it competitive with brand-new memories released in mid-2026.

The reason this card has held up so well is its team-agnostic design. It doesn't require specific teammates or protocore setups to perform. Whether you run Xavier in a hypercarry composition, a dual-DPS setup, or a support-oriented team, Stellar Contract delivers consistent top-tier output. It has also aged gracefully because the Lunar Radiance set hasn't been powercrept — no newer set offers a comparable crit multiplier.

Verdict: Pull on rerun — still Xavier's best memory after 18 months. Prioritize if you main Xavier or lack a strong Lunar Radiance carrier.
Permafrost Covenant
Zayne
Glacial Ward
Original: 2025.02.20
TOP-TIER

Permafrost Covenant defined the Zayne support meta for over a year, and it still holds up as a premier support memory. The Glacial Ward set applies a party-wide damage reduction and a stacking frost damage multiplier that enables Zayne's best freeze comp teams. What makes this card irreplaceable is its unique mechanic: at R0, it already provides a 15% team-wide damage amp that no other memory in the standard or limited pool replicates.

Unlike Stellar Contract, Permafrost Covenant is somewhat team-dependent — it truly shines in freeze composition teams with a secondary ice applicator. But in that specific archetype, no replacement exists. The memory's stat efficiency is also excellent: high HP and DEF substats that feed directly into Zayne's damage mitigation scaling.

Verdict: Pull on rerun — essential for Zayne freeze comps. Still no good replacement after 17 months.
Inferno Waltz
Rafayel
Crimson Tide
Original: 2025.03.10
DECENT

Inferno Waltz was Rafayel's first limited SSR, and it made a strong impression at launch. The Crimson Tide set focuses on burst damage from skill activations, and at R0 it provides competitive front-loaded damage that was best-in-class for Rafayel in early 2025. However, two newer Rafayel limited memories — Coral Requiem (2025.09) and Abyssal Sonata (2026.03) — have since surpassed it in sustained DPS and team synergy.

Inferno Waltz is still a perfectly functional DPS memory. It clears Abyssal Chaos floors comfortably and performs adequately in Orbital Tower. The issue is that the Crimson Tide set has been indirectly nerfed by boss design trends — newer bosses have shorter damage windows that don't favor Inferno Waltz's ramp-up pattern. Against stationary or slow bosses it performs well; against the hyper-mobile bosses introduced in version 2.0, it falls behind.

Verdict: Conditional pull — only if you main Rafayel and missed his later limited memories. Otherwise, save for Coral Requiem or Abyssal Sonata reruns.
Twilight Oath
Sylus
Shadowbound Vow
Original: 2025.05.05
POWERCREPT

Twilight Oath was revolutionary at release — it was the first memory to introduce the HP-sacrifice mechanic that became Sylus's signature playstyle. The Shadowbound Vow set converts HP lost into bonus damage, and at launch it gave Sylus the highest single-target burst potential in the game. It dominated Abyssal Chaos leaderboards for three months and was widely considered the best DPS memory in the game.

However, two major shifts have relegated Twilight Oath to "powercrept" status. First, the release of Night's Sovereign (2025.11) and Blood Pact (2026.04) introduced HP-sacrifice mechanics with better scaling, tighter stat distributions, and resonance chains that don't require R3 to unlock full potential. Second, a balance patch in early 2026 adjusted the Shadowbound Vow set's damage conversion rate downward by approximately 12%, which combined with the newer alternatives makes Twilight Oath a noticeably weaker choice. It's still usable in Sylus-specific content, but it's no longer the optimal choice for any team.

Verdict: Skip on rerun — Night's Sovereign and Blood Pact do everything better. Only pull for collection purposes.
Radiant Dawn
Xavier
Solar Flare
Original: 2025.07.22
DECENT

Radiant Dawn was intended as an alternative to Stellar Contract for Xavier players who wanted a more aggressive, burst-oriented playstyle. The Solar Flare set rewards rapid skill cycling, and at R2+ the memory delivers some of the highest burst windows in the game. The problem is that Stellar Contract exists — it outperforms Radiant Dawn in sustained DPS by roughly 8% at equivalent resonance levels, and it's also easier to play.

That said, Radiant Dawn has carved out a niche in speedrun content and time-limited event boss encounters where burst windows matter more than sustained output. If you already have Stellar Contract, Radiant Dawn is a luxury pull — nice to have, but not transformative. If you don't have Stellar Contract, Radiant Dawn serves as a decent substitute that's still competitive in most content.

Verdict: Conditional pull — good if you lack Stellar Contract or want to optimize speedrun times. Otherwise, skip and save for other operatives.

The clear takeaway: Stellar Contract and Permafrost Covenant are the only limited banners from the first wave that still deliver elite value. They are the safest rerun investments. Every other limited memory from the 2025 era has been either partially or fully replaced by newer releases. This doesn't mean they're unusable — it means their rerun value is significantly lower relative to the gem cost.

Papergames tends to follow a structured rotation: older banners (6+ months since debut) enter the rerun pool in roughly chronological order, with occasional exceptions for seasonal or story-aligned memories.

To save effectively for a specific rerun, aim to have at least 14,000 gems (or ~90 pulls) saved before the expected window — this gives you a 50% chance at pulling the featured SSR under standard rates. For a more reliable guarantee (hard pity), budget 28,000 gems (~180 pulls). If you're targeting multiple reruns, prioritize banners with irreplaceable memories (Stellar Contract, Permafrost Covenant) and skip ones with viable alternatives.

!
Overlap Alert: If your desired rerun banner overlaps with a brand-new limited banner, always prioritize the new banner. New limited memories have a higher likelihood of shaping the future meta, and they won't return for at least 6 months — whereas the rerun will cycle back again eventually. The only exception is if the rerun memory is irreplaceable (Stellar Contract or Permafrost Covenant tier) and the new banner looks skippable based on early reviews.

Tip: Use the in-game "Pity Counter" feature to track your current pity status on the limited banner. If you're at 60+ pity with a guaranteed SSR, you can secure a rerun memory with as few as 1,600 gems (10 pulls). Always check your pity before committing to a banner you're unsure about.

Summary: The rerun era has fundamentally changed how players should approach limited banners. Only two 2025-era limited SSRs — Stellar Contract and Permafrost Covenant — remain genuine must-pulls on rerun. Inferno Waltz and Radiant Dawn offer situational value but are outclassed by newer options. Twilight Oath has been fully powercrept and is no longer worth prioritizing. For future rerun planning, save 14k-28k gems per target, prioritize irreplaceable memories, and always check your pity before pulling. The golden rule: a new banner today is worth more than a rerun tomorrow.
