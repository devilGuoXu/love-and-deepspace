DEEPSPACE FIELD LOG • HUNTER STATION ARCHIVE • CLASSIFICATION: RESEARCH • LOG #K-2026-07

This document is a raw, unedited research log covering approximately 100 hours of combat observation across Abyss floors 5-12, weekly boss encounters, and protocore domain farming. The data was collected between March and July 2026 across two accounts. No conclusions were predetermined. The numbers speak for themselves, and I have tried to record them honestly even when they contradicted my assumptions.

SESSION LOG 001 — BASELINE MEASUREMENTS

SUBJECT: Account A (Main) • TOTAL POWER: 48,200 • ABYSS PB: Floor 12 (1-star) • DOMAINS FARMED: 43 • DATE: 2026.03.14

Established baseline for this research project. I need hard data on what actually happens during combat, not what I think happens. Installed a frame counter overlay and started recording all Abyss runs. Initial observation: my dodge success rate is significantly lower than I thought. I estimated 80%. The data says 62%. I am not as good at this game as I believed.

The gap between perceived performance and actual performance is the first discovery of this log. I will be tracking both moving forward. It turns out that confirmation bias applies to dodging too. I remember the perfect dodges and forget the ones that missed. The data does not have that luxury.

RUN 001 • ABYSS FLOOR 7 • 03.14 20:34

RESULT: Clear (2-star) • TIME: 2:48 • DAMAGE TAKEN: 4,200 • DODGE RATE: 58% • MISTAKES: Missed Commander priority, ate two Command Aura buffed hits, delayed rotation during Resonance window

NOTE: The Commander was alive for 47 seconds longer than optimal because I tunnel-visioned on the adds. Standard mistake. Need to train the priority order into muscle memory. The Command Aura buff makes adds hit 20% harder. Not game-ending on floor 7 but would be lethal on floor 10.

RUN 002 • ABYSS FLOOR 7 • 03.14 20:41

RESULT: Clear (3-star) • TIME: 2:12 • DAMAGE TAKEN: 1,100 • DODGE RATE: 71% • MISTAKES: One unnecessary hit from a spawned add

NOTE: Focused on Commander first. Run was 36 seconds faster and took 3,100 less damage. The difference was entirely decision-making, not mechanics. This is going to be a recurring theme in this log. The biggest performance gains come from better decisions, not better execution.

SESSION LOG 002 — PROTOCORE DROP AUDIT

SUBJECT: Accounts A+B • DOMAIN: ATK/CRIT (Lv.5) • RUNS: 100 • DATE: 2026.03.21-03.23

Ran 100 domain runs across two accounts to collect drop rate data. Account A is a moderate spender (battle pass + monthly card). Account B is completely F2P. I wanted to see if there was any hidden spending-based drop rate manipulation. Short answer: no, but the data is interesting for other reasons.

Account A got 15 SSR protocores from 50 runs. Account B got 13 SSR protocores from 50 runs. Combined rate of 14%, which aligns with community estimates. No statistical evidence of spend-based rate adjustment. However, the quality distribution was noticeably different. Account A got 4 pieces with 3+ usable substats. Account B got 1. This is almost certainly variance within normal range, but I am noting it for future observation.

The more interesting finding: 78% of all SSR drops had HP% or DEF% as the main stat. Only 22% had ATK% or elemental damage. The domain was the ATK/CRIT domain. This is either terrible luck or the game weighting main stat distributions toward defensive stats. More data needed. If true, it means the time to get a usable ATK% piece is approximately 50% longer than expected.

FINDING 003 • MAIN STAT DISTRIBUTION • 03.23

DATA: 28 SSR drops. Main stats: HP% (11), DEF% (8), ATK% (4), CRIT Rate (3), CRIT DMG (1), Healing Bonus (1). ATK% rate: 14.2%.

CONCLUSION: If ATK% is truly ~14% of SSR drops, and substats are independent, the expected runs for a usable ATK% piece with 2+ good substats is approximately 95 runs. At 40 stamina per run, that is 3,800 stamina per usable piece. For a full team of 5 pieces, 19,000 stamina. This is a depressing number and I am not sure I want to keep tracking it.

SESSION LOG 003 — DODGE TIMING ANALYSIS

SUBJECT: Account A • ENCOUNTER: Crystal Monarch (Frost) • ATTEMPTS: 50 • DATE: 2026.04.05

Focused entirely on dodge timing for a single boss. No attacking. Just dodging and recording frame data. The Crystal Monarch was chosen because her attacks have distinctive telegraphs with varying timing windows.

The perfect dodge window in this game is approximately 12 frames at 30 FPS (200ms). This is consistent across all bosses I tested. However, the telegraph duration varies significantly by attack type. Quick slashes have a 400ms telegraph. Charged attacks have 800-1,200ms. AoE attacks have 600-900ms. The variation means that muscle memory developed for one boss does not transfer perfectly to another, but the underlying timing for the dodge itself is constant.

FINDING 004 • TELEGRAPH TIMING BY ATTACK TYPE • 04.05

CRYSTAL RAIN: First icicle spawns at telegraph + 600ms. Gap appears at telegraph + 300ms. If you move to the gap at telegraph recognition, you have 300ms to position.

SHARD BURST: Telegraph + 800ms. The burst fires in a 30-degree cone. If you dodge perpendicular at telegraph + 400ms, you will clear the cone entirely.

CORE PULSE: Telegraph + 1,200ms. This is a full-room AoE. You cannot dodge it. You must block or use an invulnerability frame ability. This is the single most damaging attack in the fight and the primary cause of failed runs.

Observation: the Core Pulse attack has a 1,200ms telegraph, which is long enough for most players to recognize and react. But the correct response is not to dodge. It is to use a specific ability or shield. Players who try to dodge it die 100% of the time. In my 50 attempts, I recorded 12 deaths total. 11 were from Core Pulse. One was from getting cornered by Crystal Rain. The data is clear: knowing which attacks cannot be dodged is more important than perfecting dodge timing.

SESSION LOG 004 — ROTATION MISTAKE AUDIT

SUBJECT: Account A • CONTENT: Abyss Floors 5-12 • RUNS: 30 • DATE: 2026.04.12-04.14

Recorded and analyzed 30 Abyss runs to categorize mistakes. I categorized every clear run into mistake types and counted frequency. The goal was to identify which mistakes are most common and which are most costly.

DATA • MISTAKE FREQUENCY (30 RUNS) • 04.14

SUBOPTIMAL ROTATION: 47 occurrences • Average time loss: 12s • Most common on Floor 9 (transitions between phases)

MISSED DODGE: 38 occurrences • Average time loss: 4s (recovery) • Most common on Floor 11 (fast attack chains)

WRONG TARGET PRIORITY: 22 occurrences • Average time loss: 18s • Most common on Floor 7 & 10 (add spawns)

ULTIMATE WASTED: 14 occurrences • Average time loss: 20s • Most common on Floor 12 (poor timing with boss phases)

DEATH: 8 occurrences • Average time loss: 30s+ (restart) • Most common on Floor 10 (laser + add combo)

The data confirms that suboptimal rotation is the most common and most impactful mistake pattern. Not dying. Not missing dodges. Running the wrong rotation sequence. The average player loses 12 seconds per run to suboptimal rotations. Over 30 runs, that is 360 seconds of cumulative time loss. For context, the difference between 2-star and 3-star on floor 9 is often less than 15 seconds.

I spent the next week drilling my rotation on a single operative (Sylus) until I could execute it without thinking. My clear times improved by an average of 9% across all floors. The improvement came entirely from eliminating hesitation between abilities. I already knew the correct sequence. I was just pausing between steps to remember what came next. Drilling removed the pauses.

Practical recommendation: pick one operative and run their rotation 50 times on a training dummy or easy content. Not until you get it right. Fifty times. That is approximately 15-20 minutes. The improvement will be noticeable and immediate. I have done this with three operatives now and it works every time.

SESSION LOG 005 — ENERGY MODELING

SUBJECT: Account A • OPERATIVES: All 4 • RUNS: 40 • DATE: 2026.05.02-05.04

Modeled energy regeneration rates for all four operatives to determine optimal rotation timing. Prior community research suggests energy regen breakpoints at 125% and 145%, but I wanted to verify with my own account and gear.

FINDING 005 • ENERGY BREAKPOINT VERIFICATION • 05.04

SYLUS (145% ER): Generates 7.25 energy per basic attack, 29 per Resonance, 43.5 per Ardent Oath. Four rotations to ultimate. VERIFIED.

ZAYNE (125% ER): Shield cooldown reduced to 9.6 seconds from 12. Shield uptime approaches 100% with perfect execution. VERIFIED.

XAVIER (120% ER): Resonance cooldown at 8.3 seconds instead of 10. Minor improvement. Not worth building around.

RAFAYEL (100% ER baseline): His burn application is tied to Resonance usage, not energy. ER substats have minimal impact on his damage profile.

The Xavier finding was disappointing. I had been farming ER substats for him, hoping to reach a meaningful breakpoint. The return is approximately 1.7 seconds of cooldown reduction, which translates to roughly 3% more Resonance casts over a 2-minute fight. That is negligible. I am going to switch his substat priority back to ATK% and CRIT, where the returns are 4-5x higher.

I also tested whether the 145% ER breakpoint for Sylus was achievable without sacrificing too many offensive substats. I built a set with 143% ER (close but not at breakpoint) and compared it to my standard 120% ER set. The 143% set required sacrificing 12% CRIT DMG. The damage difference was within 2%. I suspect the 145% breakpoint is only worth chasing if you have perfect substat RNG that allows you to reach it without dropping offensive stats. For most players, 120-130% ER is the practical sweet spot.

SESSION LOG 006 — SURVIVABILITY ANALYSIS

SUBJECT: Accounts A+B • CONTENT: All Abyss floors • RUNS: 50 • DATE: 2026.05.20-05.25

Tracked every death across 50 Abyss runs to determine what actually kills players. I wanted to see if the community focus on DPS is justified or if survivability is being undervalued.

DATA • DEATH CAUSES (50 RUNS, 23 DEATHS) • 05.25

ONE-SHOT MECHANICS: 11 deaths (48%) • Primarily Floor 10 laser, Floor 12 enrage, Floor 8 DoT + add combo

CHIP DAMAGE ACCUMULATION: 7 deaths (30%) • Death by a thousand cuts. Multiple small hits over 30-60 seconds.

POSITIONING ERROR: 3 deaths (13%) • Cornered by AoE patterns, usually Floor 9 and Floor 11

UNKNOWN/TIMING: 2 deaths (9%) • Could not determine cause from recording

The finding that surprised me: chip damage accumulation caused 30% of deaths. These are not dramatic one-shot moments. They are slow, preventable deaths caused by taking avoidable small hits over time. The community fix for this is usually "get more HP" or "build shields," but the data suggests the real fix is "stop getting hit by small attacks." Each small hit is individually survivable. The problem is that players take 15-20 small hits over the course of a fight and run out of HP.

I tested a hypothesis: if I focused entirely on dodging small attacks (the ones that deal 5-10% HP each), would my death rate decrease more than if I added 20% more HP through gear? I ran 20 more runs with a dodge-first priority on small attacks. Deaths from chip damage dropped from 30% to 8%. Deaths from one-shots remained roughly the same (they are usually unavoidable without specific preparation). The conclusion: survivability improvements come more from behavior changes than gear changes, at least for chip damage.

For one-shot mechanics, the solution is binary: learn the telegraph or build specific resistance. There is no middle ground. Either you dodge the Floor 10 laser or you die. No amount of HP saves you. This reinforces my earlier finding: mechanical knowledge beats gear for specific fight-defining attacks.

SESSION LOG 007 — STELLACTRUM MATCHING ROI

SUBJECT: Accounts A+B • CONTENT: Abyss Floors 5-9 • RUNS: 40 • DATE: 2026.06.10-06.12

Quantified the actual benefit of Stellactrum matching. The community claims 30% damage bonus. I wanted to verify this with controlled testing.

FINDING 006 • STELLACTRUM DAMAGE MULTIPLIER • 06.12

TEST 1: Full match (all 6 colors aligned) • Average DPS: 21,400 • Increase over zero match: 28.3%

TEST 2: Partial match (3 colors aligned) • Average DPS: 18,900 • Increase over zero match: 13.1%

TEST 3: No match (0 colors) • Average DPS: 16,700 • Baseline

The 30% community figure is accurate for full matching. However, the more useful finding is that partial matching provides disproportionate returns. Three colors (half the requirement) gives 46% of the full bonus. This means that building for partial matching on a second team is much more efficient than most guides suggest. You do not need full matching on team 2 to get meaningful value. Three matching colors, which is achievable with most rosters, gives over 13% bonus damage. That is often enough to clear floors 5-7.

I restructured my second team around this finding. Instead of forcing full matching and using suboptimal operatives, I now use my strongest operatives with partial matching. My floor 7 clear rate went from inconsistent to 95%. The change was not gear or skill. It was roster flexibility.

SESSION LOG 008 — FINAL OBSERVATIONS

SUBJECT: All data • TOTAL RUNS: 410 • TOTAL TIME: ~100 hours • DATE: 2026.07.05

One hundred hours of data collection across two accounts and six months. Here are the conclusions I am most confident in:

Protocore farming is less efficient than the community math suggests. The published rates assume independent substat rolls. My data suggests there is a correlation between substat quality and main stat type that reduces the effective rate of usable pieces by approximately 30%. I cannot prove this mechanism exists, but the numbers on my account are worse than the theoretical rates predict.

Decision quality matters more than execution quality. The difference between a good player and a great player in this game is primarily decisions, not mechanics. Which attack to dodge versus which to tank. Which add to kill first. When to use ultimate versus when to save it. These decisions account for approximately 70% of the performance variance I observed between runs with the same account.

Community advice is skewed toward late-game assumptions. The most common advice (never pull standard, chase set bonuses, prioritize CRIT over ATK) is optimized for accounts with high baseline stats and deep rosters. For newer or mid-game accounts, the optimal strategies are often the opposite. The advice is not wrong. It is just context-dependent, and the context is rarely stated.

Taking breaks improves performance. I recorded a 6% improvement in dodge rate and an 8% improvement in rotation accuracy after taking a 3-day break. The improvement persisted for approximately two weeks before returning to baseline. Fatigue has a measurable negative impact on gameplay. The optimal play schedule appears to be 5-6 days of play followed by 1-2 days off.
