Recording & Capture Tools

For gameplay recording, OBS Studio is the gold standard — free, open-source, and feature-complete. It supports: multi-source recording (game capture + webcam + overlay), custom scene transitions, per-source audio routing (separate game audio, mic audio, and alert audio), and streaming to any platform simultaneously. Configure OBS with NVENC encoding (Nvidia GPUs) or AMF encoding (AMD GPUs) for hardware-accelerated recording with minimal performance impact on your game. Target settings: 1080p60 at 15-30 Mbps bitrate for YouTube uploads, or 936p60 at 6-8 Mbps for Twitch streaming.

For mobile recording (if you play LADS on your phone), use the built-in screen recorder (iOS and Android both have good native options) or connect your phone to a capture card. The Elgato HD60 X is the recommended capture card for phone-to-PC recording — it supports 4K60 passthrough and USB-C connectivity. For audio, a basic dynamic microphone (Samson Q2U or Audio-Technica ATR2100x) provides broadcast-quality sound for $60-80. The single biggest quality improvement for new creators is audio — viewers will tolerate mediocre video but will leave at the first sign of bad audio. Invest your first $50 in a decent mic, not a better camera.

Mobile recording tip: If recording from a phone, enable "Do Not Disturb" mode and lock screen orientation to landscape before recording. Use the phone's native screen recorder at the highest quality setting (usually 1080p60 or 4K30). Transfer files via USB 3.0 or Wi-Fi Direct (avoid cloud transfers — they compress video). For commentary, record game audio and mic audio on separate tracks using a multi-track recorder app or record mic separately in OBS while syncing via a clap slate at the start of the recording.

Editing Software

Video editing software ranges from free to professional. DaVinci Resolve is the best free option — it has professional-grade color grading, Fairlight audio tools (noise reduction, compression, EQ), Fusion visual effects compositing, and a cut page for quick editing. The learning curve is steeper than consumer editors, but the quality ceiling is unlimited. For beginners, Shotcut (free, simpler UI) or CapCut (free, mobile-friendly, good for short-form content — TikTok/Shorts/Reels) are more accessible. For paid options, Adobe Premiere Pro (industry standard, $23/mo) and Final Cut Pro ($300 one-time, Mac only) offer the smoothest workflows for long-form content.

For Love and Deepspace specifically, the editing tools you need most: fast cut/trim for removing downtime (overworld navigation, menu navigation, loading screens), text overlay for stat callouts and build information, zoom/pan for highlighting specific game elements (protocore substats, memory set names), audio ducking (auto-lower game audio when speaking), and transitions (simple cross-dissolves — avoid flashy transitions that distract from the game content). Built-in captions/ subtitles are increasingly important — 60%+ of viewers watch with sound off on mobile platforms. DaVinci Resolve and Premiere Pro both have auto-captioning features.

Free asset resources: For thumbnails and channel art: Canva (free tier has LADS-compatible templates), GIMP (free Photoshop alternative), and Photopea (browser-based Photoshop clone). For background music (royalty-free): StreamBeats by Harris Heller, Chillhop Music, and the YouTube Audio Library. For sound effects: freesound.org and Mixkit. Never use copyrighted music — LADS's own soundtrack is copyrighted and will trigger content ID claims. Use royalty-free music only, and credit the artist in your description.

Streaming Setup

Live streaming Love and Deepspace requires a different setup than recorded content. Stream to Twitch for discoverability (LADS has a dedicated category with active viewership) or YouTube for VOD archival (streams automatically save as long-form videos). Your streaming PC needs: a 6+ core CPU (Ryzen 5 or Intel i5 equivalent), 16GB+ RAM, a GPU that supports NVENC/AMF encoding (GTX 1660 or better), and stable internet with 10+ Mbps upload speed. For a single-PC stream, use NVENC encoding (it uses the GPU encoder, leaving the CPU free for game processing).

Essential streaming tools: StreamElements or Streamlabs for overlays, alerts, chatbot, and loyalty points. Use a "Starting Soon" screen (2-3 minutes of chill music + schedule info) and a "Be Right Back" screen for breaks. Set up a "!build" command in chat that auto-posts a link to your build sheet or a screenshot. Install the LADS stream overlay (available from the Workshop tools page) that shows your current team, stats, and Abyss progress on screen. For chat interaction, enable Twitch's "Verified Bot" filter to avoid spam, and use Nightbot or Fossabot for moderation commands.

Talk continuously even during quiet moments (menuing, loading screens). Read chat regularly — respond to questions about builds. Explain your decisions as you play ("I am farming dust domain because Xavier needs more CRIT substats"). This educational content is what brings viewers back.

Post-stream
End stream with a summary of what was accomplished. Save VOD to YouTube (most streaming platforms auto-archive). Post highlights (clips of 30-60 seconds) to TikTok/Shorts/Reels within 24 hours. The clips platform is how new viewers discover you — invest as much effort in clips as in the full stream.

Graphics & Assets

Consistent branding sets professional creators apart. Create a channel kit: profile picture (your avatar or a LADS character), banner art (1920×1080 for YouTube, 1200×480 for Twitch), thumbnail template (900×540 for YouTube — use a grid overlay for consistency), and a color palette that complements LADS's aesthetic (deep blues, purples, gold accents are thematic). Keep the branding minimal — the game's visuals are gorgeous on their own; your branding should frame them, not compete with them.

For LADS-specific assets: the community provides libre icons for each operative, element symbols, memory set symbols, and tier list templates. These are available in the Workshop Creator Resources pack (linked below). Use these assets to create build card templates, tier list graphics, and infographics. Infographics (stat breakpoints, farming routes, material costs) are the highest-performing content format on Pinterest, Twitter, and Reddit — they are shareable, saveable, and referenceable. A well-designed material cost infographic generates 10x more engagement than a video on the same topic.

Thumbnail strategy: Your thumbnail is 80% of your click-through rate. Use the "3-second test" — if someone cannot understand what the video is about in 3 seconds of looking at the thumbnail, redesign it. Best practices for LADS thumbnails: use a single operative as the focal point (face close-up with expression), include 1-2 text words max ("NEW META" "F2P BUILD" "FLOOR 12"), use high-contrast colors (gold text on dark blue background), and keep the image uncluttered. Study the top LADS creators' thumbnails — they all follow the same formula for a reason.

Community Management

Building a community around your LADS content requires tools beyond the recording software. Discord server (free) is the primary community hub — set up channels for: #build-discussion, #showcase (screenshot sharing), #help-questions, #event-discussion, and #creator-feedback. Use Discord bots like MEE6 (levels and moderation), Carl-bot (reaction roles, auto-moderation), and YAGPDB (custom commands, logging). For social media scheduling, use Buffer (free tier for 3 platforms) or Later (visual Instagram/Twitter scheduler).

Community engagement metrics to track: engagement rate (comments + likes / followers — target 3-5% for healthy growth), reply rate (percentage of comments you reply to within 24 hours — target 90%+), video completion rate (percentage of viewers who watch to the end — target 40%+ for tutorial content, 20%+ for entertainment content), and subscriber conversion rate (views to subscriber — target 5-10%). These metrics matter more than raw view counts because they measure how well you connect with your audience. A creator with 1,000 engaged followers is more successful than a creator with 10,000 passive followers.

Growth tip: The fastest way to grow a LADS content channel is collaboration. Reach out to other LADS creators (especially those at a similar size) for: co-op streams (play together and cross-promote), build challenge collaborations (both build the same operative and compare results), and community event hosting (joint giveaways or tournaments). Collaboration exposes both audiences to both creators and builds community connections that survive individual channel fluctuations. The LADS creator community is welcoming to newcomers — do not be afraid to reach out.

Content Planning & Scheduling

Content planning prevents burnout. Create a content calendar aligned with the LADS event schedule: when a new event drops, plan a First-Impressions video (within 24 hours), a Build Guide (within 48-72 hours, once you have tested the new memories), and an Efficiency Analysis (within 1 week). Between events, fill with evergreen content: tier lists, farming guides, beginner tutorials, and meme/fun content. The ratio: 60% timely (event-driven) content, 30% evergreen (searchable guides), 10% experimental (new formats, entertainment).

Schedule consistency matters more than schedule frequency. One video per week on the same day at the same time builds a reliable audience habit. Post on YouTube at 2 PM EST (peak afternoon viewership) and on TikTok/Shorts at 7 PM EST (peak evening scrolling). Use YouTube's "Premiere" feature for high-production videos — it creates an event-like viewing experience with live chat. Batch-record 2-3 videos on your content day (usually Sunday or Monday) so you always have a buffer. A 2-week video buffer eliminates the stress of week-of deadlines and lets you respond to unexpected events without breaking your schedule.

Pacing yourself: New creators often burn out by trying to post daily. Daily content is not sustainable for one person doing this as a hobby. Start with 1 video per week. When you have: 1,000 subscribers (add 1 Short per week), 5,000 subscribers (add 1 stream per week), 10,000 subscribers (add 1 video per week). Scale content output with audience size, not ambition. Burnout is the #1 reason LADS content creators quit. Protect your playtime — you cannot create content about a game you are burnt out on.

LADS-Specific Creator Tips

Love and Deepspace content has unique characteristics that creators should leverage. The game's visual quality (anime-style character models, detailed memory card art, flashy skill effects) makes it inherently watchable — your content starts with a visual advantage over games with simpler graphics. The community's appetite for guides is insatiable — every new event, memory, or mechanic creates a content opportunity. The four operatives create natural series potential: "Xavier Build Guide," then "Zayne Build Guide," then the series continues.

Content formats that perform best for LADS: Build Guides (40-60% of top creators' libraries — most searchable, longest shelf life), Event Reviews (20-30% — time-sensitive but high traffic during events), Lore Analysis (10-20% — passionate niche audience, highly engaged), and Entertainment (10-20% — memes, funny moments, challenges — highest viral potential but shortest shelf life). The most successful LADS creators have a mix of all four formats. Pure guide channels grow steadily but slowly. Pure entertainment channels spike in views but plateau. The hybrid approach produces sustainable growth.
