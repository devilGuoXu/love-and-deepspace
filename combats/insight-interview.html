An Interview with a Day-One Player

Conducted July 2026 — 2,500+ hours of experience

I sat down with Marcus, a launch-day player who has logged over 2,500 hours across two accounts. He has seen every patch, every meta shift, and every community drama. We talked about what he got wrong, what surprised him, and what he wishes someone had told him on day one.

Q:

Let us start at the beginning. What made you pick up this game on launch day?

A: Marcus

Honestly? I saw the trailer and thought the combat looked smooth. I had been playing other gachas and wanted something with real-time action instead of turn-based. I did not even know about the romance elements until I was already in the game. That was a surprise. A pleasant one, but a surprise.

I almost dropped it in the first week because I was overwhelmed by the number of systems. Memories, protocores, Stellactrum, affinity, the base, photo mode. I remember thinking: this game does not trust me to be bored for even five minutes. There is always another system to learn.

Q:

What was the biggest mistake you made early on?

A: Marcus

I treated this game like a sprint. I wanted to get to the endgame as fast as possible, so I rushed through everything. I skipped story dialogue. I auto-equipped memories. I ignored Stellactrum entirely because I did not understand it. I hit a wall on Abyss floor 4 around level 40 and could not figure out why. I had a team of level 70 memories with no matching elements and no set bonuses.

I spent three weeks stuck on floor 4. Three weeks. When I finally sat down and actually learned how Stellactrum works, I cleared floor 7 in the same session. The lesson stuck with me: this game rewards knowledge more than grinding. I had been grinding inefficiently because I did not understand the systems I was grinding for.

Q:

How has your opinion of the meta changed over time?

A: Marcus

Dramatically. At launch, everyone thought Xavier was the weakest operative because his numbers looked lower on paper. The community tier list had him in B-tier. Fast forward six months and people realized his Light Seeker set has a hidden internal cooldown mechanic that makes him one of the highest DPS operatives in sustained fights. The same thing happened with Rafayel's burn build. People dismissed it until someone did the math and showed that burn does 40% more damage than the tooltip suggests.

The meta in this game is not stable. It shifts as people discover new interactions. The launch tier lists are almost completely wrong in retrospect. I have learned to take any tier list with a grain of salt until at least two months of community testing have passed.

Q:

What is the most underrated system in the game?

A: Marcus

Resonance chains. Nobody talks about them enough. The damage boost from a well-timed resonance chain is massive, but most players never optimize their rotation to trigger them consistently. I see players with perfect protocores and terrible resonance chain uptime, and they wonder why their damage is mediocre.

The other underrated system is the photo mode. I am only half joking. The photo mode forces you to engage with the character models and environments in a way that normal gameplay does not. It made me appreciate the visual design of the game more, which made me more invested in the characters, which made me want to build them better. There is a feedback loop there that the game designers absolutely intended.

Q:

What do you think the game does best?

A: Marcus

The combat feel. I keep coming back to this. The animations are weighty, the sound design is punchy, the dodge mechanic is satisfying. For a mobile game, the combat is genuinely impressive. I have played console action games with worse feel. The fact that it runs smoothly on a phone is a technical achievement.

I also think the game does an excellent job of making you care about the characters. The writing is not Shakespeare, but it does not need to be. It is warm and genuine, and it gives you enough emotional hooks to invest in the characters beyond their combat utility. I have never spent money on a gacha character just because they were meta. I have spent money because I wanted to see more of a character's story. That is good writing, or at least effective writing.

Q:

And what does it do worst?

A: Marcus

Protocore enhancement, and it is not close. The RNG layered on top of RNG is excessive. You need the right set, the right main stat, the right substat lines, and then the enhancement rolls need to go into the right substats. That is four layers of randomness for a single piece of gear. Most games stop at two layers.

The worst part is that there is no pity system for protocores. No guaranteed substat roll. No way to reroll a bad enhancement. You just have to farm more and hope. I have gone over 200 runs without a single usable piece. That feels terrible. It is the one system in the game that I think is genuinely poorly designed rather than intentionally grind-heavy.

Q:

What advice would you give to a new player starting today?

A: Marcus

Three things. One: read the ability descriptions. Not just the numbers, but the actual text. The game explains mechanics in the tooltips, but most players skip them. Two: build one team before you build two. I see new players spreading resources across four operatives and ending up with four mediocre ones. Focus everything on your main DPS until they are level 80 with decent protocores, then build your second DPS, then fill out the supports last.

Three: do not compare your account to Reddit. Reddit is a highlight reel. People post their perfect protocores and their lucky pulls. They do not post the 300 dry runs or the lost 50-50s. You are seeing the best moments of thousands of players. Comparing your average account to their best moments is a recipe for frustration.

Q:

One year in, do you regret any of your spending?

A: Marcus

Some of it. I spent on banners that I did not really care about because I was caught up in the FOMO. I look back at those purchases and they do not bring me any joy. The purchases I do not regret are the ones I made for characters I genuinely love. The monthly pass has been worth it for the stamina alone. The battle passes have been hit or miss depending on the exclusive memory.

My rule now is: wait 48 hours after a banner drops before pulling. If I still want the character after two days of thinking about it, it is not an impulse. This has saved me from at least three regretful pulls.

Q:

Final question. Will you still be playing a year from now?

A: Marcus

Probably. I have tried to quit twice, and both times I came back within a month. The game has its hooks in me. The combat is still fun. The characters still make me smile. The community still has people I enjoy talking to. As long as those three things are true, I will keep playing.

The day I quit will probably not be dramatic. I will just log in one day and realize I do not want to do my dailies, and I will close the app, and I will not open it again. That day will come eventually. It comes for every game. But it has not come yet.

Marcus has been playing since global launch and maintains two accounts. He prefers Sylus for combat and Xavier for story. He has never pulled a double SSR in a single multi and is still waiting.
