Confessions of a Protocore Hoarder

By Kaelen — July 10, 2026 — 18 min read

My name is Kaelen, and I am a protocore hoarder. It started innocently enough. A protocore here, a protocore there. Before I knew it, I had 347 protocores in my inventory and had not enhanced a single piece in six weeks. This is my story. This is my intervention. This is the uncomfortable truth about what protocore farming does to a person.

The Beginning of the End

It started with a single perfect piece. I was farming the HP domain because my Zayne needed a better planetary set, and on run number 47 of that session, it dropped. A gold SSR protocore with HP% main stat, CRIT Rate substat, CRIT DMG substat, ATK% substat, and flat DEF. Four lines, three of them perfect, one dead. I stared at it for probably two full minutes. I took a screenshot. I sent it to my friend group chat. I felt like I had won the lottery.

I enhanced it to +15 and three of the four rolls went into CRIT Rate and CRIT DMG. The fourth rolled into flat DEF, which I chose to ignore. The piece was a monster. It single-handedly improved my Zayne's damage by about 12%. I was elated. I was hooked. I wanted that feeling again.

I went back to the domain. I ran it 100 more times over the next three days. I got exactly zero usable pieces. Not one. I got SSR protocores with flat HP main stats. I got SSR protocores with DEF% main stat and three offensive substats that taunted me with what could have been. I got SSR protocores that were perfect except the one substat that mattered was missing. I got 43 SSR protocores over 100 runs, by my count, and exactly zero of them were worth enhancing.

And that was when the hoarding started. I stopped enhancing entirely. Every protocore went into the inventory. I told myself I was saving them for a rainy day. I told myself I would sort through them later. I told myself a lot of things, because the alternative was admitting that I had wasted three days and 4,000 stamina for absolutely nothing.

The Inventory

By week four of my hoarding spiral, my protocore inventory had reached critical mass. I had 347 pieces. 213 SR, 74 SSR, 60 R. I had organized them by set, then by main stat, then by number of usable substat lines. I had a system. Rows and rows of color-coded potential, each one representing a run I had done, a hope I had carried, a disappointment I had deferred.

I started having dreams about protocores. Literal dreams. I would wake up in the middle of the night with the ghost sensation of a gold drop, my hand reaching for my phone before I remembered it was 3 AM and the domains were not even open. I checked my inventory before brushing my teeth in the morning. I organized it during meetings at work, swiping through screens under my desk, rearranging pieces that nobody else could see and that I was never going to use.

My friend noticed first. We were discussing our accounts over Discord, and she asked me what protocore set I was running on my Sylus. I launched into a three-minute monologue about the five different sets I was considering, the substat distributions I was tracking, the probability calculations I had done. She was silent for a long time after I finished.

"Kaelen," she said finally, "when was the last time you actually enhanced a piece?"

I did not have an answer. I checked my enhancement history. Six weeks. I had not enhanced a single protocore in six weeks. I had been farming them, sorting them, hoarding them, and using none of them. My Sylus was still running the same protocores he had been using for two months. My Zayne was still on the same pieces. I had a better inventory than 90% of players and worse equipped operatives than people who had been playing half as long as me.

That was my rock bottom.

The Five Stages of Protocore Grief

I have identified five distinct emotional stages that every protocore farmer goes through. Recognizing them in yourself is the first step toward recovery.

Stage 1: Denial

You get a protocore with a bad main stat and three amazing substats. You convince yourself that maybe, just maybe, flat DEF is secretly good. You look for niche builds that could use it. You search Reddit for copium threads about off-meta protocores. You know, deep down, that this piece is garbage. But you cannot bring yourself to trash it, because it has CRIT Rate and CRIT DMG, and if you just squint hard enough, it looks almost perfect.

I had 22 protocores in my inventory that I was keeping for "niche builds." I never built a single one of them. They sat there for months, occupying space and hope, until I finally trashed them all in a fit of clarity. I do not miss them.

Stage 2: Bargaining

You run the domain 50 times and get nothing. You swear you will quit farming if the next run gives you something usable. The next run gives you an R-tier piece. You swear again. This time you mean it. You run it 50 more times. You get an SSR with the wrong main stat. You bargain with the game, with RNGesus, with whatever force is conspiring against you. You promise to be a better person, to never skip your dailies, to leave positive comments on fan art. The domain does not care. The domain has never cared.

Bargaining is the most dangerous stage because it keeps you farming past the point of reason. There is always one more run. Always one more chance. The sunk cost fallacy is real, and the domain design exploits it ruthlessly.

Stage 3: Anger

This is the stage where you start having unreasonable emotional reactions to protocore drops. You get an SSR piece with HP% main stat and three offensive substats, and you are not happy about the potential value. You are angry that it is not the one you wanted. You trash it immediately out of spite, then immediately regret it because maybe that piece would have been good for a support you have not built yet.

I threw my phone across the room once. Not hard, not at anything breakable, just a frustrated toss onto the couch. The cause: seeing a +15 enhancement all go into flat HP for the fourth time in a row. I sat there, staring at the couch cushion where my phone had landed, and asked myself what I was doing with my life. The answer was not comforting.

Stage 4: Depression

You stop caring. You auto-enhance everything to +3 and trash it if it does not roll well. You stop checking main stats on R and SR pieces before trashing them. You run the domain on auto while watching Netflix, not even looking at the results screen. The game becomes a background process, a series of taps and swipes that you perform without emotional engagement. You are farming protocores not because you believe you will get something good, but because you do not know what else to farm.

This stage can last weeks. It is functionally similar to playing optimally, because you are still running domains and still getting drops. But the emotional experience is completely different. You are not playing the game. You are going through the motions. I stayed in Stage 4 for most of month six, and I almost quit entirely.

Stage 5: Acceptance

This is where I am now, most of the time. Acceptance is not happiness. It is not satisfaction. It is the quiet understanding that protocore farming is a long-term process with no guarantees, and that is okay. You stop expecting perfection. You stop chasing the high of the perfect piece. You enhance what is usable, settle for what is good enough, and move on with your life.

Acceptance looks like: enhancing a protocore to +12 even though it only has two usable substats, because it is a 10% upgrade over what you are currently using. Acceptance looks like: trashing a perfect piece that rolled all into flat DEF, feeling a brief pang of disappointment, and moving on to the next run. Acceptance looks like: running domains because you need the resources, not because you are chasing a dopamine hit.

I relapse sometimes. I still have days where I convince myself that this next run will be the one, that my luck is due to turn around, that I deserve a perfect piece after all this effort. But those days are fewer now. Most days, I just farm, enhance what I get, and accept the outcome.

The Numbers That Broke Me

I want to share some real numbers from my farming history. I have been tracking protocore drops for about eight months now, and I have enough data to draw some uncomfortable conclusions.

Over 500 domain runs at level 5, I recorded 72 SSR drops. That is a 14.4% SSR rate, which aligns with community estimates. Of those 72 SSRs, I would classify 18 as "usable" (correct main stat, at least two usable substat lines before enhancement) and 3 as "perfect" (correct main stat, three or more usable substat lines before enhancement, and rolls went into the right stats after enhancement).

That means 2.5% of all protocore drops I got were perfect. Or, looking at it another way, I spent 20,000 stamina to get three perfect pieces. Twenty thousand stamina. That is 83 days of natural regen. Almost three months of doing nothing but protocore farming for three pieces of gear.

Here is where it gets worse. I kept tracking what happened when I enhanced those 18 usable pieces. Of the 18, only 6 rolled well enough to actually use on a main team. The other 12 rolled predominantly into the dead substats and ended up being worse than what I already had. So my actual usable rate from 500 runs was 6 pieces. One piece per 83 runs. One piece per 3,333 stamina. One piece per 14 days of dedicated farming.

And I need 15 pieces for a full five-operative Abyss team. Fifteen pieces at 14 days per piece is 210 days. Seven months. For one team.

The game gives you the illusion of infinite customization while engineering a system of permanent scarcity.

I want to be clear: I do not think this is necessarily bad game design. The game needs a long-term grind to keep players engaged between content updates. Protocores serve that purpose. But I think it is important to go into the system with open eyes, knowing that you will probably never have a perfect set on every operative, and that is by design.

Intervention: How I Started Enhancing Again

My friend staged an intervention. That is not hyperbole. She called me on Discord, shared her screen, and walked me through trashing my hoarded protocores one by one. We set a rule: if a piece was not equipped on an operative, it had to pass the "would I enhance this immediately" test. If the answer was no, it got trashed.

We trashed 296 protocores that night. I kept 51. Of those 51, I enhanced 12 immediately and equipped them on various operatives. My account got measurably stronger in one evening than it had in the previous six weeks of hoarding. The improvement was immediate and visible. My Sylus jumped from 12,400 power to 13,100 power from three protocore upgrades alone. I had been sitting on those pieces for weeks, refusing to acknowledge that they were upgrades because they were not perfect.

I developed a simple system after that intervention, and I have been using it ever since. Every protocore that enters my inventory gets a decision within 24 hours:

ConditionAction
Correct main stat, 3+ good substatsEnhance to +12 immediately
Correct main stat, 2 good substatsEnhance to +6, check rolls, decide
Correct main stat, 1 good substatEnhance to +3, trash if first roll misses
Wrong main stat (SSR)Keep for future, max 10 pieces total
Wrong main stat (SR or below)Trash immediately

The "max 10 pieces" rule is important. I keep a small reserve of SSR protocores with wrong main stats in case a future operative needs a different stat. But only 10. Not 347. The discipline of the cap is what prevents the hoarding from restarting.

The Protocore Mindset

I have thought a lot about why protocore farming is so psychologically compelling. I think it comes down to the intermittent reinforcement schedule. The game awards protocores on a variable ratio schedule, which is the same reinforcement schedule used by slot machines. You never know when the next good piece will drop, so you keep pulling the lever, run after run, convinced that the next one could be the one.

The problem is that unlike a slot machine, where the house edge is fixed and the eventual loss is guaranteed, protocore farming has the possibility of genuine, permanent gain. You can get a perfect piece that makes your account permanently better. That possibility changes the math. It is not a negative-sum game like gambling. It is a positive-sum game with extremely high variance. And that makes it harder to walk away from, because the logic is sound. You might actually get something good next run. The math does eventually work out over hundreds of runs. The question is whether you are willing to do the hundreds of runs.

I have decided that I am not. Not anymore. I do 20 protocore runs per week, maximum. I enhance what I get. I use what is good enough. I accept that my account will never have perfect gear on every operative, and I have made peace with that. My clearing rate has not noticeably dropped. My enjoyment of the game has noticeably increased.

The best gear in the game is the gear you actually use. An unenhanced perfect piece contributes exactly zero damage. — Something I wish someone had told me six months ago

If you are reading this and nodding along, recognizing yourself in my story, here is my advice: open your inventory right now. If you have more than 50 protocores, you have a hoarding problem. If you have more than 100, you need an intervention. Trash everything you are not going to enhance in the next week. Enhance the pieces that could be upgrades. Equip them even if they are not perfect. Your account will be stronger for it, and your relationship with the game will be healthier.

Or do not. It is your account. Your journey. Your protocores. Maybe you are happy hoarding. Maybe the act of collecting is the part you enjoy. That is valid too. But if you are suffering, if you are farming past the point of fun, if you are checking your inventory at 3 AM wondering why you cannot stop. Know that there is a way out. It starts with trashing a single protocore. Just one. See how it feels.

Postscript: Six Months Later

It has been six months since my intervention. My inventory currently holds 38 protocores. I run domains for specific purposes, not as a daily ritual. I have enhanced exactly 47 pieces in that time, of which 14 made it onto my main teams. My Abyss clear rate has gone from floor 10 to floor 12. My power level has increased by about 15%. I spend less time on protocore management per week than I used to spend in a single day.

I still get tempted. I still have the urge to run the domain five more times when I have extra stamina. I still feel that brief pulse of hope when a gold protocore drops. But I have learned to recognize that feeling for what it is: a trap, a beautiful trap, but a trap nonetheless. I take a breath. I count my inventory. I remind myself of the 296 pieces I trashed and how freeing it felt.

Then I go do something else. Anything else. Because the game has more to offer than protocore domains, and I almost forgot that for a while. I am not going to forget again.

All numbers based on personal farming records. Your results will vary. If you are struggling with compulsive behavior around game systems, consider taking a break or speaking with someone. Games should be fun. If they are not, something needs to change.
