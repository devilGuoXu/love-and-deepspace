AMV & Video Scene Overview

The Love and Deepspace fan video community is one of the most dynamic creative spaces in the fandom, spanning multiple platforms, languages, and editing styles. Whether you are crafting an emotional character tribute or a fast-paced meme edit, understanding the landscape helps you find your audience and your creative voice.

Where Fan Videos Thrive. Three platforms dominate the Love and Deepspace video scene. YouTube is the home of long-form content — detailed analysis videos, extended AMVs, gameplay guides, and lore essays. Its search-driven discovery means well-optimized videos can attract viewers for months or years after upload. TikTok powers short-form discovery, with clips under 60 seconds that lean heavily on trending audio, quick transitions, and viral hooks. Bilibili is the primary platform for the Chinese fan community, hosting both short and long-form content with a culture of danmaku (real-time scrolling comments) that creates a uniquely interactive viewing experience. Editors who cross-post across platforms see the most cumulative growth.

Video Styles at a Glance. The community recognizes several major video genres. AMVs (Anime Music Videos) — character edits set to music, ranging from tightly beat-synced action montages to slow, emotional character studies. The best AMVs treat the song and footage as equal partners, using lyrical cues to guide visual choices. Compilations — collections of funny moments, emotional scenes, or gameplay highlights organized around a theme. These are the most accessible entry point for new editors since they require less narrative construction. Theory Videos — narrated analysis pieces that examine lore, character relationships, or narrative foreshadowing, supported by evidence clips from the game. These reward strong scripting and research more than flashy editing. Gameplay Edits — combat montages, build showcases, and boss kill highlights that emphasize mechanical skill and game knowledge. These thrive on clean footage and informative overlays.

Tools & Software

You do not need expensive software to create compelling Love and Deepspace fan videos. The best tool is the one you can start using today. Here is a breakdown of what is available at every budget level and skill stage.

CapCut
Free mobile/desktop editor with intuitive timeline, built-in effects, and direct TikTok integration. Best for beginners and short-form content. Limited multi-track support.
DaVinci Resolve
Professional-grade free editor with advanced color grading, Fusion compositing, and Fairlight audio tools. Steep learning curve but unmatched capability at zero cost.
Adobe Premiere Pro
Industry-standard paid editor. Robust multi-track editing, extensive plugin ecosystem, seamless integration with After Effects. Subscription required ($20+/month).
After Effects
Paid motion graphics and compositing tool. Ideal for animated text overlays, visual effects, and advanced transitions. Best paired with Premiere Pro.

Screen Recording. OBS Studio is the gold standard for recording gameplay footage — it is free, open-source, and capable of high-quality capture at any resolution and framerate. Key settings: record at 60fps in 1080p or 1440p, use CBR encoding at 30-50 Mbps bitrate, and record audio on separate tracks for post-production flexibility. For mobile game capture, iOS users can use the built-in screen recorder, while Android users may need third-party apps like AZ Screen Recorder. Ensure your device can sustain recording without frame drops — test your setup before attempting a full recording session.

Audio Extraction & Management. Love and Deepspace's soundtrack is rich with material for fan edits. Use Audacity (free) to extract and edit audio from game recordings or downloaded tracks. For isolating specific sound effects or dialogue, tools like VocalRemover or UVR5 can split audio stems with reasonable accuracy. Always keep your game audio and music tracks on separate timeline tracks — this gives you full control over the final mix and prevents the original track from clashing with your chosen song.

Editing Techniques

Mastering a few core techniques will elevate your edits from simple clip compilations to polished fan works. These skills transfer across any editing software and apply to any video genre.

Beat Sync. The foundation of any AMV is matching visual cuts to the rhythm of the music. Start by placing markers on your timeline at each significant beat of the song (kick drums, snare hits, chord changes). Cut your footage so that each marker lands on a meaningful visual event — a character's head turn, an explosion, a close-up eye opening, a sword swing. The most satisfying beat syncs do not cut on every beat; they establish a rhythm and then deliberately break it for emotional emphasis. Study existing AMVs in the Love and Deepspace community to internalize how pacing works across different song tempos and moods.

Color Grading. Love and Deepspace has a distinctive visual identity: deep purples, warm golds, cool blues, and dramatic contrast. Your color grade should honor this palette while supporting your video's mood. For Xavier edits, emphasize warm gold highlights and soft cyan shadows. For Zayne, lean into cool teals and desaturated midtones. Rafayel edits benefit from boosted saturation with crimson and ocean blue dominance. Sylus footage demands crushed blacks and selective purple/crimson highlights. Use the scopes in your editing software (waveform, vectorscope) to ensure your grade is technically sound before trusting your eyes — consistent exposure and color across all your clips is the mark of a professional edit.

Transitions. The rule of transitions in fan editing: less is more. A hard cut is almost always the right choice unless you have a specific reason to use something else. When you do use transitions, make them mean something. A quick zoom emphasizes a dramatic reveal. A whip pan (blurred horizontal movement) conveys energy between action clips. A light leak transition suits romantic or dreamy sequences. A fade to black signals the end of a section. Avoid the generic transitions built into consumer editing software — they immediately mark your edit as amateur. Study how official trailers and memory card teasers handle transitions; the game's own promotional material is your best reference for what looks polished.

Text Overlays. Text in fan videos serves three functions: displaying song lyrics for emotional resonance, showing character quotes for thematic emphasis, and providing informational overlays for guide and theory content. For lyrics and quotes, use a clean sans-serif font at a readable size,position it in the lower third or center frame depending on the composition of the footage beneath it. Add a subtle drop shadow or semi-transparent background to ensure readability against bright or busy footage. For informational text (guide steps, stat numbers, lore facts), use bold fonts with high contrast colors — your accent purple (#9c27b0) on a dark background works well. Animate text entrance and exit to match the video's rhythm, but keep animation simple: fade in/out, slide, or typewriter effects are sufficient.

Content Types Deep Dive

Each video genre has its own production requirements, audience expectations, and creative opportunities. Understanding these differences helps you choose the right format for your idea and skill level.

Character Edits — Focused Operative Studies
The most popular single genre in the community. A character edit selects moments from one operative's story and arranges them to music that reflects their personality and emotional arc. Xavier edits pair best with wistful, orchestral, or piano-led tracks that match his melancholic devotion. Zayne edits benefit from songs with building emotional intensity — tracks that start restrained and crescendo into vulnerability. Rafayel's passionate nature calls for dramatic, dynamic music with strong highs and lows. Sylus edits demand weighty, atmospheric tracks with bass presence and tension. The best character edits do not just compile pretty shots — they trace an emotional journey through the operative's key narrative beats, giving viewers who know the story a sense of re-experiencing it.

Ship Edits — Operative Pairing Montages
Ship edits focus on the relationship between the Hunter and a specific operative (or, less commonly, between two operatives). These videos draw from romantic Tender Moments, wedding memory cards, and emotional main story interactions. The editorial challenge is creating a coherent romantic narrative from non-contiguous scenes — you are building a relationship arc from scattered moments. The most effective ship edits establish a theme (first meeting, growing trust, sacrifice, reunion) and select footage that advances that theme. Choose music that reflects the specific dynamic: playful and bright for early Rafayel interactions, tender and vulnerable for Zayne's emotional breakthroughs, epic and devoted for Xavier's cosmic romance, intense and protective for Sylus's dangerous devotion.

Theory Videos — Narration + Evidence Structure
Theory and analysis videos are the most intellectually demanding genre. They require a clear thesis (what argument are you making?), evidence gathering (screenshots, clips, and text citations from the game), and structured narration that guides the viewer through your reasoning. The standard structure: hook with an intriguing question or bold claim, present your evidence in logical order (chronological or thematic), address counterarguments to show thoroughness, and conclude with implications or predictions. Use text overlays to highlight key evidence and on-screen annotations to direct attention to specific visual details. The best theory videos feel like a detective walking the viewer through a case — the evidence should lead the audience to your conclusion before you state it.
AnalysisLoreNarrated
Meme Edits — Trending Audio + Funny Moments
Meme edits are the fastest-growing video genre in the Love and Deepspace community, driven by TikTok and YouTube Shorts. The formula is simple: trending audio + well-timed game footage = shareable content. Success depends on recognizing which game moments have meme potential: characters reacting in extreme or unexpected ways, dialogue that can be recontextualized, visual gags from glitches or physics, and expressive character animations that match specific audio cues. Meme edits require less technical polish but sharper comedic timing than other genres. The best meme editors watch trends actively and create within 24-48 hours of a trend emerging. Speed matters more than production value in this space.

Sharing & Community

Creating the video is only half the process. Sharing it strategically determines whether it reaches its intended audience and starts building your reputation as a creator.

Best Platforms Per Content Type. Long-form content — AMVs over 2 minutes, theory videos, and guide content — belongs on YouTube, where search-based discovery and watch time metrics reward depth. Short-form content — meme edits, 30-second AMV previews, quick tips — thrives on TikTok and YouTube Shorts, where algorithmic discovery depends on immediate engagement. Bilibili is essential for reaching the Chinese fan community; if your content is in English, consider adding Chinese subtitles (machine translation with manual cleanup is acceptable for reach) to expand your audience significantly. Cross-platform strategy: create long-form content for YouTube, then extract highlight clips as Shorts/TikToks that drive viewers to the full video.

Hashtag Strategy. Effective tagging increases discoverability without looking spammy. Essential tags for every Love and Deepspace video: #LoveAndDeepspace #LaDS #LaDSFanEdit. Operative-specific tags for character-focused content: #Xavier #Zayne #Rafayel #Sylus. Genre tags to attract the right audience: #AMV #FanEdit #LaDSTheory. Platform-specific best practices: YouTube allows up to 500 characters in the description — use 5-10 relevant tags there. TikTok uses hashtags in captions (3-5 maximum for clean presentation). Bilibili relies more on title keywords and category tags than hashtags. Research trending tags in your niche monthly — the community's tagging conventions evolve.

Thumbnail Design Tips. Your thumbnail is the most important marketing asset for any YouTube video. Follow these principles: use a single character face with a clear emotional expression (anger, sadness, surprise, joy) as the focal point, place the character off-center to leave room for text, use high-contrast colors from the game's palette (purple background with white or gold text tests well), keep text to 3-5 words maximum in a bold sans-serif font, and maintain consistent branding across your thumbnails so viewers recognize your content in their feed. A/B test thumbnail designs by posting previews on Twitter or Discord to see which version generates more interest before finalizing.

Collaboration with Other Creators. The Love and Deepspace video editor community is collaborative by nature. Reach out to editors whose work you admire — comment thoughtfully on their videos, DM them with specific compliments about their techniques, and propose collaboration ideas that benefit both parties. Common collaboration formats: co-edited videos (two editors working on the same AMV), swap edits (each editor creates an edit using the other's footage or concept), and shoutout exchanges (feature each other's work in end screens or community posts). Cross-promotion with non-video creators is also powerful — an editor can create a video featuring a cosplayer's photos or an artist's illustrations, giving both creators exposure to each other's audiences.

Community Challenges and Events. Participating in community-organized events is one of the fastest ways to gain visibility and build relationships. Common event types: themed AMV contests (e.g., "best Xavier edit month"), prompt-based challenges (specific song assigned, all participants edit to it), collaboration projects (multi-editor AMV where each participant edits a section), and seasonal events (anniversary celebrations, holiday-themed edits). Follow the official Love and Deepspace Discord and the r/LoveAndDeepspace subreddit for event announcements. Even if you do not win, participating puts your work in front of judges and other participants who are the most engaged members of the community.

Tip: The single most important habit for growth as a video editor is watching your own work critically after posting. Re-watch your edit one week later with fresh eyes. What would you change? What pacing choices feel off? What transitions feel unnecessary? Take notes on every video you publish, and apply those lessons to your next project. The editors who improve fastest are not the most talented — they are the most honest with themselves about what works and what does not. Share your process and growth openly with the community; editors who document their learning journey build deeper audience connections than those who only post finished products.

Summary: Creating Love and Deepspace fan videos is a rewarding craft that combines technical editing skills with creative storytelling. Start with accessible tools like CapCut or DaVinci Resolve, develop core techniques including beat sync and color grading that honor the game's visual identity, and choose a content type — character edit, ship edit, theory video, or meme edit — that matches your interests and skills. Share strategically across YouTube, TikTok, and Bilibili using platform-specific optimization, and engage with the creator community through collaboration and event participation. Every great editor started with a single cut. Make yours today.
