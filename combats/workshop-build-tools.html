Why Templates Matter

Why Templates Matter: When you ask "is my Xavier build good?" without sharing your stats, nobody can help you. A standardized build template makes it easy for others to evaluate your build and give specific advice. Use the template below whenever you ask for build feedback on community platforms.

Build evaluation is a skill that requires complete information. Without knowing your ATK, CRIT Rate, CRIT DMG, ER, memory sets, and content goals, any advice is guesswork. The template below captures all essential information in a format that experienced players can evaluate in 10 seconds. Using it shows that you respect the community's time and are serious about improving.

Build Share Template

Copy and paste this template when asking for build feedback. Fill in your actual numbers.

BUILD SHARE TEMPLATE
Operative: [Name] — [Level/Rank]
Memory Set: [4pc Name / 2+2 Sets]
Memory Levels: Slot 1: 60 — Slot 2: 50 — Slot 3: 50 — Slot 4: 50 — Slot 5: 50 — Slot 6: 40
Protocore Main Stats: Slot 1: ATK% +12 — Slot 2: ER% +12 — Slot 3: ATK% +15 — Slot 4: CRIT Rate +15 — Slot 5: CRIT DMG +12 — Slot 6: ATK% +9
Final Stats: ATK: [number] | CRIT Rate: [%] | CRIT DMG: [%] | ER: [%]
Content Goal: [Abyss Floor X / Co-op / Domain]
Issue: [What you feel is wrong]

Example: Xavier Lv60/R1 — 4pc Light Seeker — ATK 2,410 | CRIT 58% | CRIT DMG 132% | ER 118% — Struggling on Abyss Floor 10 boss phase. This template gives the community everything they need to diagnose your build in 10 seconds. The first thing they will check: your CRIT Rate (58% is below the 60% floor 10 target) and your ER (118% is below the 120% DPS minimum). Two easy fixes.

Recommended Theorycrafting Tools

Three tools will dramatically improve your ability to evaluate and optimize builds.

TOOL 1 — PERSONAL SPREADSHEET
Keep a personal build tracker. Columns: Operative, Memory Set, Memory Levels, Protocore Main Stats, Final Stats (ATK/CRIT/DMG/ER), Content Cleared. Update this sheet whenever you change a protocore or level a memory. A personal tracker reveals your weak spots over time. For example, if all your operatives have low CRIT DMG, you know to prioritize CRIT DMG protocores across the board.
TOOL 2 — COMMUNITY BUILD DATABASE
Several community-maintained sheets track optimal builds for each operative. These are updated every patch. Search for "Deepspace [Operative Name] Build Sheet" to find the latest version. Cross-reference 2–3 sources before trusting a build — community consensus is more reliable than any individual guide. Check the last updated date — a build from 3 patches ago may be outdated.
TOOL 3 — SCREENSHOT JOURNAL
Take screenshots of your protocore substats before enhancing. If a protocore rolls poorly, you can review the before/after and learn which protocores to skip enhancing in the future. This is the best way to improve your protocore evaluation skills. After 20–30 documented enhances, you will instinctively know which protocores are worth the dust investment.

Pro Tip: When sharing screenshots on Discord/Reddit, use the "hide UID" option in settings. Your account UID is visible in screenshots and can be used to look up your account. Privacy first — hide your UID, hide your friend list, hide your purchase history. Share only what is necessary for build evaluation. Some malicious actors can use your UID to target your account.

How to Get Build Feedback

1
Use the template above
Post your build in the format shown. Include your content goal. The more specific you are about your issue, the better the advice you will receive.
2
Share specific screenshots
Screenshot your stat screen (press C in combat) and your protocore enhancement screens (all 6 slots). Do not ask "is this good?" without screenshots. Text alone misses subtle details like roll quality.
3
State your budget
Are you F2P, dolphin, or whale? A build that is good for F2P may be bad for a whale (who has access to better protocores). Budget determines the advice you need. An F2P player should not be told to "get more CRIT DMG from limited memories" — that advice is useless without the spending capacity.
4
Listen to consensus
If 3 people tell you your CRIT rate is too low, it is too low. Do not argue with feedback you asked for. Thank the responders and try their suggestions before pushing back.
5
Iterate and follow up
Post your updated build after making changes. Community members who helped you will appreciate seeing the improvement and will be more likely to help you again in the future. A simple "updated based on your advice, here are the new stats" goes a long way.

Build Feedback Checklist

Before asking for feedback, check these common issues yourself. If you identify the issue yourself, you do not need to ask — and you learn faster.

☐ Is your main stat correct?
Xavier Slot 4 should be CRIT Rate, not HP%. Each slot has an optimal main stat — check the community database if unsure.
☐ Are set bonuses active?
3 memories required for 4pc, 2 for 2pc. Count your equipped memories — common mistake is having only 2 of a 4pc set.
☐ Are all memories level 40+?
A level 20 memory is missing 30%+ of its stat potential. Level all 6 slots to at least 40 before optimizing substats.
☐ Is CRIT Rate above 50%?
For DPS operatives, 50% is the minimum viable. If below 50%, farm CRIT Rate protocores before anything else.
☐ Is ER above 120% (DPS) / 150% (support)?
Energy Recharge is often the forgotten stat. Check your ER — if it is below the threshold, that explains rotation issues.

Before posting: Run through this checklist. If you find and fix 2–3 issues, your build will already be noticeably stronger. The checklist catches 80% of common build problems. Most feedback requests are answered with "fix your CRIT Rate" or "level your memories" — you can fix these before asking.

Community Etiquette

Getting build feedback is a privilege, not a right. Community members volunteer their time to help you. Respect that.

✓ DO
  • Use the template — make it easy for others to help
  • Say thank you after receiving advice
  • Follow up with results after making changes
  • Help others when you learn enough to do so
  • Upvote/thank posts that helped you
✗ DON'T
  • Don't argue with feedback you asked for
  • Don't ask "is this good?" without any context
  • Don't post the same question in multiple channels
  • Don't demand instant answers — people have lives
  • Don't share your UID or personal account info

Summary: Use the build template when asking for feedback — include operative, set, levels, main stats, final stats, and goal. Keep a personal spreadsheet to track your builds. Screenshot protocores before enhancing to learn from roll patterns. Hide your UID in screenshots. Check the 5 common issues before asking (main stat, set bonus, levels, CRIT Rate, ER). Community feedback is a gift — respond to it with gratitude, not defensiveness. Pay it forward when you can.
