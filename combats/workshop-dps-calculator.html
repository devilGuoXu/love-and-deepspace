Why Use a DPS Calculator

Why Calculate? A DPS calculator converts your raw stats (ATK, CRIT Rate, CRIT DMG, ER, DMG Bonus) into a single number: theoretical DPS. This lets you compare builds that have different stat distributions. Without a calculator, you cannot know if 10% more CRIT DMG is better than 5% more ATK. The calculator removes guesswork from build optimization.

Most players evaluate their builds by feel — "this build feels stronger" or "I cleared 5 seconds faster." Feel is unreliable. A 5% DPS improvement is barely noticeable in gameplay but adds up to significant time savings across multiple Abyss floors. The calculator quantifies improvements that your perception cannot detect. Players who use calculators consistently outperform players who build by feel, especially in endgame content where optimization matters most.

The calculator is not perfect — it cannot account for player skill, boss movement patterns, or team synergy buffs. But it is 90% accurate for single-target DPS scenarios. For multi-target and AoE, the accuracy drops to 70-80% depending on enemy density. Use the calculator as a guide, not gospel. The best builds are calculator-optimized and then playtested to confirm the theoretical numbers match real performance.

When NOT to trust the calculator: (1) AoE-heavy floors where enemy grouping matters more than raw DPS. (2) Boss fights with invulnerability phases — the calculator assumes 100% uptime. (3) Co-op mode where team buffs affect your damage in ways the calculator cannot predict. (4) Fights where positioning and dodge timing are more important than stat optimization. Use the calculator for Abyss floors 1-8 and domain farming — these are pure stat checks.

How the Calculator Works

The DPS calculator uses this formula to compute your damage:

DPS FORMULA
Average Hit = ATK × (1 + CRIT Rate × CRIT DMG) × (1 + DMG Bonus) × Skill Multiplier × Element Multiplier
EFFECTIVE DPS
Effective DPS = Average Hit × Hits per Second × (1 + ER Bonus) × (1 + Set Bonus)

The calculator accounts for: base ATK, bonus ATK from protocores, CRIT Rate, CRIT DMG, elemental damage bonuses, skill-specific multipliers, set bonuses, and energy recharge for ultimate uptime. It assumes perfect rotation execution (no missed skill inputs, no wasted energy). The calculator does NOT account for: enemy DEF and RES stats (these are assumed constant), reaction damage, healing/shielding output, or downtime from dodging.

Input Parameters Guide

Accurate input = accurate output. Here is how to fill each field correctly.

Character LevelYour actual character level (1-60). Level affects base ATK and scaling.
Character RankR0 (base), R1, R2, R3. Rank increases skill multipliers.
Memory SetSelect your equipped set: 4pc Light Seeker, 4pc Frostguard, 2+2 combinations, etc.
Memory LevelsLevel of each memory slot (1-60). Slot 1 (ATK memory) has the biggest impact.
Protocore Main StatsSlot 1-6 main stats. Critical: Slot 4 = CRIT Rate or CRIT DMG. Slot 6 = ATK% or ER.
Protocore SubstatsTotal substat values across all 6 slots. Add them up: total ATK+, total CRIT Rate+, etc.
Skill LevelsCurrent skill level of basic attack, dodge counter, resonance skill, and ultimate.

Input Accuracy Tip: Do not estimate your protocore substats. Open your character sheet, read the actual numbers, and type them exactly. A 0.5% difference in CRIT Rate changes the DPS output by ~1%. Screenshot your stat screen and read the numbers from the screenshot to avoid transcription errors. The calculator is only as accurate as the input — garbage in, garbage out.

Average Hit is useful for comparing basic attack damage but does not reflect total combat performance. DPS is the most important number — it reflects sustained damage output over your full rotation. Rotation Duration tells you how long your burst window lasts. ER Efficiency over 1.0 means you generate more energy than you spend — your ultimate is always available on cooldown. Below 1.0 means you have downtime between ultimates.

OUTPUT INTERPRETATION
DPS above 8,000: Excellent. You are ready for Abyss floor 12.
DPS 6,000-8,000: Good. Ready for floors 8-10.
DPS 4,000-6,000: Average. Floors 5-7 content.
DPS below 4,000: Needs improvement. Focus on base stats and protocore levels.
ER Efficiency below 0.8: You need more Energy Recharge. Ultimate downtime is hurting your DPS.

Important caveat: DPS numbers vary significantly between characters. Xavier's DPS is naturally higher than Zayne's because Xavier is a DPS character and Zayne is a support. Compare DPS within the same character only. Do not compare Xavier's 8,000 DPS to Zayne's 5,000 DPS and conclude Zayne is weak — they have different roles. The calculator supports role-specific benchmarks for each character.

Comparing Builds

This is the calculator's most powerful feature. You can compare two builds side-by-side and see exactly which one performs better. The comparison reveals the marginal benefit of each stat.

BUILD A: HIGH CRIT RATE
65% CRIT Rate, 120% CRIT DMG, 2,200 ATK
Calculated DPS: 7,200. Consistency-focused build. You CRIT on 2 out of 3 hits. Lower damage ceiling but higher floor. Better for content where you cannot restart (domain runs, co-op). Recommended for content where reliability matters more than peak damage.
BUILD B: HIGH CRIT DMG
45% CRIT Rate, 180% CRIT DMG, 2,200 ATK
Calculated DPS: 7,100. Gambler build. Higher peak damage but inconsistent. You CRIT on less than 1 in 2 hits. When you do not CRIT, the damage is noticeably lower. Better for content where you can restart (Abyss floors) — reset until you get good CRIT RNG.
BUILD C: BALANCED
55% CRIT Rate, 150% CRIT DMG, 2,200 ATK
Calculated DPS: 7,500 (highest). The balanced build outperforms both extremes. This is because the DPS formula multiplies CRIT Rate × CRIT DMG — the product is maximized when the two stats are balanced. Rule of thumb: CRIT DMG should be ~2.5x CRIT Rate for optimal DPS.

How to perform a comparison: Save Build A as your baseline. Change one variable (e.g., swap Slot 4 from CRIT Rate to CRIT DMG). The calculator shows the DPS difference as a percentage. A 3% DPS increase from a protocore swap means that swap is worth approximately 1-2 weeks of domain farming. Prioritize changes that give the largest DPS increase per unit of investment.

Common Calculator Mistakes

Mistake 1: Forgetting set bonuses
The calculator has a toggle for set bonuses. If you have 4pc Light Seeker equipped, enable that passive. Without it, your calculated DPS will be 15-20% lower than reality. Double-check that your set bonus is active in the calculator.
Mistake 2: Wrong rotation selected
Different rotations have different DPS. Using the "optimal" rotation preset when your actual gameplay uses a different rotation will give misleading results. Select the rotation that matches how you actually play, not the theoretical best rotation.
Mistake 3: Ignoring ER
The calculator penalizes low ER by reducing ultimate uptime. If your ER is below 120%, the calculator shows significantly lower DPS than what you would get if you had 140% ER. Many players ignore ER and wonder why their DPS is low — check ER first.
Mistake 4: Comparing across characters
As mentioned earlier, Xavier DPS is not comparable to Zayne DPS. The calculator has character-specific presets. Always select the correct character preset before entering stats.
Mistake 5: Over-optimizing
A 2% DPS improvement is not worth 2 weeks of farming. The calculator makes small improvements visible, but not all improvements are worth pursuing. Aim for the "good enough" threshold — once your DPS is within 5% of the theoretical maximum, stop optimizing and play the game. The last 5% takes 80% of the effort.
✓ DO
  • Input exact stats from your character sheet
  • Enable correct set bonuses and passives
  • Select the rotation you actually use
  • Compare builds within the same character only
  • Stop optimizing at 95% of theoretical max
✗ DON'T
  • Don't estimate substats — type exact numbers
  • Don't forget ER in your calculations
  • Don't compare DPS across different characters
  • Don't chase 2% improvements for weeks
  • Don't trust the calculator for AoE content

Final Tip: The DPS calculator is a tool, not a goal. A 7,500 DPS build that you enjoy playing is better than an 8,000 DPS build that feels clunky. If a build performs well in the calculator but feels bad in gameplay, trust your feel. The calculator cannot measure comfort, consistency, or fun. Use it to inform decisions, not dictate them.
