The Hunter's Manifesto

Ten Principles for a Better Game — July 2026

I have been playing this game for long enough to have strong opinions about how it should be played. These are not rules. They are principles I have developed through trial and error, frustration and joy, loss and victory. Take what works for you. Leave the rest.

Principle I

The Game Is a Marathon, Not a Sprint

The most common mistake new players make is treating Love and Deepspace like a game that can be finished. It cannot be finished. It is designed to never be finished. The content resets. The banners rotate. The power ceiling rises. You will never reach a point where you have everything and there is nothing left to do. Accepting this is not defeat. It is wisdom. Play at a pace you can sustain for months, not days. The game will be here tomorrow. It will be here next year. You do not need to clear everything this week.

Principle II

Pull for Love, Not for Meta

Meta changes. Love does not. I have seen too many players pull for an operative because the tier list said they were S-tier, only to regret it when the next patch shifted the meta. I have never seen anyone regret pulling for a character they genuinely love. The enjoyment you get from using a character you care about far outweighs the marginal performance difference between S-tier and A-tier. Build the characters who make you smile when they appear on screen. The meta will catch up to your feelings eventually.

Principle III

Your Account, Your Rules

The community has strong opinions about how you should play. Spend only on limited banners. Never enhance flat stats. Always farm the highest domain level. Some of this advice is good. Some of it is context-dependent. Some of it is just people repeating what they heard without understanding why. You are the only person who knows your account, your schedule, your budget, and your goals. Make your own decisions. If you want to pull on the standard banner because you need an SSR now, do it. If you want to enhance a protocore with flat ATK because it is your only option, do it. Your account will survive suboptimal decisions. It will not survive you quitting because you were following someone else's rules.

Principle IV

Rest Is Part of the Rotation

I once played for 47 consecutive days without missing a single daily login. I was proud of my streak. I was also exhausted, irritable, and not having fun. I took a break. When I came back, my dodge rate was higher, my rotation was cleaner, and my enjoyment was noticeably improved. Fatigue has a measurable negative impact on gameplay performance. Taking a day off is not wasted progress. It is maintenance. The game will be there when you get back. The FOMO is manufactured. Do not let it control you.

Principle V

Good Enough Is Better Than Perfect

The perfect protocore does not exist. If it did, you would not get it. If you got it, the next patch would make it obsolete. The pursuit of perfect gear is a trap that will consume your stamina, your time, and your enjoyment. Settle for good enough. A protocore with two usable substats that rolled into the right stats is a success. A memory at level 70 instead of 80 is fine if it clears the content you need it to clear. The difference between good and perfect is usually less than 10%. The difference between good and nothing is 100%. Use what you have. Improve incrementally. Stop chasing the dragon.

Principle VI

Acknowledge the Design

The game is designed to make you want to spend money. The 50-50, the limited banners, the FOMO timers, the stamina gates. These are not accidental features. They are deliberate psychological mechanisms. Recognizing them does not make you immune to them, but it helps. When you lose a 50-50 and feel the urge to spend, pause and say to yourself: I am feeling a designed response. The game wants me to spend right now. That awareness creates a gap between impulse and action. In that gap, you have a choice. The game wants you to make that choice quickly. Take your time.

Principle VII

The Community Is a Tool, Not a Judge

The Love and Deepspace community has incredible resources. Theorycrafting, fan content, guides, and genuine friendships. I have met people through this community who I now talk to every day. But the community also has a dark side. Tribalism, gatekeeping, spending shaming, and purity testing. Take what is useful from the community and leave the rest. You do not have to engage with every argument. You do not have to defend your preferences. You do not have to justify your spending or your lack of spending. The community is there to help you enjoy the game more. If it is doing the opposite, disconnect and play solo for a while.

Principle VIII

Spending Is a Choice, Not a Status

F2P players who act superior to spenders are wrong. Spenders who act superior to F2P players are also wrong. Spending is a financial choice that has no moral dimension. The game needs both to survive. F2P players provide the playerbase. Spenders provide the revenue. Neither group is better. The only wrong financial decision is spending more than you can afford. If you can afford to spend and it makes you happy, spend. If you cannot or do not want to, do not. Your value as a community member is not determined by your account’s power level or your banner pull history.

Principle IX

Learn the Systems, Then Trust Yourself

The most powerful thing you can do for your account is understand how the game works. Read the ability descriptions. Learn how damage is calculated. Understand Stellactrum matching and resonance chains. The knowledge you gain from understanding the systems is permanent. Gear gets replaced. Meta shifts. But understanding stays with you forever. Once you understand the systems, you can make your own decisions. You do not need to ask Reddit what to farm. You will know. Trust that knowledge. It is more reliable than any tier list.

Principle X

Know When to Walk Away

This is the hardest principle and the most important. There will come a day when the game is not fun anymore. Maybe it is burnout. Maybe it is frustration with the direction of the game. Maybe you just found something else you enjoy more. When that day comes, walk away. No guilt. No farewell post. No deleting your account in a fit of rage. Just stop logging in. The game will be here if you want to come back. And if you do not come back, that is okay too. Games are supposed to make your life better. The moment they stop doing that, you have permission to leave. Thank the game for the good times and move on. That is not quitting. That is growing.

These are my principles. They are not universal. They are mine, hard-earned through a year of play, hundreds of dollars spent and not spent, countless protocore runs, and more emotional investment in fictional characters than I am entirely comfortable admitting. If they help you, use them. If they do not, write your own. The important thing is not which principles you follow. It is that you have principles at all. Play with intention. Not on autopilot.

Signed by Kaelen, July 2026. These principles are subject to revision as new experience demands.
