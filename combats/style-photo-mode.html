STYLE GUIDE — PHOTO

Photo Mode Advanced Techniques

Deepspace has one of the best photo modes in any mobile game. Master the camera, lighting, and pose system to create professional-quality character portraits.

ENTRY_#STY_022026.07.02PHOTO

Core Tools: Photo mode gives you: Camera position/movement (free 3D), FOV slider (20-120), lighting (3-point system), filters (15+), pose library (per-character), expression controls, prop placement, and background selection. Most players use 10% of these tools. Understanding all of them separates snapshots from art.

Lighting: The 3-Point System

Key Light (Main): The primary light source. Position it 45 degrees above and 45 degrees to the side of the character's face. This creates natural shadows that define facial features. Intensity: 70-90%. Fill Light (Secondary): Opposite the key light, at a lower intensity (30-50%). This fills in shadows created by the key light. Without fill light, half the face is in deep shadow. Rim Light (Back): Behind the character, slightly above. Intensity: 40-60%. This creates a glowing edge that separates the character from the background. Essential for dark backgrounds. Common mistake: Using only one light source. Two lights minimum, three lights ideal. The default auto-lighting is acceptable but never great — manually adjust all three lights.

Camera & Composition

Rule of Thirds: Enable the grid overlay. Place the character's eyes on the upper third line. This is the most visually pleasing composition for portraits. FOV Settings: Portrait: 40-50mm equivalent (flattering facial proportions). Full body: 24-35mm (slight distortion makes legs look longer). Environmental: 20mm (dramatic perspective). Avoid 60mm+ for portraits — facial distortion becomes unflattering. Camera Height: Eye level for neutral portraits. Slightly below eye level (shooting up) for heroic/majestic poses. Slightly above eye level for vulnerable/intimate poses. Depth of Field: Use aperture mode (f/1.4-f/2.8) to blur the background. This creates professional-looking bokeh. Maximum blur at f/1.4. Use f/5.6+ for group shots where all characters must be in focus.

Pose & Expression

Each character has 15-20 unique poses. Poses are grouped by mood: Casual, Battle, Elegant, Playful. Expression pairing: Match the expression to the pose mood. Smiling + Casual = natural. Smiling + Battle = jarring (unless intentional). Serious + Elegant = sophisticated. Advanced trick: Use the "freeze frame" feature during combat or cutscenes — capture a pose that is not available in the standard pose library. Time your screenshot during a skill animation for unique action shots. Prop placement: Use the prop system to add foreground elements (flowers, furniture, etc.). A foreground prop with shallow DOF creates depth. Do not place props in the center of the frame — place them at the edges for a natural framing effect.

Pro Tip: The best photo backgrounds are NOT in the background selection menu. The best backgrounds are found in the game world — explore the home base, event maps, and Abyss arenas. Use the "hide UI" feature to capture screenshots during exploration. Event-exclusive maps often have unique lighting and architecture that cannot be replicated in the standard photo studio.

Post-Processing Tips

After taking your screenshot, you can apply filters in photo mode. Filter recommendations: "Warm Glow" for sunset scenes and romantic portraits. "Cool Tone" for battle shots and Abyss backgrounds. "Monochrome" for dramatic character studies. "Vignette" (enable at 20-30%) for focus on the character's face. Contrast: Increase by 10-20% for more dramatic images. Saturation: Do not exceed +10% — oversaturated images look amateur. Sharpness: +5-10% for character close-ups. Zero for environmental shots.

Summary: 3-point lighting (key/fill/rim). Rule of thirds composition. 40-50mm FOV for portraits. f/1.4 for single character bokeh. Match pose to expression. Use game world as background, not the studio. Freeze frame during combat for unique poses. Minimal post-processing (contrast +10%, saturation +5% max).
