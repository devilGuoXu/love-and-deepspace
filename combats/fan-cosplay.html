Cosplay Guide & Resources

Everything you need to cosplay your favorite Love and Deepspace operative — costume sourcing, wig styling, prop construction, and community resources for cosplayers of all skill levels.

Character Design Overview

Each operative in Love and Deepspace has a distinct visual identity that translates well to cosplay. Xavier's designs feature light colors (whites, pale blues, gold accents) with cape-like outerwear and celestial motifs — his "Light Seeker" outfit is the most popular cosplay choice due to its striking silhouette and recognizable star patterns. Zayne's aesthetic is clinical and elegant (dark blues, silver, white coats) with medical/research themes — his standard Hunter uniform with the white coat overlay is a beginner-friendly choice because it is essentially modern formalwear with fantasy accents.

Rafayel's costumes are the most elaborate — flowing fabrics, ocean-inspired color gradients (teal to deep blue), art-themed accessories, and asymmetrical cuts. His "Inferno Waltz" outfit is the most complex to cosplay but also the most rewarding, with its layered sleeves, intricate embroidery, and dramatic cape. Sylus's designs are dark and commanding (black, crimson, gold) with Onychnus military influences — structured jackets, high collars, metal accessories, and his signature eyepatch. Sylus's outfits are the most beginner-friendly due to their structured, commercial-pattern-compatible construction. Each operative also has casual/modern outfits (beachwear, street style, formal wear) that are simple to cosplay and good for conventions.

Costume Sourcing

Cosplay costumes can be sourced through three main channels: Commission (best quality, most expensive — $300-600 for a full LADS cosplay from a skilled costume maker), Pre-made (medium quality, medium price — $150-300 from online cosplay stores like Miccostumes, RoleCosplay, or Uwowo), and DIY (variable quality, cheapest — $30-150 using thrifted base items + modifications). For beginners, pre-made costumes from reputable sellers are recommended — they arrive ready-to-wear with minimal adjustments needed. For experienced cosplayers, DIY allows for screen-accurate details that pre-made costumes often miss (incorrect embroidery, wrong fabric type, simplified construction).

When commissioning or ordering: provide the reference image with clear views of front, back, and side. Note specific details: collar shape, sleeve length and construction, belt/pocket placement, embroidery patterns, fabric texture (LADS art often suggests specific materials like brocade, satin, or matte cotton). For pre-made orders, check measurements carefully — Chinese sizing runs smaller than Western sizing. Order 1-2 sizes up from your usual size and plan for tailoring adjustments. Key measurements for LADS costumes: chest (most important for fitted jackets like Sylus's), waist (belted outfits like Xavier's), shoulder width (structured coats like Zayne's), and arm length (long sleeves on Rafayel's designs).

Fabric recommendations: Xavier: lightweight brocade or cotton-satin blend for his flowy outer garments. Avoid heavy fabrics — his silhouette depends on movement. Zayne: cotton-poplin for the coat (stiff, professional) and lightweight knit for underlayers. Rafayel: silk charmeuse or polyester charmeuse for the flowing, iridescent look. Sylus: faux leather or heavyweight twill for structured jackets, with matte finish (not shiny — Sylus's outfits are dark but not glossy). For all operatives: match the reference image's fabric drape, not just the color. Fabric choice makes the difference between a costume that looks good and one that looks screen-accurate.

Wig Styling Guides

Each operative has a distinctive hairstyle that is critical to recognition. Xavier's hair is medium-length, swept back with volume at the crown and wispy layers. Use a light ash-brown wig with cool undertones. Style: backcomb the crown for volume, use strong-hold hairspray to create the swept-back shape, and thin the ends slightly for the wispy effect. Xavier's hair should look effortless but intentional — too stiff and it loses the character's relaxed elegance.

Zayne's hair is the most structured — neat, side-parted, with clean lines and minimal volume. Use a dark charcoal-brown wig. Style: the side part is the defining feature. Use a fine-tooth comb for precision, heat-set the part with a flat iron, and seal with hairspray. Zayne's hairstyle should look clinical and perfect — any flyaways break the character's precise aesthetic. Rafayel's hair is long and layered, reaching past the shoulders with a casual, artistic flow. Use a teal-to-blue gradient wig (professionally dyed or pre-colored). Style: loose waves created with foam rollers (not heat — heat damages gradient wigs). The key is natural movement — Rafayel's hair should sway and flow like water.

Sylus's hair is the most dramatic — silver-white, slicked back with a defined widow's peak and long tail. Use a white or silver-gray wig (white with silver highlights is most accurate). Style: slick the front and crown back with strong-hold gel or wax, leaving the back tail loose. The widow's peak requires precise cutting — mark the peak point at the center of the forehead and cut the lace front accordingly. Sylus's hair is the most challenging to style but the most recognizable — a well-styled Sylus wig is instantly identifiable even from a distance.

Wig care: LADS cosplay wigs require maintenance between wears. Store wigs on a wig head (not folded) to preserve the styled shape. For gradient wigs (Rafayel), avoid heat styling — use steam or foam rollers instead. For heat-styled wigs (Zayne, Sylus), use heat-protectant spray and keep the temperature below 180°C (350°F). Carry a mini hairspray and comb for touch-ups at conventions. A wig that looks perfect at 9 AM can look disheveled by 3 PM — plan for maintenance. Pack a wig kit: small spray bottle (water + fabric softener for detangling), fine-tooth comb, hairspray, bobby pins, and extra wefts for emergency repairs.

Prop Construction

Love and Deepspace characters carry distinctive props that complete the cosplay. Xavier's celestial staff or orb (varies by outfit) — the Light Seeker staff is a popular build: a 4-foot staff with a translucent orb at the top. Construct from PVC pipe (staff body), a clear acrylic ball (orb), and LED strip lighting (for the glow effect). Paint the staff with metallic silver and gold, and use frosted spray on the orb for a mystical diffusion effect. Total build cost: $30-50, difficulty: moderate.

Zayne's props include medical equipment and research tools depending on the outfit. The most recognizable is his Frost-element crystal (a palm-sized glowing crystal). Construct from clear resin cast in a crystal mold, with a small battery-powered LED inside. For convention safety compliance: use a clear or translucent resin with no sharp edges. Difficulty: moderate-advanced (resin casting requires ventilation and safety equipment). Rafayel's art-themed props (paintbrush, palette, sketchbook) are the easiest — a large artist's brush (modified with gold paint and ocean-themed embellishments), a wooden palette with resin "paint" dots, and a leather-bound sketchbook. These are beginner-friendly and largely thriftable.

Sylus requires the most iconic prop: his Onychnus blade or his eyepatch. The eyepatch is a beginner project (faux leather, elastic band, gold emblem) but must be screen-accurate — the shape, size, and emblem placement are critical. The Onychnus blade is an advanced build: a 3-foot curved dagger constructed from EVA foam or 3D-printed PLA, with silver and black paint, and LED effects along the blade. Con-safe rules: if the blade is longer than 2 feet, many conventions require it to be peace-bonded (tied to its sheath) or checked at the weapon desk. Check your convention's weapon policy before building sharp or long props. Nerf or foam is always preferred for conventions.

Convention prop safety: Most conventions have strict prop policies. General rules: no metal blades (real or replica), no functional projectile weapons, props over 6 feet must be flagged at entry, prop guns require orange tips, and props must not obstruct walkways. Check your convention's specific policy 2 weeks before the event. The safest approach: build all props from EVA foam, foam board, or 3D-printed PLA, and leave the "weapon" look clearly artificial (paint with visible brush strokes, use bright colors on edges). A clearly fake prop is less likely to cause alarm than a hyper-realistic one. When in doubt, leave the prop at home — a cosplay is still recognizable without weapons.

Makeup & Body Art

LADS character makeup focuses on enhancing facial features to match the character's art style. The game's character art uses defined jawlines, sharp cheekbones, and expressive eyes. For masculine-presenting cosplayers: contour is the most important technique. Define the jawline (square for Sylus, angular for Zayne, softer for Xavier and Rafayel), sharpen the cheekbones, and narrow the nose bridge. Use matte products only — the LADS art style avoids shimmer on male characters. For feminine-presenting cosplayers cosplaying male characters: focus on brow blocking and redrawing (create stronger, straighter brows) and jaw contouring.

Eye makeup is critical for character recognition. Xavier: soft browns and golds on the eyes, slightly elongated outer corners, natural brows. Zayne: cool grays and silvers, precise liner (sharp outer point), straight brows with minimal arch. Rafayel: the most eye makeup — teal and purple accents, winged liner, and slightly rounded eyes to match his expressive art. Sylus: the most dramatic — dark smoky eye with sharp liner, heavy lower lash definition, and angled brows to match his intense expression. For all characters: use false lashes for definition (natural length for Xavier/Zayne, longer for Rafayel/Sylus). Eye contacts are optional but recommended for complete transformation — use character-appropriate colors (Xavier: warm brown or amber, Zayne: cool gray or silver-gray, Rafayel: turquoise or aqua blue, Sylus: dark red or crimson).

Contact lens safety: If using colored contact lenses, buy from reputable sellers (Pinky Paradise, HoneyColor, or prescription lenses from your optometrist). Never buy contacts from unauthorized sellers (Amazon, eBay, TikTok shops) — they may be counterfeit and cause eye damage. Get a prescription even if you have perfect vision (contacts require a prescription by law in many countries). Insert contacts before makeup, remove them before removing makeup. Carry eye drops and a contact case for conventions (dry convention air can irritate eyes). Do not share contacts with other cosplayers — eye infections spread easily through shared lenses.

Budget Cosplay Tips

Cosplay does not have to be expensive. Budget-friendly approaches: thrift base items and modify them (a charcoal blazer for Zayne, a white dress shirt for Xavier, black dress pants for Sylus). Use fabric paint for details instead of embroidery (paint the Light Seeker star patterns, Frost Weaver snowflakes). Wigs from Amazon or eBay ($15-30) can be styled to look like premium wigs with proper care and products. For Rafayel's gradient wig, buy a white wig and use fabric dye for the teal-to-blue gradient (cheaper than pre-made gradient wigs).

Collaborate with other cosplayers in your area — splitting costume costs (sharing wig styling tools, pooling material orders for bulk discounts) makes everything cheaper. Join the LADS Cosplay Discord (linked in community resources) to find local cosplayers. Borrow props and accessories from other cosplayers — many are happy to lend items for specific conventions. The LADS cosplay community is particularly supportive of new cosplayers, with many experienced cosplayers offering free advice and occasional material donations. Start with a simpler outfit (casual wear, standard Hunter uniform) before attempting the elaborate combat outfits. Build your skills progressively — a simple cosplay executed well is more impressive than a complex cosplay executed poorly.

Cosplay Community & Events

The LADS cosplay community is active across multiple platforms. Instagram is the primary showcase platform — use hashtags #LoveAndDeepspaceCosplay, #LADSCosplay, and each operative's name + cosplay tag (e.g., #XavierCosplay). Please tag the cosplayer when sharing their photos. Twitter and TikTok (Reels/Shorts format) are growing fast — short transformation videos (makeup to full cosplay) perform well. The Reddit community r/LoveAndDeepspace has cosplay threads during convention seasons.

Annual events for LADS cosplayers include: Anime Expo (July, Los Angeles — largest LADS cosplay gathering), Comiket (August/December, Tokyo — massive gathering of LADS cosplayers), ChinaJoy (July/August, Shanghai — origin country LADS cosplay), and local anime conventions worldwide (most have at least a few LADS cosplayers). The LADS Cosplay Meetup is organized at each major convention through Discord — join the meetup to take group photos with other LADS cosplayers. Group cosplay photos (all 4 operatives + Hunter) are the most shared content in the community. If you attend a convention alone, join the meetup to complete the set.

Cosplay photography tips: LADS character art uses specific lighting and posing. Research character official art and memory card illustrations for pose inspiration. For Xavier: soft, ethereal lighting (golden hour or soft diffused studio light). Zayne: cool, clinical lighting (blue-tinted, sharp shadows). Rafayel: warm, dynamic lighting (sunset colors, backlighting for the water-gradient effect). Sylus: dramatic, high-contrast lighting (single key light, deep shadows, rim lighting). Posing: study the character's official art posture — Xavier stands with relaxed confidence, Zayne with clinical precision, Rafayel with artistic flair, Sylus with commanding dominance. A cosplay photo that captures the character's signature pose and lighting is immediately recognizable even without visible costume details.
