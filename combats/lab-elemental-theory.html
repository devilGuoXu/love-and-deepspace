Elemental Reaction & Stellactrum Theory

Stellactrum matching and elemental reactions are separate multiplicative damage layers. Understanding how they stack — and when they conflict — is the difference between a good team and a mathematically optimal one.

Stellactrum Matching

Key Concept: Stellactrum matching and elemental reactions are separate multiplicative damage layers. The stellactrum system gives a flat ATK bonus for matching colors, while elemental reactions provide conditional damage amplifiers. These layers multiply against each other, meaning a team that optimizes both will out-damage a team that optimizes only one.

The stellactrum system assigns each operative a primary color and a secondary color. When an operative's stellactrum matches the memory set bonus requirement, they receive a flat ATK bonus. The exact multiplier works as follows:

Same color match = +15% ATK bonus to that operative
Off-color = no bonus

A +15% ATK bonus at +80% total ATK represents an 8.3% total damage increase. At +120% total ATK, the same 15% bonus represents a 6.8% increase. Matching stellactrum is always valuable, but its relative value decreases as you stack more ATK% from other sources — a classic diminishing returns pattern.

ON-COLOR MATCH
+15% ATK. If base ATK = 1,800 and ATK% = +80%, total ATK = 3,240. With match: +15% → 1.95x multiplier → 3,510 ATK. Effective damage gain: +8.3%.
OFF-COLOR COST
Zero ATK bonus from stellactrum. To compensate, you need substats worth ~8% more damage — roughly 2 high CRIT rolls or 1.5 ATK% rolls. The opportunity cost is real but surmountable with better individual gear.

The opportunity cost equation: forcing an off-color match to activate a set bonus is worth it if the set bonus provides >8% total damage increase. Most 2-piece set bonuses provide 10–15% damage, making off-color matching viable when the set bonus is strong. However, if the set bonus provides less than 8% (some defensive sets), on-color matching with a weaker set bonus is mathematically superior.

Decision Rule: Calculate the set bonus damage multiplier × the stellactrum multiplier. Compare both options: Option A (on-color, weak set) vs Option B (off-color, strong set). Pick the higher product. Example: On-color with +0% set = 1.083 × 1.0 = 1.083. Off-color with +12% set = 1.0 × 1.12 = 1.12. Off-color wins by 3.4%. But if the set gives only 6%: 1.0 × 1.06 = 1.06 — on-color wins. Do the math before committing resources.

Elemental Resonance Formula

When two operatives with Light + Dark elements are on the same team, Elemental Resonance is triggered. This is the most impactful reaction in the game because it provides a team-wide damage multiplier with no stat condition — it works regardless of gear quality.

Resonance: +20% True Damage bonus on all attacks for 8s
Trigger: Apply both Light and Dark elements within 3s
Internal cooldown: 12s

Uptime calculation: 8 seconds active ÷ 12 seconds cooldown = 67% uptime. At 67% uptime, the 20% True Damage bonus becomes a 0.67 × 20% = 13.4% overall damage multiplier. This is the highest unconditional team-wide damage increase available from any single team composition choice.

Why True Damage matters: True Damage bypasses enemy DEF entirely. At Abyss floor 12 where DEF reduces normal damage by ~50%, a 20% True Damage bonus effectively becomes a ~40% effective damage increase during its window — because it is applied after DEF reduction. This makes Light + Dark resonance disproportionately powerful in endgame content. Teams that struggle with high-DEF floors should prioritize unlocking this resonance.

True Damage being applied after DEF reduction means the resonance multiplier grows stronger as enemy DEF increases. Against floor 5 enemies (20% DEF reduction), the 13.4% average is accurate. Against floor 12 enemies (50% DEF reduction), the effective value of True Damage doubles — making the resonance worth ~26.8% effective damage at floor 12. This scaling property makes Light + Dark teams the mathematically optimal choice for endgame Abyss pushing.

Other Elemental Interactions

Beyond the Light + Dark resonance, there are three additional elemental reactions. Each has a different trigger condition and damage profile.

Melt — Fire + Frost
50% bonus ATK for 1 hit on the triggering operative. This is a single-hit nuke, not a sustained buff. Best used when you can line up your highest-multiplier skill (usually skill 3 or ultimate) to consume the Melt proc. The 50% bonus applies to base ATK before other multipliers, making it additive with ATK% in the damage formula. At +80% ATK, a Melt proc increases that single hit's damage by ~28%. Highest burst potential of all reactions.
Conduct — Water + Lightning
15% CRIT Rate team buff for 6 seconds. This is a team-wide CRIT Rate increase that affects all operatives on field. At 60% baseline CRIT Rate, this buff increases CRIT multiplier from 1 + (0.6 × CRIT DMG) to 1 + (0.75 × CRIT DMG). With 150% CRIT DMG: from 1.9x to 2.125x = 11.8% damage increase during the 6s window. Best sustained DPS reaction.
Illuminate — Light + Any
Reveals stealth enemies for 10 seconds. This reaction has zero direct damage value. Its value is entirely situational: against stealth-based enemies (Phantom-type wanderers, some Abyss floor layouts), it negates the stealth mechanic entirely. In content without stealth enemies, this reaction provides no combat benefit. Never build a team around Illuminate — it is a convenience mechanic, not a damage strategy.

Reaction Priority: Light + Dark Resonance (13.4% avg team multiplier) > Melt for burst comps (~28% single-hit) > Conduct for sustained comps (~11.8% during window) > Illuminate (situational only). In most content, you will only have one reaction type active per team composition. Choose based on your team's damage profile — burst teams want Melt, sustained teams want Conduct, endgame Abyss teams want Resonance.

Elemental Application Rate

Elemental reactions are not automatic — they depend on how much "element gauge" each skill applies. The gauge system determines how quickly you can trigger reactions and how long the element persists on the enemy.

Basic Attack (hit)1 unitApplies element on hit
Resonance Skill2 unitsStandard skill cast
Ultimate3 unitsArdent Oath cast
Decay Rate1 unit per 3sGauge decreases over time
Gauge Cap5 unitsMaximum stackable gauge

Multiple applications stack the gauge, but the cap is 5 units. This means you cannot "over-apply" elements beyond 5 units — any excess application beyond the cap is wasted. The practical implication: once an enemy has 5 units of an element applied, further applications of that element do nothing until the gauge decays below the cap.

The decay rate of 1 unit per 3 seconds means a single basic attack (1 unit) lasts 3 seconds, a resonance skill (2 units) lasts 6 seconds, and an ultimate (3 units) lasts 9 seconds. The trigger window for reactions is 3 seconds — so you need at least 1 unit of both elements present on the enemy simultaneously within that 3-second window to trigger a reaction.

Practical rotation: To trigger Resonance (Light + Dark), apply one element first (e.g., Light via basic attack, 1 unit, lasts 3s), then apply the other element (Dark via resonance skill, 2 units, lasts 6s) within 3 seconds. The reaction consumes 1 unit from each element, reducing the gauge but not fully clearing it. After the reaction, you have 1 unit of Light remaining (from the original 1 unit - 1 consumed = 0 ... this is tricky — reactions consume 1 unit of the triggering element and 1 unit of the base element). This means you need to reapply elements more frequently than you might expect.

Element gauge consumption rule: When a reaction triggers, it consumes 1 unit from each element present. If only 1 unit was applied, the element is fully consumed and must be reapplied. If 2+ units were applied, the element persists with reduced gauge, enabling back-to-back reactions in some cases. A perfectly timed rotation using resonance skills (2 units) on both elements can trigger a reaction, leave 1 unit of each remaining, and trigger a second reaction within the same window if you reapply quickly.

Team Composition Math

The choice between building for elemental reactions versus raw stat stacking is a mathematical optimization problem. Here is the damage uplift for different team compositions.

2-Man Team (e.g., Light + Dark): One reaction type active. Resonance activates every 12s (cooldown) and lasts 8s. During the 4s downtime, you have no reaction benefits. Average team DPS uplift: 13.4% from Resonance. Efficiency: high — low skill ceiling, consistent returns.

3-Man Team (e.g., Light + Dark + Fire): Two reactions possible: Resonance (Light + Dark) and Melt (Fire + Frost, if Frost is present). In practice, managing two reaction gauges simultaneously creates complexity. The reactions can overlap ~20% of the fight time, but the effective damage uplift from the second reaction is reduced because you cannot always align skill cooldowns. Average total uplift: ~16–18% from reactions combined. Efficiency: moderate — higher ceiling but requires precise rotation management.

Raw Stat Stacking (3 same element or no reactions): Without reactions, you build extra ATK% or CRIT stats by ignoring elemental variety. The opportunity gain is approximately 6–9% more damage from stats compared to a reaction-focused build (because you can stack set bonuses and substats without worrying about element coverage). This option has the lowest ceiling but the highest consistency — no rotation management required.

Decision Framework: (1) Are you pushing Abyss floor 10+? Yes → Build for Resonance (Light + Dark) — the True Damage scaling with DEF makes it mathematically dominant at high floors. (2) Is your team DPS low but your survival high? Yes → Build for Melt (burst) to create clear windows. (3) Do you have excellent gear but struggle with clear times? Yes → Build for Conduct (sustained CRIT) to amplify your already-strong stats. (4) Are you speedrunning? Yes → Build for Melt + Resonance combo for maximum burst overlap.

OPTIMAL — RESONANCE
Light + Dark core. Add a flex element. The 13.4% average True Damage multiplier scales with content difficulty. Best for Abyss 10+ and general clearing. Low execution barrier.
BURST — MELT
Fire + Frost + Flex. The 50% single-hit bonus enables devastating burst windows. Best for boss floors and content with short DPS windows. Higher ceiling, higher skill requirement.

The final consideration: elemental reaction value changes with gear quality. A whale with +150% ATK and 70/170 CRIT gets proportionally less benefit from the 15% stellactrum bonus (diminishing returns) but full benefit from the 13.4% Resonance multiplier (which is multiplicative with everything). This is why whales prioritize elemental reactions over stellactrum matching — the reaction bonus is a separate multiplier that does not suffer from their already-saturated stat brackets.

Summary: Stellactrum matching = +15% ATK (diminishing with ATK% saturation). Elemental Resonance (Light + Dark) = 13.4% avg True Damage multiplier (increases with enemy DEF). Melt = 50% single-hit burst. Conduct = 15% CRIT team buff 6s. Illuminate = stealth reveal (situational). Gauge: basics 1 unit, skills 2, ultimates 3. Decay: 1 unit/3s. Cap: 5 units. Optimal team: Resonance > Melt > Conduct > raw stats for floor 10+. Whale optimization: reactions over stellactrum due to separate multiplicative layer.
