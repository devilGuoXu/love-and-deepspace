Launch Interview — Design Philosophy

Key Quote: "We wanted to create a combat system that feels good on mobile but has enough depth for PC players. The 3-character swap system was designed to be easy to learn but difficult to master — you can button-mash through early content, but endgame requires precise execution of rotations." This statement set the tone for the entire game's design philosophy.

In the launch interview (translated from a Chinese gaming publication), the lead combat designer discussed the core design pillars: approachability (anyone can play), depth (experts can optimize), and identity (each character feels unique). The 3-character swap system was inspired by team-based action games but simplified for mobile — instead of managing 4 characters with complex rotations, you manage 3 with simple swap mechanics.

The interview also revealed that the developers deliberately designed Xavier to be the "entry point" character. His kit is straightforward (high damage, simple combos) so new players feel powerful immediately. Zayne was designed as the "skill gate" — his shield-timing mechanic rewards players who learn enemy patterns. This design philosophy — gradual complexity — is why the game retains players through the early-to-mid game transition.

"We spent six months just on the feel of the basic attack. Every frame, every sound effect, every camera movement during combat. If the basic attack does not feel good, nothing else matters. Players spend 80% of combat time using basic attacks. They had to feel perfect."

— Lead Combat Designer, Launch Interview

1.2 Update Interview — Combat Direction

The 1.2 update interview was conducted after the co-op mode release. The developers addressed the community's main concerns about combat balance and future content. This interview provided the clearest picture of the game's combat direction.

CO-OP DESIGN PHILOSOPHY
"Co-op was designed to be optional but rewarding. We did not want to create content where co-op is mandatory — that alienates solo players. Instead, we made co-op give bonus rewards that are efficient but not essential. The co-op boss design focuses on coordination without requiring voice chat — visual cues and telegraphed attacks allow any group of three to succeed with practice." This design choice explains why co-op rewards are generous but never better than solo domain farming for specific materials.
BALANCE PHILOSOPHY
"We will never directly nerf a character. When we need to adjust the meta, we do so through new content that favors different playstyles. If Xavier is too dominant in Abyss floor 10, we design floor 11 to favor Sylus's execute mechanics. This way, investments are protected — your Xavier is still strong, but you are encouraged to build other characters too." This is the most important balance philosophy in the game — no nerfs, only content shifts.
DIFFICULTY SCALING
"Our difficulty curve has three phases: Early game (levels 1-30) — generous resources, forgiving enemies. Mid game (30-50) — stat checks begin, domain farming becomes important. End game (50-60) — skill checks, team composition matters, protocore optimization required. Each phase introduces a new layer of complexity. Players who rush through early content without learning the systems will hit a wall in mid game."

Interview insight: The developers explicitly stated they monitor which characters are used in each Abyss floor. If one character has >60% usage rate on a floor, they consider that floor "unbalanced." Future content is designed to address usage disparities. This means if you main a low-usage character, you can expect future content that favors your character. If you main a high-usage character, expect content that challenges your dominance.

1.5 Mid-Update — Community Feedback

The 1.5 interview focused on community feedback and how the developers incorporate player suggestions. This interview revealed the most about the developer-player relationship and how the game's roadmap is shaped by player data.

MOST REQUESTED FEATURES
Protocore QoL, more endgame content, better stamina efficiency
The developers rank community requests by frequency and feasibility. Protocore QoL (substat locking, filtering) was the #1 request for 3 consecutive patches — this directly led to the 2.0 protocore overhaul. More endgame content was #2 — leading to Abyss floors 13-15. Stamina efficiency was #3 — leading to the domain auto-clear feature. The developers track request frequency per patch and implement the top requests within 2-3 patches.
REJECTED SUGGESTIONS
Pity system changes, direct protocore purchase, power level removal
Some suggestions are consistently rejected. The developers will not increase the pity system (current 70-pull soft pity is staying). They will not allow direct protocore purchase (protocores must remain farmable only). They will not remove the power level system (it serves as a "you are ready for this content" gate). Understanding what the developers will NOT change helps players adapt their expectations.
SURPRISING COMMUNITY TRENDS
Photography community exceeded expectations, co-op competitive scene emerged
The developers were surprised by the size and engagement of the photography community. The photo mode, initially designed as a secondary feature, became one of the most-used features. Future updates will include more photo mode tools. The co-op competitive scene (speedrun competitions) was also unexpected — the developers are exploring formal competitive support.

"We read every piece of feedback. Every survey, every Reddit post, every Discord message. We have a dedicated team that categorizes feedback by topic and sentiment. When 1,000 players say the same thing, we listen. When 10,000 players say the same thing, we act. The protocore substat lock system is a direct result of community feedback — players told us for 8 months that protocore RNG was too punishing, and we finally had the solution ready."

— Producer, 1.5 Mid-Update Interview

Anniversary Preview — Future Vision

The anniversary preview interview (conducted 2 weeks before the 1-year anniversary) outlined the game's direction for the next year. This is the most forward-looking interview the developers have given.

YEAR 2 ROADMAP (TEASED)
The developers outlined four pillars for Year 2: (1) More operatives — multiple new characters planned, each with unique combat styles. (2) Deeper combat — new mechanics beyond the current dodge/resonance/ultimate system. (3) Expanded social features — guilds or teams with shared progression. (4) Cross-platform improvements — better PC client, controller support. The roadmap is intentionally vague — the developers learned from other games that over-promising leads to disappointment when plans change.
MONETIZATION PHILOSOPHY
"We want players to feel that spending money accelerates progress but is not required to experience all content. A free player should be able to clear all story content and most endgame content. Spending gives convenience and cosmetics, not power that free players cannot access. The new operative in 2.0 will be fully functional at R0 — rank-ups are for players who want to invest more in their favorite characters."
LONGEVITY PLANS
"We are building Deepspace for the long term. Our content pipeline is planned 12-18 months ahead. We have a multi-year story arc planned. We are investing in engine improvements and new features for years to come. We have seen gacha games fail because they ran out of content in year 2. We are committed to avoiding that — our content production rate will increase, not decrease."

Key takeaway from the anniversary interview: The developers are planning for the long term. They are not treating Deepspace as a short-term cash grab. The 12-18 month content pipeline means: (1) You can invest in characters knowing they will have relevant content. (2) Powercreep will be gradual, not explosive. (3) The game will continue to receive major updates for years. This is reassuring for players who are hesitant to invest time and money into a game that might decline.

What It Means for Players

These developer patterns have practical implications for how you should play the game:

INVEST IN ROSTER DEPTH
Build all available characters — not just one or two
Since the developers balance through content shifts, future content will favor different characters. A one-character roster will struggle in future patches. Build Xavier, Zayne, Sylus, Rafayel, and the new 2.0 operative to at least level 50. This ensures you always have the character favored by current content.
GIVE CONSTRUCTIVE FEEDBACK
The developers read and act on well-articulated feedback
When giving feedback, be specific. "Protocore enhancement RNG feels too punishing because I spent 10K dust without getting CRIT Rate" is actionable. "Protocores are bad" is not. The developers categorize feedback by specificity — actionable feedback gets prioritized. Detailed feedback with supporting data (screenshots, video clips) is most effective.
TRUST THE LONGEVITY
Long-term investments in characters and account progression are safe
The developers have committed to the game's long-term future. Your account progression will not be reset. Characters will not be powercrept into irrelevance. Protocores will continue to be useful through system updates. This is not a game where you need to "catch up" after every patch — consistent play builds lasting value.
DO
  • Build a diverse roster — 4+ characters at level 50+
  • Provide specific, actionable feedback to developers
  • Invest confidently in characters you enjoy
  • Expect future content to favor different characters
  • Trust that protocore and system improvements are coming
DON'T
  • Don't worry about nerfs — they never happen
  • Don't skip characters because "they might get powercrept"
  • Don't feel pressured to spend — F2P is viable long-term
  • Don't ignore community feedback channels
  • Don't expect immediate changes — feedback takes 2-3 patches

Staying informed: Developer interviews are published on the official website and translated by the community. Follow the official social media accounts and the community translation team for timely access. Major interviews happen around every significant patch (launch, 1.2, 1.5, anniversary). The next major interview is expected at the 2.0 launch window.

Summary: Developer interviews reveal five consistent patterns: (1) Balance through content shifts, never nerfs. (2) Data-driven decisions based on player behavior. (3) Community feedback is tracked and acted on within 2-3 patches. (4) Long-term commitment with 12-18 month content pipeline. (5) Monetization restraint — R0 characters are fully functional. Build roster depth, provide specific feedback, and trust the game's longevity. The protocore overhaul (substat locking) and Abyss expansion (floors 13-15) are direct results of community feedback. The developers are building Deepspace for the long term — your investments are safe.
