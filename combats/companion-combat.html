Companion Combat Basics

The companion (your chosen operative) fights alongside the Hunter in combat. The companion has their own HP bar, energy pool, and skill set. They auto-attack enemies (dealing approximately 25-35% of the Hunter's DPS in passive mode) and can be commanded to use their active skill on a 30-second cooldown. The companion's stats are derived from their Affinity level and the memories you have equipped on them. A well-built companion at Affinity 80+ contributes 40-50% of total team DPS — effectively doubling the Hunter's damage output.

Companion combat effectiveness depends on three factors: Affinity level (unlocks skill tiers and stat multipliers), equipped memory set (determines which skills and bonuses are active), and AI behavior settings (target priority, skill usage frequency). The companion AI is configurable in the combat settings menu: you can set them to "Aggressive" (prioritize attacking, uses skills on cooldown), "Support" (prioritizes staying near the Hunter, uses skills defensively), or "Balanced" (default). Aggressive mode yields the highest DPS but requires the companion to be well-geared enough to survive enemy attention.

Affinity & Damage Scaling

Companion damage scales with Affinity level in discrete tiers. At Affinity 20, the companion unlocks their first active skill (200% damage). At Affinity 40, the active skill upgrades to 300% damage and gains a secondary effect (e.g., 30% slow for 3 seconds). At Affinity 60, the companion gains a passive aura that boosts the Hunter's damage by 10%. At Affinity 80, the active skill reaches its final form (500% damage with a powerful secondary effect). At Affinity 100 (max), the companion gains a second passive — usually a significant stat boost for the entire team.

The stat scaling is equally important. Each Affinity level grants the companion: +0.5% base ATK, +0.3% base HP, and +0.2% base DEF. At Affinity 100, this translates to +50% base ATK, +30% base HP, and +20% base DEF — all multiplicative with gear. A companion at Affinity 100 with good gear is genuinely a second main DPS character. The investment to reach Affinity 100 is substantial (approximately 3-4 months of daily interaction for F2P players), but the combat return is equivalent to building a third team member. For players serious about endgame content, companion Affinity farming is mandatory, not optional.

Skill Timing & Rotation Integration

The companion's active skill should be integrated into your rotation, not used randomly. The optimal timing depends on the companion's skill effect. For damage-type skills: use the companion skill at the start of your burst window (when your Hunter's Resonance is ready, elemental reactions are primed, and enemy debuffs are active). This ensures the companion's 500% damage is amplified by all active buffs. For utility-type skills (heals, shields, debuffs): use them when the corresponding need arises — heals when your HP drops below 50%, shields before boss ultimate attacks, debuffs at the start of the fight.

A good initial rotation script: T=0s: Companion active skill (applies debuff, deals initial damage). T=1.5s: Swap to secondary operative (if using a 2-op team). T=3s: Use secondary operative's debuff/buff skill. T=5s: Swap to Hunter main DPS. T=6s: Hunter Resonance skill (amplified by companion debuff + secondary buff). T=10-30s: Filler skills and basic attacks while waiting for companion cooldown. T=30s: Companion active skill available again — repeat cycle. This 30-second rotation aligns the companion skill with every other Resonance use, creating consistent burst windows.

Manual vs auto command: The companion active skill can be set to auto-cast (on cooldown) or manual-cast (commanded by tapping the companion button). For maximum DPS, use manual command — auto-cast often wastes the skill on low-value targets or at suboptimal timing. The only exception is if you are playing a support role in co-op, where auto-cast is acceptable because you are focused on healing and positioning. For solo content, always manual-command the companion active skill. The 15% DPS difference between auto and manual is significant in endgame content.

Best Memory Setups for Companion

The companion uses the same memory system as the Hunter — they equip up to 6 memories with set bonuses and protocores. The companion's memory set should be built differently from the Hunter's. Since the companion auto-attacks and uses skills on command (not on a fixed rotation), CRIT Rate and CRIT DMG are less reliable — the companion might not crit when you need them to. Instead, prioritize flat ATK and Elemental Damage for consistent damage output. Energy Regen is also valuable because it reduces the cooldown of the companion's active skill from 30s to approximately 24s at 150% ER.

By Operative: Best Companion Combos

Xavier Companion + Hunter: Xavier's companion active skill is a light-element execute that deals bonus damage to low-HP enemies. Pair with a Hunter using Light Seeker set for maximum execute synergy. Rotation: weaken enemy to 40% HP → command Xavier companion skill → follow with Hunter Resonance for the kill. This combo eliminates high-priority targets (elites, boss adds) in seconds.

Zayne Companion + Hunter: Zayne's companion skill freezes enemies in an AoE for 2 seconds. This is the best crowd-control companion ability in the game. Pair with a Frost-element Hunter for the freeze-detonate combo: command Zayne freeze → switch to Hunter → use Frost Resonance on frozen enemy (guaranteed CRIT on frozen targets). This combo deals massive burst damage and controls dangerous enemy groups.

Rafayel Companion + Hunter: Rafayel's companion skill applies a stacking burn DoT that ramps up over time. Best paired with a Hunter who can keep enemies in a small area (Rafayel's water AoE skills). The burn ticks for 8 seconds, dealing approximately 200% total damage. This is the best companion for sustained DPS fights (bosses with long HP bars).

Sylus Companion + Hunter: Sylus's companion skill teleports behind the target and strikes for massive Dark damage, applying a vulnerability debuff (+15% damage taken for 5 seconds). Pair with a burst-damage Hunter (any element) for the vulnerability window. This combo is the best "nuke" opener — command Sylus → swap to Hunter → unload all skills into the debuffed enemy.

Multi-operative accounts: If you play multiple operatives, note that companion Affinity does not transfer between operatives. Each operative's companion has their own Affinity level. Focus on building companion Affinity for one operative first (the one you use most in combat). The combat benefit of a high-Affinity companion for your main operative outweighs having four low-Affinity companions across all characters. Secondary operatives can use the "Support" companion build (which requires lower Affinity to be effective) while you build their Affinity passively.

Team Composition Strategies

Incorporating the companion changes team composition math. In a standard 2-operative team, the companion effectively makes it a 3-character team — but the companion shares Affinity and gear with your chosen operative, not the second operative. This creates two viable team structures: "Dual DPS + Companion" (Hunter + Operative Companion + Secondary Operative) — optimal for burst compositions where the companion provides the opening burst, the secondary operative provides debuffs, and the Hunter executes. "Hypercarry + Companion" (Hunter + Companion + Support Operative) — optimal for sustained fights where the companion and support provide consistent DPS uptime while the Hunter cycles through skill rotations.

The Dual DPS structure is generally recommended for most content because it provides more flexibility and redundancy. If the companion takes heavy damage and needs to disengage, the secondary operative can fill the gap. The Hypercarry structure has higher theoretical DPS but is more fragile — if the support operative is knocked out, the team loses significant combat effectiveness. For new players, start with Dual DPS. For endgame players with high-Affinity companions, Hypercarry can push speedrun records.

Co-op considerations: In co-op content, each player brings their own companion. Companions in co-op are separate entities and do not interfere with each other. However, companion skills can overlap — two companions using freeze at the same time results in wasted crowd control. Coordinate companion skill usage with your co-op partner(s): one companion opens with CC (Zayne freeze), the other follows with burst (Sylus vuln debuff), then both Hunters attack the debuffed/frozen target. Coordinated companion usage in co-op can clear boss phases 20-30% faster than uncoordinated play.

Affinity Farming for Combat

Combat-focused Affinity farming prioritizes different activities than cosmetic-focused farming. Daily interactions that give combat-relevant Affinity EXP: completing daily missions with the companion (5 missions × 10 EXP each = 50/day), bringing the companion on Abyss runs (100 EXP per floor, cap 300/day), winning co-op battles with the companion active (50 EXP per win, cap 200/day), and using companion skill in domain runs (10 EXP per run, cap 100/day). Total combat Affinity EXP per day: approximately 650-900, depending on Abyss progression.

The weekly Affinity cap from combat activities is approximately 6,000 EXP. To reach Affinity 100 from Affinity 1 requires approximately 250,000 total EXP. At the optimal daily rate (900 EXP/day), this takes approximately 280 days of consistent play — approximately 9 months. However, special events (double Affinity weekends, companion-focused event bonuses) can accelerate this by 20-30%. Plan for 6-8 months to reach Affinity 100 on your main operative's companion. Use the gift system to supplement — high-rarity gifts matching your companion's preferences give 200-500 EXP each and are the fastest way to bridge gaps between combat farming sessions.
