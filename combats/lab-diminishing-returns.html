Stat Diminishing Returns

Every stat in Deepspace follows the law of diminishing returns. The first 30% of CRIT Rate is worth more than the second 30%. Know where the breakpoints are and stop investing past them.

Core Concept

Core Concept: Diminishing returns in Deepspace is not a mechanic — it is a mathematical inevitability. Every additive stat bracket has a saturation point where adding more of the same stat yields less percentage increase than adding a different stat. The optimal build is the one that balances all brackets, not one that maxes a single stat.

Imagine you are pouring water into three buckets. If you pour all the water into one bucket, you get one full bucket and two empty ones. If you distribute the water evenly, you get three partially full buckets — more total water capacity used. Stats work the same way. ATK%, CRIT, DMG Bonus, and Element are the four buckets. Your goal is to fill them evenly, not to overflow one while the others stay empty.

The key insight: multiplication punishes imbalance. Your final damage is the product of several brackets. If one bracket is very high and another is very low, the product suffers. Raising the low bracket by a small amount often gives a bigger total increase than raising the high bracket by a large amount.

The 100% Rule: Once you have +100% ATK from all sources (memories, protocores, set bonuses), switch to investing in other brackets. The 100% ATK breakpoint is the single most important threshold in the game. Every ATK% roll after +100% is worth roughly half of what it was worth before +100%. At +150%, it is worth a quarter. Stop at +100% and diversify.

CRIT Rate Diminishing Returns

Formula: CRIT Multiplier = 1 + (CRIT Rate × CRIT DMG). This is a product, not a sum — the relationship between Rate and DMG is multiplicative, which creates a different kind of diminishing return.

Rate vs DMG relationship: The optimal ratio is approximately 1:2 (e.g., 70% Rate : 140% DMG). At this ratio, your CRIT multiplier is 1 + (0.7 × 1.4) = 1.98x. If you shift to 80% Rate / 120% DMG, the multiplier becomes 1 + (0.8 × 1.2) = 1.96x — slightly worse, even though Rate is higher. The ratio matters more than the individual numbers.

Rule: Stop investing in CRIT Rate at 70% for most operatives. Beyond 70%, the rolls are better spent on CRIT DMG or ATK%. The only exception is Sylus with execute passive — he benefits from higher Rate to guarantee crits during the short execute window. For everyone else, 70% is the ceiling.

Energy Recharge Diminishing Returns

Energy Recharge affects how quickly you charge your Ardent Oath. The relationship is not linear — ER has a soft cap at 120% and a hard cap at 200%.

Never build ER on a DPS past 130%. Every ER roll past 130% on a DPS operative is a roll that could have been ATK%, CRIT Rate, or CRIT DMG. The damage loss from missing those offensive substats is greater than the gain from having Oath available slightly earlier. On DPS, 130% ER is the absolute ceiling. On supports, 160% is the ceiling. Only speedrunners need more.

Defense & HP Diminishing Returns

For support builds (Zayne): HP% has a similar curve to ATK% for DPS. DEF% has even steeper diminishing returns and should be avoided entirely.

0–50% HP (Zayne)+7% shield value per 10% HP — high value
50–80% HP (Zayne)+5% shield value per 10% HP — medium value
80%+ HP (Zayne)+3% shield value per 10% HP — low value
Zayne breakpoint35k HP (~+70% HP) — past this, invest in ER instead
DEF soft capPast 30% DEF from gear, <2% effective HP per point

Zayne HP Optimization: The ideal Zayne build reaches exactly 35k HP and then stops. Every HP roll past 35k gives diminishing shield value. At 35k HP, switch all remaining substat investment to Energy Recharge (target 150%+). A 35k HP / 150% ER Zayne contributes more to team survivability and DPS than a 40k HP / 120% ER Zayne because the extra Oath uptime from ER gives more frequent healing and shield casts.

Golden Rule of Build Optimization

The Golden Rule of Build Optimization: Your damage is the product of (ATK bracket) × (CRIT bracket) × (DMG Bonus bracket) × (Element bracket). To maximize the product, you need all brackets to be roughly equal. A build with +80% ATK / 60% CRIT Rate / 120% CRIT DMG / 30% DMG Bonus has a multiplier product of 1.8 × 1.72 × 1.3 = 4.02. A build with +120% ATK / 40% CRIT Rate / 100% CRIT DMG / 10% DMG Bonus has a product of 2.2 × 1.4 × 1.1 = 3.39. The first build is 18% stronger despite having lower ATK. Balance over peak stats.

Here is a step-by-step way to evaluate your own build balance:

1
Calculate your ATK multiplier
Take your total ATK% from all sources (memories + protocores + set bonuses). ATK multiplier = 1 + (total ATK% / 100). Example: +80% ATK = 1.8x.
2
Calculate your CRIT multiplier
CRIT multiplier = 1 + (CRIT Rate% × CRIT DMG%). Example: 60% Rate × 120% DMG = 0.72. CRIT multiplier = 1.72x.
3
Calculate your DMG Bonus multiplier
Total all additive DMG bonuses: elemental DMG + skill type DMG + set bonuses. DMG multiplier = 1 + (total DMG Bonus% / 100). Example: +30% = 1.3x.
4
Multiply all three
Total multiplier = ATK × CRIT × DMG. Compare across builds — the higher product wins. A build with 1.8 × 1.72 × 1.3 = 4.02 beats a build with 2.2 × 1.4 × 1.1 = 3.39 every time.

Damage Formula Walkthrough

Let us apply the golden rule to a real example. Consider two Xavier builds:

Build A — ATK% focused+120% ATK / 45% CRIT / 100% CRIT DMG / 15% Light DMG
Build B — Balanced+85% ATK / 65% CRIT / 130% CRIT DMG / 25% Light DMG

Build A: ATK = 2.2, CRIT = 1 + (0.45 × 1.0) = 1.45, DMG = 1.15. Product = 2.2 × 1.45 × 1.15 = 3.67

Build B: ATK = 1.85, CRIT = 1 + (0.65 × 1.3) = 1.845, DMG = 1.25. Product = 1.85 × 1.845 × 1.25 = 4.27

Build B is 16% stronger than Build A, even though Build A has 35% more ATK. The balanced distribution of stats creates a higher total product. This is why chasing one stat (ATK%) while neglecting others (CRIT, DMG Bonus) is a trap.

Practical application: When comparing two protocore builds, this calculation takes 30 seconds and is more accurate than "feeling" which build is stronger. Keep a simple DPS calculator in a spreadsheet with your operative's stats. Update it whenever you change protocores. Do not trust "bigger number = better" — burst damage and sustained DPS are different metrics, and the multiplier product method accounts for sustained performance.

Summary: ATK% soft cap at +100%. CRIT Rate soft cap at 70% (1:2 ratio with CRIT DMG). ER soft cap at 120% for DPS, 160% for supports. Hard cap 200% ER. Build balance: ATK bracket × CRIT bracket × DMG Bonus bracket = damage. Balance beats peak stat stacking every time. The Build B approach (balanced) is 16% stronger than the Build A approach (ATK-stacked) in our example.
