Build Feedback & Sharing Guide

How to give and receive build feedback effectively — community etiquette, standardized formatting, and evaluation frameworks for meaningful critique.

WRK_082026.07.03COMMUNITY

Key Insight: Most build feedback requests fail because they lack context. A screenshot of stats without operative name, content goal, or account tier is nearly impossible to evaluate. Standardizing how you present builds for feedback reduces back-and-forth questions and produces actionable advice in the first response. Learn the framework once, apply it to every build review request.

Why Standardized Feedback Matters

The Love and Deepspace build community spans Reddit, Discord, Twitter, and dedicated forums. Each platform has different norms for sharing builds, but the underlying information needed for evaluation is universal. A well-formatted build request includes: operative name and R-level, all final stats (ATK, CRIT Rate, CRIT DMG, ER, Elemental Damage), memory sets equipped (4-piece + 2-piece), protocore main stats for all 6 slots (not just the highlights), the specific content you are trying to clear (boss name, floor number, game mode), and your account tier (F2P, BP, monthly pass, whale).

Without this information, feedback is guesswork. A player saying "my Xavier does low damage" could have 1,800 ATK (under-geared) or 2,400 ATK with 30% CRIT (stat imbalance) or 2,200 ATK with correct stats but wrong set (set bonus missing). Each of these requires a different fix. Standardized requests eliminate the diagnostic phase and let the community jump directly to solutions. The time saved is significant — a good request gets actionable advice in the first reply, while a poor request requires 3-5 rounds of clarification.

Before you post: Take screenshots of: (1) The stat screen (showing all 6 stats, not cropped). (2) The memory set screen (show all 6 equipped memories with set names visible). (3) The protocore screen for each slot (tap through all 6 and screenshot each). (4) Your account tier proof (optional — your BP level or purchase history screen). Having these screenshots ready before posting means responders can help you immediately rather than asking for more information.

Build Request Format

STANDARD BUILD REQUEST TEMPLATE
Operative: Xavier (R0 — no resonance dupes).
Stats: ATK 2,180 / CRIT 52% / CRIT DMG 115% / ER 108% / Light DMG 58%.
Sets: 4pc Light Seeker (+9 avg) + 2pc Storm Chaser (+6 avg).
Protocores: S1 ATK%+12, S2 ATK%+12, S3 ATK%+9, S4 CRIT+12, S5 ATK%+12, S6 ER+9.
Content Goal: Abyss Floor 10 (stuck at 2.3M damage, need 2.5M).
Account Tier: Monthly pass only. F2P since patch 2.0.
Question: Should I push protocores to +15 or farm for a better 4-piece?

Copy this template and fill in your details when requesting feedback. A response to this request would be immediate and specific: "Push your CRIT rate to 60% first — your 52% is below the Breakpoint 2 threshold. Replace Slot 3 ATK% with CRIT% if you have one, or farm dust and fish for CRIT substats on your existing pieces. Your +9/+12 protocores are fine for Floor 10 — the issue is CRIT consistency, not protocore level. Do not farm for better 4-piece yet; your current set is adequate." This is actionable feedback in a single response, made possible by the complete information in the template.

Template shortcut: Save the template as a text file or clipboard snippet. When you want feedback, paste the template, fill in your numbers, and post. The template takes 2 minutes to fill and saves 10+ minutes of back-and-forth questions. Experienced community members are much more likely to respond to well-formatted requests because they can help immediately without playing 20 questions.

Evaluation Framework

When evaluating someone else's build, use the "First Principles" framework: (1) Check set activation — is the 4-piece bonus active? If not, that is issue #1 regardless of anything else. (2) Check main stats — does every protocore slot have the correct main stat for the operative's role (DPS/support/tank)? Slot 4 CRIT for DPS, Slot 5 Elemental DMG, Slot 6 ATK or ER. (3) Check stat thresholds — does the build meet minimum breakpoints for the content goal (e.g., 50% CRIT for Abyss 10)? (4) Check stat balance — is the build over-invested in one stat at the expense of another (e.g., 2,500 ATK but 20% CRIT)? (5) Check efficiency — is the investment proportional to the account stage (whale with +9 protocores suspicious, F2P with +15 protocores impressive)?

This framework produces a prioritized improvement list: "Fix set bonus first (change 2 memories to activate 4-piece). Then change Slot 3 from ATK% to Elemental DMG. Then farm dust to push CRIT from 45% to 55%. Then upgrade your protocores from +9 to +12." Each step has a clear action, a reason, and an expected impact. The framework prevents vague feedback like "your stats look low" or "farm more" — it pinpoints exactly what to change and why.

How to Give Feedback

Effective feedback is: specific (not "your build is bad" but "your CRIT rate needs to be 10% higher for content consistency"), actionable (not "get better protocores" but "replace Slot 6 ER with ATK% — you have enough ER from substats"), prioritized (not a list of 10 things but "start with these 3 changes in this order"), and kind (remember that someone spent time and resources on this build — acknowledge effort before critique). A good feedback format is the "sandwich": positive observation → constructive suggestion → encouraging close.

When giving feedback on the Standard Build Request Template: (1) Acknowledge something they did well — "Your stat balance is actually quite good for a monthly pass account. The 52% CRIT / 115% CDMG ratio is in the right ballpark." (2) Give the most impactful suggestion first — "However, your CRIT rate is 8% below the Floor 10 breakpoint. I would prioritize replacing your Slot 3 ATK% protocore with a CRIT% main stat. That alone will push you past 60% CRIT and should close the damage gap." (3) Follow up with a less urgent suggestion — "Once you clear Floor 10, consider working on your 4-piece set quality — your current set is SR-heavy. The Light Seeker 4-piece bonus will scale better as your CRIT improves."

How to Receive Feedback

Receiving build feedback well is as important as giving it. Remember: the feedback is about the build, not about you. Defensiveness ("I tried that and it didn't work") blocks improvement. Instead, ask clarifying questions: "Can you explain why CRIT% at 60% is the breakpoint?" or "Would you prioritize CRIT% over maintaining 2,100 ATK?" These questions deepen your understanding and show the responder that their time was valued.

After receiving feedback: (1) Thank the responder(s) — they spent time helping you for free. (2) Implement the changes in the order suggested. (3) Test the build against the content goal. (4) Report back — posting "I swapped Slot 3 to CRIT%, my CRIT went from 52% to 61%, and I cleared Floor 10 with 2.7M damage. Thank you!" completes the feedback loop and encourages future participation. Responders who see results from their advice are more likely to help others, strengthening the entire community.

Follow-up etiquette: If you try the advice and it does not work, do not blame the advisor. State what you changed and what result you got — "I swapped to CRIT% on Slot 3 but my ATK dropped to 1,900 and my damage went down even with higher CRIT." This invites a second round of feedback that can diagnose the new issue (in this case, your ATK dropped below a minimum threshold). Build optimization is iterative — rare is the single change that solves everything. Each adjustment reveals the next bottleneck.

Common Feedback Pitfalls

THE STAT ONLY FOCUS
"Your ATK is too low" without context
ATK requirements vary by operative, content, and account tier. Xavier needs different ATK than Zayne. Floor 6 needs different ATK than Floor 12. Always reference specific content and operative when talking about stat targets. Generic stat advice is often wrong for the specific situation.
THE PERFECT SOLUTION
"Just get the limited SSR memory"
Suggesting solutions that require significant spending or luck ignores the player's account reality. If someone is asking for F2P build advice, do not tell them to pull on limited banners. Suggest alternatives using free memories, SRs, or universal sets that are accessible to their account tier.
THE OVERWHELMING LIST
10+ improvements listed in random order
Human beings can focus on 3-4 changes at a time. List improvements in priority order and group them: "Short-term (this week): fix Slot 3 main stat. Medium-term (this month): upgrade 4-piece to SSR. Long-term (next 2 months): push all protocores to +15." This gives the player a roadmap rather than a to-do list.
THE ONE-TRUE-BUILD
"X set is the only viable option"
Build diversity exists within reasonable parameters. Multiple set combinations can work for each operative. Do not dismiss a non-meta set that fits the player's available memories. Suggest the optimal choice but acknowledge that their current set is workable. Flexibility keeps the community inclusive.

Community Norms & Etiquette

The Love and Deepspace build feedback community has developed informal norms that make interactions productive. Always preface unsolicited advice with "May I suggest..." or "Consider..." — this frames the feedback as optional rather than mandatory. Upvote or thank responses that help you, even if you do not implement their exact suggestion. If you disagree with a suggestion, state why with data or reasoning ("I found that ER 130% allows 3 Resonance uses per fight versus 2 at 108%, so I prioritize ER higher"). Do not downvote or dismiss advice just because it contradicts your current build.

Platform-specific norms: On Reddit, include the template in your post body (not just screenshots) for search indexing. On Discord, use the dedicated build-feedback channels and follow the pinned format. On Twitter, tag @LADS_BuildHelp for community visibility and include all screenshots in a single thread. The community values effort — a well-formatted request with screenshots and the template gets 3-5x more responses than a low-effort "help me" post. Put in the effort, and the community will match it.

Building the community: Once you have received helpful feedback and improved your build, pay it forward. Review 2-3 other build requests per week using the evaluation framework. You do not need to be a top player to give good feedback — the First Principles framework works at any knowledge level. Early-stage players often give the most empathetic feedback because they recently struggled with the same issues. A community where everyone contributes feedback grows stronger than one where a few experts carry the load.

Summary: Standardized build requests include: operative, stats, sets, protocores, content goal, account tier, and specific question. Use the template to format requests (2 minutes of effort saves 10+ minutes of clarification). Evaluate builds using First Principles: set activation → main stats → thresholds → balance → efficiency. Give feedback with the sandwich method: positive → constructive → encouraging. Receive feedback with gratitude and implementation. Common pitfalls: contextless stat advice, suggesting paid solutions, overwhelming to-do lists, and one-true-build dogma. Community norms: preface unsolicited advice, upvote helpful responses, state disagreements with data. Pay it forward by reviewing other builds — the community thrives on reciprocal participation.
