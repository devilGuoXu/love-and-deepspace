The Great Debate

F2P PLAYER VS SPENDER — MODERATED BY KAELEN — JULY 2026

Two players. Two accounts. Two completely different philosophies. We sat down to argue the question that divides every gacha community: is spending money on Love and Deepspace worth it? No holds barred. No polite agreement. Just two people who genuinely believe they are right.

§ § §
ROUND 1 — THE OPENING STATEMENT
ALEX • F2P (ACCOUNT B)

Look, I am not going to sit here and tell you that spending money is morally wrong. That is stupid. It is your money. But I am going to tell you that spending money is unnecessary. I have been playing this game for ten months on a completely free account. I have cleared Abyss floor 10. I have three SSR memories. I have built a competitive team that can handle all current content. I did not spend a single cent. The game gives you enough resources to clear everything if you are patient and strategic. Spending skips the patience part, sure, but it also skips the satisfaction of earning it yourself.

JORDAN • SPENDER (ACCOUNT A)

I agree that you can clear content without spending. I do not think anyone disputes that. But let us be honest about what "clearing content" means on a F2P account. Alex cleared floor 10. I cleared floor 12 in month four. Alex has three SSRs. I have twelve. Alex spent zero dollars. I have spent approximately what I would spend on a new console game plus a few DLC packs over a year. The question is not can you play for free. The question is what is your time worth. I have a job. I have limited gaming hours. I would rather spend money to accelerate past the grind and spend my limited time on the parts I actually enjoy.

ROUND 2 — THE PSYCHOLOGY OF PULLING
ALEX • F2P

The best feeling in this game is pulling a limited character that you saved for. I saved for two months for Sylus's banner. I did every daily, every event, every gem source I could find. When I finally pulled him on pull 67, I literally yelled. I called my friend. I took a screenshot. That moment was worth more than any purchase could be because I earned it. If I had just swiped a card, I would have gotten the same character, but the emotional payoff would have been completely different. Spending removes the journey and leaves only the destination.

JORDAN • SPENDER

I hear this argument a lot and I think it romanticizes frustration. What you are describing is two months of anxiety. Logging in every day wondering if you will have enough. Checking your gem count obsessively. Calculating pity over and over. Skipping banners you wanted because you were saving for one specific character. That does not sound like an enjoyable journey to me. That sounds like a second job. When I want a character, I pull for them. Sometimes I get them early and I feel great. Sometimes I hit pity and I pay the difference. The feeling of getting the character is the same. The difference is I did not spend two months being anxious about it.

ALEX • F2P

But that is the game. The anxiety is part of the experience. Making hard choices about which banners to skip is where the strategy lives. If you can pull on every banner, you are not really making choices. You are just collecting. The game becomes a shopping trip instead of a strategy game. F2P forces you to engage with the resource management systems that make gacha games interesting in the first place.

JORDAN • SPENDER

Or it forces you to miss content you would have enjoyed. I pulled for Rafayel's limited outfit because I liked the design. I did not need it. It was not meta. I just wanted it. On a F2P account, that pull would have meant giving up the next limited banner. I did not have to make that choice. I got the outfit and I still had gems for the banner. I got to enjoy something that a F2P player would have had to skip. That is not shopping. That is access to content. The game designed the outfit for people to enjoy. Spending allows me to enjoy more of what the game offers.

ROUND 3 — THE POWER GAP
ALEX • F2P

Here is the thing about the power gap: it is smaller than people think. Jordan cleared floor 12 in month four. I cleared floor 10 in month ten. That sounds like a big difference, but consider this. Floor 11 gives 50 more gems per reset than floor 10. Floor 12 gives 50 more than floor 11. Over a year, Jordan gets about 5,200 more gems from Abyss than me. That is roughly 37 pulls per year. Jordan spent money to get twelve SSRs. I spent patience to get three. The gem gap from clearing higher floors does not close the SSR gap. But it also does not need to, because I do not need twelve SSRs to clear the content I care about.

JORDAN • SPENDER

I think Alex is underselling the gap. It is not just Abyss gems. It is protocore domain efficiency. It is weekly boss kill speed. It is event completion time. Everything in this game scales with account power. A stronger account clears everything faster, which means more time to do other things or play other games. Also, and I think this matters: Alex is an experienced gacha player who knows how to optimize. A new player going F2P without that experience is going to have a much harder time. The F2P path requires game knowledge that new players do not have. Spending is an equalizer that lets new players learn the game without being punished for every mistake.

ALEX • F2P

I will concede that the F2P path requires more game knowledge. But that knowledge is freely available. There are guides, community resources, tier lists, farming calculators. The information exists. The barrier is not knowledge. It is the willingness to learn. Spending is a shortcut past learning, and I think that actually hurts players in the long run. If you spend to skip understanding the systems, you never learn the systems. You end up with a strong account and weak fundamentals, which means you will hit a wall eventually and not know how to fix it.

JORDAN • SPENDER

Or you spend to give yourself room to learn. I spent money to build multiple operatives, which let me experiment with different team compositions and find what I enjoyed. A F2P player has one team and one strategy. If that strategy does not work, they have to farm for weeks to pivot. I can pivot in a day because I have the resources. Spending gives me the flexibility to learn through experimentation rather than through spreadsheets. Both paths lead to knowledge. They just take different routes.

ROUND 4 — THE VALUE QUESTION
ALEX • F2P

Let us talk about what you actually get for your money. The monthly pass costs $5 and gives 3,000 gems over 30 days. That is 600 gems per dollar. Direct top-up gives about 100 gems per dollar. The battle pass costs $10 and gives about 2,000 gems worth of value plus exclusive materials. Are these good value? Compared to other forms of entertainment, maybe. A movie ticket costs $15 for two hours. The monthly pass costs $5 for thirty days of bonus resources. But you are not buying entertainment. You are buying acceleration. You are paying money to play less of the game. That is a strange transaction when you think about it.

JORDAN • SPENDER

I think that framing is dishonest. I am not paying to play less. I am paying to play more of what I want. There are parts of this game I enjoy less than others. I do not enjoy farming ascension materials for the fifth time. I do enjoy trying new team compositions in Abyss. By spending, I reduce the time I spend on the parts I like less and increase the time I spend on the parts I like more. That is not paying to play less. That is paying to customize my experience. It is the same reason I buy convenience products in real life. I could grow my own vegetables. I choose to buy them at the store because my time is better spent on other things.

ALEX • F2P

But that convenience comes with a risk. Gacha monetization is designed to exploit psychological vulnerabilities. The 50-50, the soft pity system, the limited time banners, the FOMO. These are not neutral mechanics. They are designed to make you spend more than you intended. Every spender I know has a story about spending more than they planned on a banner they did not really care about because they were tilted from losing the 50-50. The game is engineered to erode your self-control. Playing F2P is the only way to opt out of that system entirely.

JORDAN • SPENDER

I am not going to defend exploitative monetization practices. They exist. They are real. But the solution is not to pretend the problem does not affect you by refusing to engage with it. The solution is to develop healthy spending habits. I set a monthly budget. I track my spending. I never pull on the last day of a banner. I take breaks when I feel the tilt building. The game will exploit you if you let it. The answer is to not let it. F2P is one solution. Budgeting is another. Both are valid. The problem is not spending itself. It is spending without awareness.

§ § §
ROUND 5 — CLOSING STATEMENTS
ALEX • F2P

I am not going to tell anyone what to do with their money. If you can afford to spend and it makes you happy, that is your choice. But I want people to know that spending is optional. The game is completable without it. The satisfaction of building an account from nothing, of earning every SSR through patience and planning, of knowing that your account's strength comes from your decisions and not your wallet. That satisfaction is real and it is available to everyone. You do not have to spend to love this game. You do not have to spend to be good at it. The only requirement is time and attention. If you have those, the rest will come.

JORDAN • SPENDER

And I want people to know that spending is not something to feel guilty about. You are supporting a game you love. You are buying access to content you enjoy. You are choosing how to spend your entertainment budget. The guilt around spending in gacha communities is manufactured by people who have turned frugality into a moral virtue. It is not virtuous to suffer through a game you could be enjoying more. It is not virtuous to miss content you would have loved because of an arbitrary spending cap. Spend what you can afford. Enjoy the game. Ignore the judgment. It is your money and your time. No one else gets a vote.

MODERATOR'S VERDICT

I am not going to declare a winner here, because I do not think there is one. Alex and Jordan are both right. The F2P path is valid, rewarding, and entirely viable. The spending path is also valid, rewarding, and entirely viable. The real answer to "should I spend" depends on your financial situation, your tolerance for grind, your available play time, and your personality. If you are prone to impulse spending, play F2P. If you have disposable income and limited time, spend wisely. If you are enjoying the game, you are doing it right. The only wrong answer is spending more than you can afford. Everything else is just preference dressed up as principle.

Alex and Jordan are composite characters representing common viewpoints in the community. The arguments presented are based on real discussions from Discord, Reddit, and personal conversations. The author has been both a F2P player and a spender at different points in their gaming career and sees merit in both positions.
