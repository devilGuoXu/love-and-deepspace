Two Accounts, One Year

A side-by-side case study — F2P vs light spender

For the past 12 months, I have maintained two accounts with different spending profiles. Account A buys the monthly pass and occasional gem packs. Account B has never spent a cent. Same playtime, same knowledge, different budgets. Here is what happened.

Case Study 1: The First 3 Months
Account A (Spender)
Total Spent: ~$45
Account B (F2P)
Total Spent: $0
Gap
2.5 Abyss floors

Account A Progress

Cleared Abyss floor 6 by month 2. Had two SSR memories by month 3 (one from limited banner, one from standard pity). Main DPS reached level 70. Had a functional second team with partial Stellactrum matching. Account power: 28,400 at month 3.

Account B Progress

Cleared Abyss floor 4 by month 2. Had one SSR memory from standard pity at month 2. Main DPS reached level 60 by month 3. Second team was barely functional with SR memories. Account power: 22,100 at month 3. The gap was 6,300 power and 2.5 Abyss floors. Significant but not game-breaking.

MONTHS 1-3 VERDICT

The spender account had a clear advantage in early progression. The monthly pass stamina alone translated to approximately 40 extra domain runs over 3 months, which meant better protocores and more enhancement materials. However, the F2P account was still progressing. It was just progressing slower.

Case Study 2: Months 4-8
Account A
Total Spent: ~$120
Account B
Total Spent: $0
Gap
1.5 Abyss floors

Account A Progress

Cleared Abyss floor 9 by month 6. Had 5 SSR memories (2 limited, 3 standard). Main DPS at level 80 with +15 protocores. Second DPS at level 70. Three supports at level 60. Account power: 38,500 at month 6. Hitting diminishing returns on spending as roster filled out.

Account B Progress

Cleared Abyss floor 7 by month 6. Had 2 SSR memories (both standard). Main DPS at level 75 with +12 protocores. Second DPS at level 60. Supports at level 50-55. Account power: 31,200 at month 6. The power gap was still 7,300, but the Abyss floor gap had narrowed from 2.5 to 1.5 floors.

MONTHS 4-8 VERDICT

The gap started narrowing. The F2P account was catching up because progression in this game is asymptotic. Early advantages matter less over time because everyone hits the same stat caps and content ceilings. The spender account reached power milestones faster, but the F2P account always reached them eventually. The satisfaction gap was also narrowing as the F2P account's deliberate resource management became more rewarding.

Case Study 3: Months 9-12
Account A
Total Spent: ~$200
Account B
Total Spent: $0
Gap
1.0 Abyss floors

Account A Progress

Cleared Abyss floor 12 (1-star) by month 11. Had 9 SSR memories (5 limited, 4 standard). Multiple fully built operatives. Account power: 46,800 at month 12. Clear goals were now about optimizing substats rather than acquiring new pieces.

Account B Progress

Cleared Abyss floor 11 (2-star) by month 11. Had 4 SSR memories (all standard, plus one lucky limited pull from free event tickets). Main DPS fully built. Second team functional but not optimized. Account power: 39,500 at month 12. The power gap was 7,300, but the Abyss floor gap was now only 1 floor.

MONTHS 9-12 VERDICT

After 12 months, the F2P account was 1 Abyss floor behind the spender account. The spender had more SSRs and a deeper roster, but the F2P account was clearing 95% of the same content. The spending gap of $200 translated to approximately one additional floor of Abyss progress per year. Whether that is worth $200 is a personal question, but the data suggests that spending accelerates early progress and compresses the timeline rather than enabling content that is otherwise impossible to access.

Final Analysis

What Spending Actually Buys

Time. Spending compresses the timeline. The spender account reached month-12 power levels by approximately month 8. The F2P account took 12 months to get there. Spending bought 4 months of accelerated progression. Whether that is worth $200 depends on how much you value those 4 months.

What F2P Actually Costs

Flexibility. The F2P account had to make harder choices about which banners to skip and which operatives to build. There were banners I wanted to pull on but could not afford. The F2P path requires more discipline and more patience. The trade-off is that every pull and every piece of progress feels earned.

FINAL VERDICT

Neither path is objectively better. The spender account had more stuff. The F2P account had more satisfaction per SSR. Both accounts cleared 95% of the same content. The spending did not unlock content that was otherwise inaccessible. It just unlocked it faster. If you have the patience, F2P is a completely viable path. If you have the budget and limited time, spending is a reasonable choice. The gap between them is much smaller than the community rhetoric suggests.
