Story Summary · Plot Overview

He's been watching the same stars for two hundred years.

Released in December 2024, "The Voyage of the Outcast" is Xavier's personal branch in the "Tomorrow's Prelude" series. It is the quietest of the three — no explosions, no dramatic confessions, no battle against impossible odds. Just a man, a telescope, and two hundred years of waiting.

The story begins with a routine observation. Xavier has been tracking an unusual protocore signal — not from N109, not from any known source, but from somewhere far beyond the stars. MC joins him at the observatory, expecting a normal night of stargazing. She doesn't get it.

"There was a planet once. Philos. It was dying long before I left. I stayed until the last star went out."
—— Xavier, finally speaking about his past

For the first time, Xavier talks about Philos. Not in fragments, not in half-finished sentences — fully. He tells her about the planet he couldn't save, the people he left behind, the oath he swore before he left. He was a knight. His duty was to protect. He failed.

The protocore signal is revealed to be a fragment of Philos — a memory capsule, launched into space before the planet's final collapse, carrying the last recording of his people. Xavier has been tracking it for two centuries. It's finally within reach.

✨ The Knight's Oath

"I swore I would protect Philos with my life. I failed."

"I swore I would find her again. I kept that one."

"I swore I would never forget. I haven't."

"But sometimes — I wish I could."

The memory capsule contains the faces of everyone he lost. Their voices. Their names. Their last words. Xavier listens to all of it. He doesn't cry. Xavier doesn't cry. But he's quiet for a long time afterward.

MC asks him if he regrets leaving.

"Every day," he says. "But I couldn't save Philos. I could only carry it with me."

He looks at her. The first person in two hundred years who made the weight feel lighter.

"Maybe that's enough."

02

Deep Dive Analysis

The Promise · A Knight's Oath

He was a knight once. He's been keeping his promises ever since.

Xavier's entire life can be understood through the promises he made. He promised to protect Philos. He failed. He promised to find her again. He succeeded — after two hundred years. He promised to never forget. He hasn't. The branch suggests that these promises are both his anchor and his prison.

✧ The Paradox of the Knight

Xavier is defined by duty. He was trained to protect, to serve, to sacrifice. But Philos is gone. There's no one left to protect. No one left to serve. No one left to sacrifice for — except her.

The branch argues that Xavier has quietly transferred his knight's oath from Philos to MC. He doesn't say it. He doesn't even consciously acknowledge it. But his actions speak: he stays close. He watches over her. He would die for her without hesitation.

It's not romantic in the traditional sense. It's deeper. He's found a new purpose — and he's terrified of failing this one too.

The Weight of Survival

"Everyone else died. Why am I the one who gets to be here?"
—— Xavier, survivor's guilt

One of the branch's most powerful themes is survivor's guilt. Xavier doesn't just miss Philos — he feels responsible for its destruction. He was a knight. He should have been able to save it. He couldn't.

The memory capsule contains the last words of his comrades. Some of them blamed him. Most of them didn't. The ones who didn't were worse. Their forgiveness, their understanding, their lack of anger — it ate at him more than any accusation could.

"They told me it wasn't my fault," he says. "That's what made it hurt."

MC doesn't try to comfort him with platitudes. She doesn't say "it's not your fault" or "you did everything you could." She just sits with him. Lets him carry the weight. Doesn't try to take it away.

Sometimes, the branch suggests, that's all anyone can do.

Philos · The Dying Star

A planet of light and shadow. Now nothing but memory.

Philos is described in fragments — a planet of eternal twilight, where the stars never fully set, where the light filtered through crystalline structures, where the people had voices like wind chimes and eyes that held too much hope.

Xavier's descriptions of Philos are the most poetic lines in the entire branch. He talks about it the way someone talks about a lover they lost decades ago — with a mixture of longing, pain, and impossible tenderness.

✨ Philos: What We Know

From Xavier's dialogue in the branch:

  • Philos was dying long before Xavier left — a slow collapse, not a sudden explosion
  • The people of Philos knew they couldn't survive. They sent out scouts to find new homes. Xavier was one of them.
  • He was supposed to bring help back. He couldn't. By the time he found a way, it was too late.
  • The last message from Philos: "Don't come back. Live."

The memory capsule contains that message. Xavier has listened to it hundreds of times. He knows every pause, every breath, every crack in the speaker's voice. He can't stop listening.

"I know I should let go," he says. "I don't know how."

MC takes his hand. "You don't have to let go. You just have to let someone help you carry it."

He looks at her. For a long moment, he doesn't say anything.

"Okay," he says.

It's the first time he's ever accepted help.

03

Character Development & Emotional Core

"The Voyage of the Outcast" is Xavier's most vulnerable appearance. It strips away the sleepy exterior, the gentle smile, the careful distance. What remains is someone exhausted by two hundred years of carrying everything alone.

Xavier · The Knight Without a Kingdom

He's been watching the stars for two hundred years. They remind him of home.

✧ Xavier · Key Quotes

  • "I stayed until the last star went out." — He didn't abandon Philos. He outlived it.
  • "Everyone else died. Why am I the one who gets to be here?" — Survivor's guilt, unvarnished
  • "I know I should let go. I don't know how." — The admission he's never made before
  • "You don't have to carry it alone." — MC, finally reaching him

This branch recontextualizes everything we know about Xavier. His sleepiness isn't laziness — it's exhaustion from two hundred years of grief. His distance isn't coldness — it's fear of attachment. His silence isn't emptiness — it's the weight of everything he can't say.

MC · The One Who Stayed

In Zayne's branch, she was the supporter. In Rafayel's, she was the answer. In Xavier's, she is simply — present. She doesn't save him. She doesn't fix him. She doesn't offer solutions. She just stays. She listens. She doesn't look away.

"I can't bring back Philos. I can't undo what happened. But I can be here. Tonight. Tomorrow. For as long as you need."
—— MC, to Xavier

It's the most understated of the three branches. There's no dramatic rescue, no world-saving. Just two people, sitting under the stars, learning to carry the weight together.

04

Viewing Guide & Tips

📖 Before You Watch
  • Complete main story — understand Xavier's role as a deepspace hunter and his origins
  • Experience Xavier's personal story "Starlight Promise" for additional context
  • Pay attention to the observatory — it's the setting for many of his memories
🎮 Immersion Tips
  • Listen to the silence: Xavier's pauses are as meaningful as his words
  • Watch the stars: The visual storytelling in the memory sequences is stunning
  • Don't rush: This branch is slow and meditative — let it breathe

💡 Easter Egg: The constellation Xavier points to during the story is the same one featured in his 5-star memory "Stargazer." It's his private name for her.

05

Personal Rating & Recommendation

★★★★☆

✧ What Works

  • Quiet power: This branch doesn't scream for attention — it earns it
  • Xavier's vulnerability: Finally, we see beneath the gentle smile
  • Survivor's guilt theme: Handled with nuance and care
  • Beautiful prose: Xavier's descriptions of Philos are poetry
  • Understated romance: No grand gestures, just profound connection

✧ What Could Be Better

  • Pacing: Some players may find it too slow
  • Minimal action: If you're looking for combat, this isn't the branch
  • Emotionally heavy: Not a light read — be prepared
8.8
Overall Score / 10
Quiet, devastating, and beautiful. Essential for Xavier fans.
"The stars don't judge. They just watch. That's why I keep looking at them."
—— Xavier, finally ready to let someone watch with him

"The Voyage of the Outcast" is the quietest of the three Tomorrow's Prelude branches. It doesn't have the dramatic climax of Rafayel's story or the tense horror of Zayne's. It just has Xavier — finally, fully, heartbreakingly honest about who he is and what he's lost.

If you've ever wondered what lies behind Xavier's gentle smile, this is your answer. Grief. Exhaustion. Survivor's guilt. And beneath all of it — a desperate, quiet hope that maybe, this time, he won't have to carry it alone.

It's not the flashiest branch. But it might be the one that stays with you longest.
