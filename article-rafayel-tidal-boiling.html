Story Summary · Plot Overview

The sea remembers. He remembers everything.

Released in late 2024, "Tidal Boiling Land" is Rafayel's personal branch in the "Tomorrow's Prelude" series. It is widely considered the emotional core of the event — a story about promises made in childhood, a kingdom lost beneath the waves, and a wait that spans over a thousand years.

The story begins with an investigation in a coastal town. A strange protocore phenomenon is causing the tides to behave erratically — waters boiling, sea creatures dying, and locals reporting visions of an underwater city. MC is dispatched as the hunter on site. Rafayel, conveniently, is already there. He's "researching" for a painting.

"I've been here before. Not in this lifetime. But I remember the salt."
—— Rafayel, staring at the sea

As they investigate, Rafayel's behavior grows increasingly strange. He stares at the ocean for hours. He murmurs words in a language no one recognizes. He draws the same scene over and over — a child, a shore, someone walking away.

The truth emerges through a series of flashbacks: over a thousand years ago, on the day the tide reversed, a young Rafayel was stranded on a beach. The sea had rejected him. He was dying. And then she came — a girl who pulled him from the shallows, who spoke to him in a language only he understood, who promised "next year, same time, I'll be here."

🌊 The Day the Tide Reversed

"You're not from here, are you?" she asked.

He couldn't speak. He was too weak, too scared, too young to understand what was happening.

"Next year," she said. "Same time. I'll be here. Promise?"

She held out her hand. He took it.

He's been keeping that promise ever since.

The girl didn't return the next year. Or the year after. Or any year after. But Rafayel never stopped waiting. He returned to that shore every year on the same day, watching the tide, watching the horizon, watching for someone who might never come back.

In the present, the protocore phenomenon is revealed to be a fragment of Lemurian memory — a tear in reality caused by the kingdom's fall, replaying the same moment over and over. The only way to stop it is to confront the memory, to let go of the promise, to finally move on.

Rafayel refuses.

"I don't want to move on," he says. "I want to remember."

In the climax, MC enters the memory with him. They stand on the same shore, a thousand years ago, watching the child and the girl make their promise. And MC realizes — the girl was her. Not this incarnation. A past life. But still her.

She takes his hand. "I'm here now."

Rafayel looks at her. For the first time in a thousand years, he doesn't look like he's waiting for someone else.

"I know," he says. "You're always late."

02

Deep Dive Analysis

The Promise · "Next Year, Same Time"

A promise made. A promise kept. For a thousand years.

At the heart of "Tidal Boiling Land" is a single promise: "Next year, same time, I'll be here."

Rafayel has been keeping this promise for over a thousand years. He returned to that shore every year — through storms, through wars, through the rise and fall of civilizations. He watched the coastline change. He watched languages die and be reborn. He watched her be born, live, and die. Multiple times. And every time, he waited.

This isn't romantic. It's devastating. The branch doesn't shy away from that. Rafayel isn't presented as a tragic hero; he's presented as someone who cannot let go. His waiting isn't noble — it's compulsive. It's the only thing keeping him tethered to a world that took everything from him.

✧ The Mathematics of Waiting

If Rafayel has lived approximately 1,200 years, and returned to that shore every year, that's 1,200 visits. 1,200 times he stood on that beach. 1,200 times he watched the horizon. 1,200 times he left disappointed.

And he kept coming back.

That's not hope. That's obsession. But the branch argues — perhaps obsession and love are the same thing, when you've been alive long enough.

The Twin Flames · Parallel Destinies

One of the most striking aspects of this branch is its use of parallelism. Rafayel and MC are mirrors of each other — both searching for something they can't name, both waiting for someone they can't remember, both carrying the weight of a past they didn't choose.

"You're looking for someone too, aren't you?"
—— Rafayel, to MC, recognizing her before she recognizes herself

MC's role in this branch is unique among the three. Unlike Zayne's branch, where she is the supporter, or Xavier's, where she is the mystery, here she is the answer. She is the girl from the shore. She made the promise. She forgot. He remembered.

This creates a poignant power imbalance: he has loved her for a thousand years. She has known him for a few months. He knows the sound of her voice in every incarnation. She is still learning his name.

But the branch doesn't dwell on the tragedy of this imbalance. Instead, it focuses on the hope — the possibility that even after a thousand years of waiting, the right person might finally, finally stay.

Lemuria's Echo · The Weight of a Lost Kingdom

A kingdom beneath the waves. A prince who couldn't save it.

The protocore phenomenon in the branch is a fragment of Lemuria — a memory bubble, frozen in time, replaying the last moments of a civilization that no longer exists. The boiling tide isn't natural. It's the sea mourning. It's Lemuria calling out to its lost prince.

Rafayel's connection to Lemuria is more than cultural — it's elemental. His Evol is water-based. His dreams are filled with coral and salt and voices he can't quite understand. He is the last Lemurian, and he carries the weight of an entire kingdom on his shoulders.

🌊 The Kingdom's Final Words

In the memory bubble, Rafayel hears the voices of his people. They don't ask him to save them. They ask him to remember them.

"Don't let us disappear," they say. "Remember the coral. Remember the songs. Remember the way the light filtered through the water."

He has kept that promise too. He has never forgotten. He has painted Lemuria a thousand times, in a thousand ways, trying to make it real again.

The branch suggests that this is the true tragedy of Rafayel — not that he lost his kingdom, but that he's the only one left who remembers it.

The resolution of the branch doesn't offer easy answers. The protocore phenomenon is stopped, but Lemuria isn't restored. The memory bubble bursts, taking the last echo of the kingdom with it. Rafayel watches it disappear — and doesn't look away.

"Goodbye," he says. Not to the kingdom. To the weight he's been carrying.

It's not closure. It's not healing. It's just... release. Permission to stop carrying what was never his to carry alone.

Character Development & Emotional Core

"Tidal Boiling Land" is Rafayel's most vulnerable appearance. It strips away the artist's performance, the dramatic complaints, the mask of indifference. What remains is someone exhausted by his own history.

Rafayel · The Prince Who Refuses to Forget

Throughout the branch, Rafayel oscillates between hope and resignation. He wants to believe she'll come back. He's afraid that if he stops believing, he'll have nothing left.

✧ Rafayel · Key Quotes

  • "I've been here before. Not in this lifetime. But I remember the salt." — The past bleeding into the present
  • "I don't want to move on. I want to remember." — Refusing to let go
  • "You're always late." — A thousand years of waiting, summarized in three words
  • "The sea remembers. That's why I can't forget." — Trapped by memory
He stopped waiting. He started living.

MC · The One Who Finally Came Back

In this branch, MC is not just a supporting character — she is the answer to a thousand-year-old question. She is the girl from the shore, the promise made in childhood, the face Rafayel has been painting for centuries.

She doesn't remember any of it. But she chooses to believe him. She chooses to stay. She chooses to be the one who finally — finally — keeps the promise.

"I don't remember making that promise. But I'm here now. That counts for something, doesn't it?"
—— MC, to Rafayel

Viewing Guide & Tips

📖 Before You Watch
  • Complete main story — understand the protocore phenomenon and the secrets of the deep
  • Experience Rafayel's personal story "Tides of Memory" for additional context
  • Pay attention to the paintings in his studio — they're not random
🎮 Immersion Tips
  • Watch his eyes: Rafayel's expressions shift subtly — he's more readable than he thinks
  • Listen to the ocean: The sound design in this branch is exceptional; use headphones
  • Take your time: The flashback sequences are dense. Don't rush through them

💡 Easter Egg: The phrase "tidal boiling land" appears in Lemurian texts mentioned in Rafayel's 5-star memories. It refers to the day the tide reversed — the day Lemuria fell.

Personal Rating & Recommendation

★★★★★

✧ What Works

  • Emotional depth: This is Rafayel at his most vulnerable — and most human
  • Beautiful structure: The flashbacks are woven seamlessly into the present narrative
  • MC's agency: She's not passive; she chooses to believe, to stay, to love
  • The promise motif: "Next year, same time" — devastating and beautiful
  • Visual storytelling: The memory bubble sequences are hauntingly beautiful

✧ What Could Be Better

  • Pacing: Some players may find the flashbacks disorienting
  • Lemurian lore: Could use more explanation for new players
  • Rafayel's defensiveness: Some might find his avoidance frustrating (though that's the point)
9.2
Overall Score / 10
The emotional peak of Tomorrow's Prelude. Essential for Rafayel fans.
"The sea remembers. But maybe — it's time to let the sea rest."
—— Rafayel, finally ready to stop waiting

"Tidal Boiling Land" is the best-written branch in Tomorrow's Prelude. It's not flashy. It's not action-packed. It's a quiet meditation on promises, waiting, and the weight of memory.

Rafayel is often dismissed as "the annoying artist," the "bratty one." This branch proves that his performance is exactly that — a performance. Beneath the complaints and the drama is someone who has been carrying an unbearable weight for over a thousand years. Someone who is terrified of being forgotten. Someone who has loved the same person across lifetimes, across deaths, across the slow erosion of everything he ever knew.

If you only play one branch of Tomorrow's Prelude, make it this one.
